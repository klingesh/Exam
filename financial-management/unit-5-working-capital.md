# Financial Management — Unit V: Working Capital Management

Importance, Concepts, Types, Operating Cycle, Sources, Factors.

---

## 1. What is Working Capital?

**Working Capital** is the money a business needs for its **day-to-day operations** — to buy raw material, pay wages, hold stock, and give credit to customers, until cash comes back from sales.

> **Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities**

- **Current Assets:** cash, debtors (receivables), inventory/stock, bills receivable, prepaid expenses, short-term investments.
- **Current Liabilities:** creditors (payables), bills payable, outstanding expenses, short-term loans, bank overdraft.

**Analogy:** Working capital is the "**fuel**" that keeps the business engine running daily, while fixed assets are the "engine" itself.

### Gross vs Net Working Capital
- **Gross Working Capital** = total Current Assets.
- **Net Working Capital** = Current Assets − Current Liabilities.
  - Positive = healthy liquidity; Negative = possible liquidity trouble.

---

## 2. Importance of Working Capital Management

1. **Smooth operations** — ensures materials, wages, and bills are paid on time.
2. **Liquidity** — ability to meet short-term obligations (avoids insolvency).
3. **Profitability** — right balance avoids idle funds and shortages.
4. **Creditworthiness** — good liquidity improves reputation with suppliers/banks.
5. **Uninterrupted production & sales.**
6. **Handles emergencies/seasonal demand.**

> ⚖️ **The trade-off:** Too much working capital = idle funds, low profitability. Too little = risk of stockouts, inability to pay dues. Goal = **optimum** level (balance liquidity & profitability).

---

## 3. Types / Kinds of Working Capital

### On the basis of Concept
- **Gross Working Capital** = total current assets.
- **Net Working Capital** = current assets − current liabilities.

### On the basis of Time
- **Permanent (Fixed) Working Capital** — the **minimum** level always needed to run operations, regardless of activity. Sub-types:
  - *Regular WC* — bare minimum for the cycle.
  - *Reserve WC* — extra buffer for contingencies.
- **Temporary (Variable/Fluctuating) Working Capital** — the **extra** needed during peak/seasonal periods. Sub-types:
  - *Seasonal WC* — for seasonal demand (e.g., umbrellas in monsoon).
  - *Special WC* — for one-off events (big marketing drive, special order).

> Rule of thumb: fund **permanent WC** with long-term sources, **temporary WC** with short-term sources.

## 4. Operating Cycle (Working Capital Cycle) ⭐

The **operating cycle** is the **time taken to convert cash back into cash** — from buying raw material, making & selling goods, giving credit, and finally collecting cash.

```
Cash → Raw Material → Work-in-Progress → Finished Goods → Debtors (Sales on credit) → Cash
```

The longer the cycle, the **more** working capital is needed.

### Operating Cycle Formula
> **Operating Cycle = R + W + F + D − C**
> where:
> R = Raw material holding period
> W = Work-in-progress period
> F = Finished goods holding period
> D = Debtors (receivables) collection period
> C = Creditors (payables) payment period

- **Gross Operating Cycle = R + W + F + D**
- **Net Operating Cycle (Cash Cycle) = Gross Operating Cycle − Creditors period**

**Component formulas (in days):**
- Raw material period = (Avg raw material stock ÷ Raw material consumed) × 365
- WIP period = (Avg WIP ÷ Cost of production) × 365
- Finished goods period = (Avg finished goods ÷ Cost of goods sold) × 365
- Debtors period = (Avg debtors ÷ Credit sales) × 365
- Creditors period = (Avg creditors ÷ Credit purchases) × 365

> **Shorter operating cycle = less working capital needed = more efficient.** Managers try to shorten it (faster collection, less stock, more supplier credit).

## 5. Sources of Working Capital

### Long-term / Permanent sources (for permanent WC)
- Equity shares, Preference shares
- Retained earnings (ploughing back of profits)
- Debentures & long-term loans

### Short-term sources (for temporary WC)
- **Trade credit** (buying on credit from suppliers)
- **Bank finance** — cash credit, overdraft, bills discounting, working capital loans
- **Commercial paper** (large firms)
- **Public deposits**
- **Factoring** (selling receivables to get immediate cash)
- **Outstanding expenses & provisions** (spontaneous financing)

---

## 6. Factors Affecting Working Capital Requirement

1. **Nature of business** — trading needs less; manufacturing needs more.
2. **Scale of operations** — bigger firms need more WC.
3. **Production cycle length** — longer cycle → more WC.
4. **Business cycle** — boom needs more WC; recession less.
5. **Credit policy** — liberal credit to customers → more WC; more credit from suppliers → less WC.
6. **Seasonality** — seasonal businesses need extra WC in peak season.
7. **Growth & expansion** — growing firms need more WC.
8. **Inventory policy** — large stock holding → more WC.
9. **Operating efficiency** — efficient operations reduce WC needs.
10. **Availability of raw material** — scarce/irregular supply → hold more stock → more WC.
11. **Price level changes (inflation)** — rising prices increase WC needs.
12. **Dividend policy** — high payouts reduce internally available WC.

---

## 7. Approaches to Financing Working Capital
- **Conservative approach** — use more long-term funds (low risk, low return, high liquidity).
- **Aggressive approach** — use more short-term funds (high risk, high return, low liquidity).
- **Hedging/Matching approach** — match asset life with financing (permanent WC ← long-term; temporary WC ← short-term). *Balanced and most recommended.*

---

## ✅ Quick Recap (Unit V)
- **Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities**; fuels daily operations.
- **Types:** Gross vs Net; Permanent (fixed) vs Temporary (variable/seasonal).
- **Operating Cycle = R + W + F + D − C**; shorter cycle = less WC needed.
- **Sources:** long-term (shares, retained earnings) for permanent WC; short-term (trade credit, bank finance, factoring) for temporary WC.
- **Factors:** nature & scale of business, production cycle, credit policy, seasonality, inflation.
- Goal: **optimum WC** — balance liquidity vs profitability.
