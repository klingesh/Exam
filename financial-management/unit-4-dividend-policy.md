# Financial Management — Unit IV: Dividend Policy & Decisions

Determinants, Theories of Dividend, Relevance & Irrelevance Concepts.

---

## 1. What is Dividend Policy?

A **dividend** is the part of profit **distributed to shareholders**. **Dividend policy** is the company's strategy on **how much profit to pay out as dividends vs how much to retain** (keep) for reinvestment.

The core trade-off:
- **Pay more dividend** → shareholders happy now, but less money to grow.
- **Retain more** → more funds to grow (future gains), but less immediate income to shareholders.

**Key terms:**
- **Dividend Payout Ratio** = Dividend ÷ Earnings (% of profit paid out).
- **Retention Ratio** = Retained Earnings ÷ Earnings (= 1 − payout).

---

## 2. Types of Dividend Policies
- **Stable/Constant dividend** — fixed amount per share regardless of profit.
- **Constant payout ratio** — fixed % of profit each year (dividend varies with profit).
- **Residual dividend** — pay only what's left after funding all good projects.
- **Stable + extra** — regular small dividend plus a bonus in good years.

**Forms of dividend:** cash dividend, stock dividend (bonus shares), scrip dividend, property dividend.

---

## 3. Determinants of Dividend Policy (Factors)

1. **Earnings & their stability** — steady profits allow steady dividends.
2. **Liquidity / cash position** — dividends need cash, not just profit.
3. **Growth & investment opportunities** — high-growth firms retain more.
4. **Legal constraints** — Companies Act rules on dividends.
5. **Taxation** — tax treatment of dividends vs capital gains.
6. **Access to capital markets** — big firms can raise funds easily, pay more dividend.
7. **Shareholders' expectations** — some want regular income (retirees), some want growth.
8. **Control** — issuing new shares dilutes control; retaining earnings avoids that.
9. **Contractual restrictions** — loan agreements may limit dividends.
10. **Inflation & stability of policy** — firms prefer consistent, predictable dividends.

---

## 4. Theories of Dividend Policy

Two camps: **Relevance** (dividend affects firm value) vs **Irrelevance** (it doesn't).

---

## A. RELEVANCE THEORIES — *Dividend decision affects share price/value*

### (1) Walter's Model
Walter says dividend policy **matters** and depends on the relationship between the firm's **rate of return (r)** and its **cost of capital (Ke)**.

- **If r > Ke (growth firm):** retain everything → optimal payout = **0%**. (Firm earns more than shareholders could elsewhere.)
- **If r < Ke (declining firm):** pay out everything → optimal payout = **100%**.
- **If r = Ke (normal firm):** dividend policy is **irrelevant** (any payout gives same value).

**Walter's Formula:**
> **P = [ D + (r ÷ Ke)(E − D) ] ÷ Ke**
> P = market price, D = dividend per share, E = EPS, r = rate of return, Ke = cost of equity.

**Assumptions:** all-equity firm (no debt), constant r and Ke, constant EPS & DPS, 100% payout or retention, infinite life.

---

### (2) Gordon's Model (Dividend Capitalization / "Bird-in-hand")
Gordon also says dividends **matter**. Investors prefer **current dividends** over uncertain future gains — "a bird in hand is worth two in the bush." Higher dividends → lower risk perception → higher share price.

**Gordon's Formula:**
> **P = E(1 − b) ÷ (Ke − br)**
> where b = retention ratio, (1−b) = payout, br = growth rate (g), E = earnings.

- Conclusions match Walter's: r > Ke → retain; r < Ke → distribute.

**Assumptions:** all-equity firm, no external financing, constant r & Ke, Ke > g, perpetual earnings.

---

## B. IRRELEVANCE THEORY — *Dividend does NOT affect value*

### (3) Modigliani-Miller (MM) Hypothesis
MM argues dividend policy is **irrelevant** to share value. What matters is the firm's **earning power and investment policy**, not how profit is split between dividend and retention.

**Logic:** If a firm pays more dividend, it must raise the same amount from new shares (to fund investment). The gain from dividend is exactly offset by the loss from dilution. So shareholders' total wealth is unchanged.

**Arbitrage argument:** dividends and capital gains are perfect substitutes; investors can create "homemade dividends" by selling shares.

**MM Formula (valuation):**
> **P₀ = (D₁ + P₁) ÷ (1 + Ke)**
> Current price = (next year's dividend + next year's price) discounted at Ke.

**Assumptions:** perfect capital markets, no taxes, no transaction/floatation costs, rational investors, certainty of future, fixed investment policy.

**Criticism:** real markets have taxes, transaction costs, and information signaling — so dividends *do* matter in practice.

## ✅ Quick Recap (Unit IV)
- Dividend policy = payout vs retention decision.
- **Relevance (Walter, Gordon):** dividends affect value.
  - r > Ke → retain (low payout); r < Ke → pay out (high payout); r = Ke → indifferent.
  - Gordon = "bird-in-hand" — investors value certain current dividends more.
- **Irrelevance (MM):** dividend policy doesn't affect value in perfect markets; only earning power matters.
- Determinants: earnings stability, liquidity, growth opportunities, taxes, legal rules, control.
