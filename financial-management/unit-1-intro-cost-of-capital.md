# Financial Management — Unit I: Introduction & Cost of Capital

Financial Management, Financial Goals, Finance Decisions, Profit vs Wealth Maximisation, Cost of Capital (Equity & Debt).

---

## 1. What is Financial Management?

**Financial Management** is the planning, organizing, directing, and controlling of the **money (financial resources)** of an organization. In simple terms: **how a business raises money, invests it, and manages it** to achieve its goals.

It answers three big questions:
1. **Where to invest?** (Investment/Capital Budgeting decision)
2. **Where to get the money?** (Financing decision)
3. **How much profit to distribute vs retain?** (Dividend decision)

---

## 2. The Three Key Finance Decisions

| Decision | Question | Concerned with |
|----------|----------|----------------|
| **Investment (Capital Budgeting)** | Which long-term assets/projects to invest in? | Fixed assets, project selection (NPV, IRR) |
| **Financing** | What mix of debt & equity to raise funds? | Capital structure, cost of capital |
| **Dividend** | How much profit to pay out vs reinvest? | Dividend policy, retained earnings |

*(Some texts add a 4th: **Working Capital / Liquidity decision** — managing short-term assets & liabilities.)*

---

## 3. Financial Goals: Profit Maximisation vs Wealth Maximisation

### Profit Maximisation
The goal of earning **maximum profit** in the short run.

**Drawbacks:**
- **Vague** — profit before or after tax? Short or long term?
- **Ignores time value of money** — ₹1 today ≠ ₹1 next year.
- **Ignores risk** — high profit may come with high risk.
- Encourages **short-term** thinking.

### Wealth Maximisation (Value Maximisation) — the MODERN goal
The goal of maximizing the **market value of the owners' (shareholders') wealth**, i.e., maximizing the value of the firm / share price.

**Why it's superior:**
- Considers **time value of money** (uses discounted cash flows).
- Considers **risk**.
- Focuses on **long-term** sustainable value.
- Based on **cash flows**, not accounting profit.

| Basis | Profit Maximisation | Wealth Maximisation |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Focus | Short-term profit | Long-term shareholder value |
| Time value of money | Ignored | Considered |
| Risk | Ignored | Considered |
| Measure | Accounting profit | Market value / share price |

> ✅ **Wealth maximisation** is the widely accepted objective of financial management.

## 4. Cost of Capital

### What is it?
**Cost of Capital** is the **minimum rate of return** a company must earn on its investments to satisfy its investors (lenders and shareholders). It is the "price" a firm pays for using funds.

**Why it matters:**
- Used as the **discount rate / hurdle rate** in capital budgeting (NPV, IRR).
- Helps in **capital structure** decisions.
- A project is worthwhile only if its return **exceeds** the cost of capital.

### Types of cost
- **Specific cost** — cost of each source (equity, debt, preference).
- **Overall/Composite cost = WACC** (Weighted Average Cost of Capital).

---

## 5. Cost of Debt (Kd)

Debt = borrowed funds (loans, debentures, bonds). Interest is **tax-deductible**, so debt has a tax benefit.

**Cost of Irredeemable (perpetual) Debt:**
> **Kd (before tax) = Interest ÷ Net Proceeds (or Sale value)**
> **Kd (after tax) = Kd (before tax) × (1 − Tax rate)**

Or simply: **Kd (after tax) = [I × (1 − t)] ÷ Net Proceeds**

**Cost of Redeemable Debt (repaid after n years):**
> **Kd = [ I(1−t) + (RV − NP)/n ] ÷ [ (RV + NP)/2 ]**
> where I = annual interest, t = tax rate, RV = redemption value, NP = net proceeds, n = years.

**Example:** ₹100 debenture, 10% interest, issued at par, tax 30%.
- Kd (after tax) = 10 × (1 − 0.30) ÷ 100 = **7%**

---

## 6. Cost of Equity (Ke)

Equity = owners' funds (equity shares + retained earnings). This is the return equity shareholders expect. It has **no tax benefit** (dividends are not tax-deductible), so equity is usually **costlier** than debt.

### (a) Dividend Yield / Dividend Price Method
> **Ke = D ÷ MP** (or Net Proceeds)
> D = expected dividend per share, MP = market price per share.

### (b) Dividend Growth Model (Gordon's Model)
> **Ke = (D₁ ÷ P₀) + g**
> D₁ = expected dividend next year, P₀ = current price, g = growth rate of dividends.

**Example:** D₁ = ₹5, P₀ = ₹50, g = 8%.
- Ke = (5 ÷ 50) + 0.08 = 0.10 + 0.08 = **18%**

### (c) Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)
> **Ke = Rf + β (Rm − Rf)**
> Rf = risk-free rate, β = beta (systematic risk), Rm = market return.

**Example:** Rf = 6%, β = 1.2, Rm = 14%.
- Ke = 6% + 1.2 × (14% − 6%) = 6% + 9.6% = **15.6%**

### (d) Earnings Yield Method
> **Ke = EPS ÷ Market Price per Share**

---

## 7. Cost of Preference Capital (Kp)

Preference shares carry a fixed dividend. Dividend is **not tax-deductible**.

> **Kp (irredeemable) = Preference Dividend ÷ Net Proceeds**
> **Kp (redeemable) = [ PD + (RV − NP)/n ] ÷ [ (RV + NP)/2 ]**

---

## 8. Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)

The **overall** cost of capital — a weighted average of the costs of all sources, using their proportions in the capital structure.

> **WACC = (We × Ke) + (Wd × Kd) + (Wp × Kp)**
> where W = weight (proportion) of each source, and Kd is after-tax.

Weights can be based on **book value** or **market value** (market value preferred).

**Example:**
| Source | Amount (₹) | Weight | Cost | Weight × Cost |
|--------|-----------|--------|------|---------------|
| Equity | 6,00,000 | 0.60 | 18% | 10.80% |
| Debt (after tax) | 4,00,000 | 0.40 | 7% | 2.80% |
| **Total** | 10,00,000 | 1.00 | | **WACC = 13.60%** |

## ✅ Quick Recap (Unit I)
- FM = managing raising, investing & distributing funds. 3 decisions: **Investment, Financing, Dividend**.
- **Wealth maximisation** > profit maximisation (considers time value & risk).
- **Cost of capital** = minimum required return; used as hurdle/discount rate.
- **Kd (after tax) = Interest(1−t) ÷ Net Proceeds.**
- **Ke (Gordon) = D₁/P₀ + g; CAPM: Ke = Rf + β(Rm − Rf).**
- **WACC** = weighted average of all specific costs.
