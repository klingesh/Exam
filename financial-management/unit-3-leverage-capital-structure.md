# Financial Management — Unit III: Leverage & Capital Structure

Operating, Financial & Combined Leverage; Capital Structure Theories (NI, NOI, Traditional, MM).

---

## 1. What is Leverage?

**Leverage** means using **fixed costs** (fixed operating costs or fixed financial costs like interest) to **magnify returns**. Just like a physical lever lets you lift a heavy load with small effort, financial leverage lets a small change in sales/EBIT cause a bigger change in profits.

**But leverage is a double-edged sword** — it magnifies gains *and* losses.

### The income statement roadmap (know these levels)
```
Sales
− Variable Costs
= Contribution
− Fixed Operating Costs
= EBIT (Operating Profit)   ← Operating leverage acts up to here
− Interest
= EBT (Profit before tax)
− Tax
= EAT (Profit after tax)
÷ No. of shares
= EPS                        ← Financial leverage acts from EBIT to EPS
```

---

## 2. Operating Leverage (OL)

Arises from **fixed operating costs** (rent, salaries, depreciation). It measures how a change in **sales** affects **EBIT**.

> **Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL) = Contribution ÷ EBIT**
> Or = % change in EBIT ÷ % change in Sales

- **High OL** → high fixed operating costs → EBIT is very sensitive to sales changes → **higher business risk**.

**Example:** Contribution = ₹2,00,000; EBIT = ₹1,00,000 → DOL = 2. A 10% rise in sales → 20% rise in EBIT.

---

## 3. Financial Leverage (FL)

Arises from **fixed financial costs** (interest on debt, preference dividend). It measures how a change in **EBIT** affects **EPS** (or EBT).

> **Degree of Financial Leverage (DFL) = EBIT ÷ EBT**  (EBT = EBIT − Interest)
> Or = % change in EPS ÷ % change in EBIT

- **High FL** → more debt/interest → EPS very sensitive to EBIT changes → **higher financial risk**.
- Financial leverage is **favourable ("trading on equity")** when the return on investment > cost of debt.

**Example:** EBIT = ₹1,00,000; Interest = ₹20,000 → EBT = ₹80,000 → DFL = 1,00,000 ÷ 80,000 = **1.25**.

---

## 4. Combined Leverage (CL)

Total effect of both leverages — how a change in **sales** affects **EPS**.

> **Degree of Combined Leverage (DCL) = DOL × DFL = Contribution ÷ EBT**
> Or = % change in EPS ÷ % change in Sales

**Example:** DOL = 2, DFL = 1.25 → DCL = 2.5. A 10% rise in sales → 25% rise in EPS.

### Importance / Uses of Leverage
- Measures **business risk** (OL) and **financial risk** (FL).
- Helps design an **optimal capital structure**.
- Aids **profit planning** and understanding EPS sensitivity.

| Leverage | Source | Relationship | Risk type |
|----------|--------|--------------|-----------|
| Operating | Fixed operating costs | Sales → EBIT | Business risk |
| Financial | Fixed financial costs (interest) | EBIT → EPS | Financial risk |
| Combined | Both | Sales → EPS | Total risk |

## 5. Capital Structure

**Capital Structure** = the **mix of debt and equity** a firm uses to finance its assets.

**Optimal capital structure** = the debt-equity mix that **minimizes WACC** and **maximizes the firm's value** (share price).

### Factors affecting capital structure
Risk, cost of capital, control, flexibility, tax (interest is deductible), industry norms, cash flow stability, size of firm, market conditions.

---

## 6. Capital Structure Theories

The core question: **Does changing the debt-equity mix change the firm's value and WACC?**

Common assumptions across theories: only debt & equity; no taxes (except where stated); 100% payout; constant total assets; EBIT unchanged; investors are rational.

---

### (a) Net Income (NI) Approach — *Capital structure MATTERS*
**Proposed by Durand.** Says a firm **CAN** increase its value and reduce WACC by using **more debt**.

- Assumes Kd (cost of debt) < Ke (cost of equity), and both stay constant.
- More debt (cheaper) → **WACC falls** → **value of firm rises**.
- **Conclusion:** Maximum debt = optimal. Value is highest at 100% debt (theoretically).

> Value of firm V = Market value of Equity (S) + Market value of Debt (D)
> S = (EBIT − Interest) ÷ Ke

---

### (b) Net Operating Income (NOI) Approach — *Capital structure is IRRELEVANT*
**Also by Durand.** Says capital structure has **NO effect** on the firm's value or WACC.

- As debt increases, Ke rises exactly enough to offset the benefit of cheaper debt.
- **WACC stays constant**; firm value depends only on **operating income (EBIT)** and business risk, not on financing mix.
- **Conclusion:** There is **no optimal** capital structure — every mix gives the same value.

> Value of firm V = EBIT ÷ Ko (overall capitalization rate, constant)

---

### (c) Traditional Approach — *A middle path (optimal mix EXISTS)*
A compromise between NI and NOI.

- Up to a point, using more debt **reduces WACC** and **increases value** (debt benefit dominates).
- Beyond that point, more debt makes both Kd and Ke rise (risk perceived), so **WACC starts rising** and value falls.
- **Conclusion:** There **IS an optimal capital structure** — the point where WACC is **minimum** and value is **maximum** (U-shaped WACC curve).

---

### (d) Modigliani-Miller (MM) Approach
**Nobel-winning theory.** Has two versions:

**MM without taxes (Irrelevance):**
- Firm value is **independent** of capital structure (same conclusion as NOI but with rigorous proof).
- Based on **arbitrage**: if two identical firms (one levered, one unlevered) had different values, investors would arbitrage until values equalize.
- **Assumptions:** perfect capital markets, no taxes, no transaction costs, homogeneous risk classes, free arbitrage.
- **Proposition I:** V(levered) = V(unlevered).
- **Proposition II:** Ke rises with debt in a way that keeps WACC constant.

**MM with taxes (Relevance):**
- Since **interest is tax-deductible**, debt provides a **tax shield**.
- **Value of levered firm = Value of unlevered firm + (Tax rate × Debt)**.
- **Conclusion:** more debt → more tax shield → higher value. (Suggests firms should use maximum debt — though real-world bankruptcy costs limit this.)

---

### Summary of Theories (exam gold)
| Theory | Does structure affect value? | WACC behaviour | Optimal structure? |
|--------|------------------------------|----------------|--------------------|
| **NI** | Yes | Falls with more debt | 100% debt (max) |
| **NOI** | No | Constant | None |
| **Traditional** | Yes (up to a point) | U-shaped (falls then rises) | Yes, at min WACC |
| **MM (no tax)** | No | Constant | None |
| **MM (with tax)** | Yes | Falls with debt (tax shield) | Max debt |

---

## ✅ Quick Recap (Unit III)
- **DOL = Contribution ÷ EBIT** (business risk).
- **DFL = EBIT ÷ EBT** (financial risk).
- **DCL = DOL × DFL = Contribution ÷ EBT.**
- **NI:** structure matters, max debt best. **NOI & MM(no tax):** structure irrelevant. **Traditional:** optimal mix exists (min WACC). **MM(with tax):** debt adds value via tax shield (V_L = V_U + t×D).
