# 1️⃣3️⃣ DIRECTION SENSE 🟢

⏱️ **Study time: 20 minutes** | 🎯 **Typical questions: 2–3**

> 🟢 **EASY MARKS.** This topic takes 20 minutes to learn and you'll get nearly every question right. **Do not skip it to spend more time on maths** — that would be a bad trade.
>
> 🖊️ **The golden rule of this topic: ALWAYS DRAW.** Never try to do it in your head. Every mark lost here is lost by someone who didn't pick up a pen.

## 📖 PART 1 — THE COMPASS (draw this on your rough sheet first thing)

```
                    NORTH
                      ↑
         NW           │           NE
             ╲        │        ╱
               ╲      │      ╱
                 ╲    │    ╱
   WEST ←─────────────┼─────────────→ EAST
                 ╱    │    ╲
               ╱      │      ╲
             ╱        │        ╲
         SW           │           SE
                      ↓
                    SOUTH
```

### Two facts to lock in
```
NORTH is UP        SOUTH is DOWN
EAST is RIGHT      WEST is LEFT
```
> 💡 **Memory hook for the order (clockwise from North):** **N**ever **E**at **S**oggy **W**heat → **N**orth, **E**ast, **S**outh, **W**est.

## 📖 PART 2 — TURNING ⭐ (this is the whole topic)

```
RIGHT turn  =  CLOCKWISE        (N → E → S → W → N)
LEFT  turn  =  ANTICLOCKWISE    (N → W → S → E → N)
```

### The turning table — memorize this and you're done

| If facing… | Turn RIGHT → | Turn LEFT → | Turn BACK (180°) → |
|---|---|---|---|
| **North** | East | West | South |
| **East** | South | North | West |
| **South** | West | East | North |
| **West** | North | South | East |

> 🔑 **You don't need to memorize the table if you draw the compass.** Put your pen at the direction you're facing and rotate: clockwise for right, anticlockwise for left. That's it.

### Degrees version
| Turn | Effect |
|---|---|
| 90° clockwise | one step right (N → E) |
| 180° | complete reversal (N → S) |
| 270° clockwise | same as 90° anticlockwise (N → W) |
| 360° | back to the start |

### 🔍 Example 1 — Two turns
*A person facing North turns 90° clockwise, then 180° anticlockwise. Which direction is he facing now?*
```
Step 1: Start: North.
Step 2: 90° clockwise from North → EAST
Step 3: 180° from East → WEST  (180° is the same either way)
✅ He faces WEST
```

## 📖 PART 3 — THE NET DISPLACEMENT METHOD ⭐⭐

For "how far and in which direction from the start?" questions:

```
STEP 1: Draw the path, arrow by arrow.
STEP 2: Add up all the NORTH distances, and all the SOUTH distances.
        Net vertical = North total – South total
STEP 3: Add up all the EAST distances, and all the WEST distances.
        Net horizontal = East total – West total
STEP 4: If only one of them is non-zero → that's your answer directly.
        If both are non-zero → use Pythagoras for the distance.
```

### 🔍 Example 2 — The cancelling case (very common)
*A man walks 5 km North, then 3 km East, then 5 km South. How far and in which direction is he from his starting point?*

```
Step 1: Draw it.

              3 km E
        ┌───────────────┐
        │               │
   5 km │N              │ 5 km S
        │               │
      START           END

Step 2: Vertical: 5 North and 5 South → 5 – 5 = 0. They cancel!
Step 3: Horizontal: 3 East, no West → 3 km East.
Step 4: Only the horizontal survives.

✅ He is 3 km EAST of his starting point.
```

### 🔍 Example 3 — Both directions survive → Pythagoras
*A man walks 12 m North, then 5 m East. How far is he from the starting point?*
```
Step 1: Net North = 12 m, Net East = 5 m. Neither cancels.

Step 2: These form a right-angled triangle.

              5 m
           ┌───────  END
           │      ╱
      12 m │   ╱  ← this is what we want
           │╱
         START

Step 3: Distance = √(12² + 5²) = √(144 + 25) = √169 = 13

✅ 13 metres, in the North-East direction.
```

### 🔑 Pythagorean triplets — memorize these, they're always used
```
3 – 4 – 5             6 –  8 – 10           9 – 12 – 15
5 – 12 – 13           8 – 15 – 17           7 – 24 – 25
```
> 💡 Exam questions **always** use these numbers so the answer comes out whole. If you see 6 and 8, the answer is 10 — don't even compute.

### 🔍 Example 4 — Multiple legs
*A man walks 3 km East, 4 km North, 3 km West, then 6 km South. Where is he relative to the start?*
```
Step 1: Vertical:   North 4, South 6  →  4 – 6 = –2, i.e. 2 km SOUTH
Step 2: Horizontal: East 3, West 3    →  3 – 3 = 0. Cancels.
Step 3: Only vertical survives.

✅ 2 km SOUTH of the starting point.
```

## 📖 PART 4 — SHADOW PROBLEMS

The sun rises in the **East** and sets in the **West**. **A shadow always falls opposite the sun.**

| Time | Sun is in the… | So the shadow falls to the… |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning / sunrise** | East | **WEST** |
| **Noon (12 pm)** | overhead | no shadow (or negligible) |
| **Evening / sunset** | West | **EAST** |

### 🔍 Example 5
*Early one morning, a man walks towards a pole and finds his shadow falling exactly to his right. Which direction is he facing?*
```
Step 1: Early morning → the sun is in the EAST → shadows fall WEST.
Step 2: His shadow is on his RIGHT. So his right hand points WEST.
Step 3: Stand up and try it: if your right hand points West, you face SOUTH.
        (Check with the compass: facing South, right = West ✔)
✅ He faces SOUTH
```
> 💡 **Physically act it out** with your own hands under the desk. It takes 3 seconds and is far more reliable than reasoning it out abstractly.

## 📖 PART 5 — LEFT / RIGHT WHEN A PERSON IS FACING YOU

```
If two people face EACH OTHER, their left and right are OPPOSITE.
```
*A faces North. B stands in front of A, facing A (so B faces South). Something on A's left is on B's right.*

> ⚠️ Watch for this in questions phrased as *"X is to the right of Y, who is facing X."*

## ⚡ SHORTCUTS & SPEED TRICKS

1. **DRAW. EVERY. TIME.** Draw the compass in the corner of your sheet at the start of the exam. Then draw each path.

2. **Start each path at the bottom-left of your rough space** so you have room to go North and East.

3. **Track only two numbers:** net vertical (N–S) and net horizontal (E–W). Ignore the intermediate route entirely — only the totals matter.

4. **Memorize the Pythagorean triplets.** 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 6-8-10, 8-15-17, 9-12-15.

5. **Diagonal directions:** if the net is North + East, the direction is **North-East**. North + West → **North-West**. And so on. If the question wants more precision, it's "North-East" unless the two legs are equal.

6. **"Which direction is he facing?" vs "where is he?" are different questions.** Facing = the direction of his last movement/turn. Position = the net displacement. Read carefully.

7. **Turning right 3 times = turning left once.** Saves you a step.

8. **Use your hands.** Point your right hand and physically rotate. This is not cheating — it's the fastest method.

## ✍️ PRACTICE (do these — 10 min)

1. A man facing North turns right. Which direction does he face?
2. A man facing South turns left. Which direction does he face?
3. A man facing East turns right twice. Which direction does he face?
4. A man walks 5 km North, 3 km East, then 5 km South. Where is he relative to the start?
5. A man walks 4 km North, then 3 km West. How far is he from the start and in which direction?
6. A man walks 10 m South, 6 m West, then 10 m North. Where is he relative to the start?
7. A man walks 3 km East, 4 km North, then 3 km West. Where is he relative to the start?
8. A person facing North turns 90° clockwise, then 180° anticlockwise. Which direction does he face?
9. Early in the morning, the shadow of a pole falls in which direction?
10. A man walks 12 m North, then 5 m East. How far is he from his starting point?
11. A man walks 8 km North, 6 km East, then 8 km South. Where is he relative to the start?
12. A man walks 3 km East, 4 km North, 3 km West, then 6 km South. Where is he relative to the start?
13. In the evening, a man's shadow falls to his left. Which direction is he facing?

### ✅ ANSWERS

1. **East** — right from North is clockwise one step
2. **East** — left from South is anticlockwise: S → E
3. **West** — East → South → West
4. **3 km East** — the 5 N and 5 S cancel
5. **5 km, North-West** — √(4² + 3²) = √25 = 5; net is North + West
6. **6 m West** — the 10 S and 10 N cancel
7. **4 km North** — the 3 E and 3 W cancel
8. **West** — North → (90° cw) East → (180°) West
9. **West** — the morning sun is in the East, so shadows fall West
10. **13 m** — √(144 + 25) = 13
11. **6 km East** — the 8 N and 8 S cancel
12. **2 km South** — vertical 4 N – 6 S = 2 S; horizontal cancels
13. **South** — Evening → the sun is in the West → the shadow falls **East**. The shadow is on his **left**, so his left hand points East. Now check the compass: facing South, your left hand points East ✔ So he faces **South**.

## 🎯 QUICK RECAP — say these out loud

- **N**ever **E**at **S**oggy **W**heat → N, E, S, W clockwise
- **Right = clockwise. Left = anticlockwise.**
- Facing North: right → East, left → West
- **ALWAYS DRAW.** Never solve in your head.
- Track only **net vertical (N–S)** and **net horizontal (E–W)**
- Both non-zero → **Pythagoras**. Memorize 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 6-8-10, 8-15-17.
- **Morning sun East → shadow West.** Evening sun West → shadow East.
- Two people facing each other have **opposite** left and right
