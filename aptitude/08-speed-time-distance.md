# 8️⃣ SPEED, TIME & DISTANCE ⭐⭐

⏱️ **Study time: 45 minutes** | 🎯 **Typical questions: 3–4 directly, plus it powers Trains and Boats**

> 🔥 **Study this before Trains and Boats & Streams.** Those two topics are just this one with extra vocabulary. Three topics, one set of formulas.

---

## 📖 PART 1 — THE ONE FORMULA (and its triangle)

```
Distance  =  Speed  ×  Time

              Distance                          Distance
Speed  =  ────────────            Time  =  ────────────
                Time                              Speed
```

```
        ┌─────────────┐
        │      D      │      Cover the one you want:
        ├──────┬──────┤      Cover D → S × T
        │  S   │  T   │      Cover S → D / T
        └──────┴──────┘      Cover T → D / S
```

### 🚨 THE UNIT RULE — the #1 source of errors
**Speed, time and distance must use matching units.** If speed is in km/h, time must be in hours and distance in km.

```
km/h  →  m/s     :   multiply by  5/18
m/s   →  km/h    :   multiply by  18/5
```

> 💡 **Memory hook:** going to the **s**maller unit (m/s), use the **s**maller fraction (5/18).

### 🔍 Practice the conversion right now
```
72 km/h  →  72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s
90 km/h  →  90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s
54 km/h  →  54 × 5/18 = 15 m/s
36 km/h  →  36 × 5/18 = 10 m/s

20 m/s   →  20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h
25 m/s   →  25 × 18/5 = 90 km/h
```

**Worth memorising these four** — they appear in almost every train question:

| km/h | m/s |
|---|---|
| **18** | 5 |
| **36** | 10 |
| **54** | 15 |
| **72** | 20 |
| **90** | 25 |

## 📖 PART 2 — BASIC WORKED EXAMPLES

### 🔍 Example 1
*A car travels at 60 km/h for 2.5 hours. Find the distance.*
```
Step 1: D = S × T
Step 2: D = 60 × 2.5 = 150 km
✅ 150 km
```

### 🔍 Example 2 — Watch the units
*A man covers 600 metres in 5 minutes. Find his speed in km/h.*
```
Step 1: Decide the units. We want km/h, so convert to km and hours.
        600 m = 0.6 km
        5 minutes = 5/60 = 1/12 hour

Step 2: Speed = 0.6 ÷ (1/12) = 0.6 × 12 = 7.2 km/h

✅ 7.2 km/h

(Alternative route: 600 m / 300 s = 2 m/s, then 2 × 18/5 = 7.2 km/h ✔ Same answer.)
```

## 📖 PART 3 — THE INVERSE RELATIONSHIP ⭐⭐

For a **fixed distance**, speed and time are inversely proportional.

```
Speed ratio   a : b     →     Time ratio   b : a       (flip it)
```

### 🔍 Example 3
*Two cars cover the same distance. Their speeds are in the ratio 3 : 4. Find the ratio of the times taken.*
```
Step 1: Same distance → speed and time are inverse.
Step 2: Speed ratio 3 : 4  →  Time ratio 4 : 3
✅ 4 : 3

Logic check: the FASTER car (speed 4) takes the SMALLER time (3). ✔
```

### 🔍 Example 4 — Percentage version ⭐
*If a man increases his speed by 25%, by what percent does his travel time decrease?*
```
Step 1: New speed = 125% of old = 5/4 of old
Step 2: Time is inverse, so new time = 4/5 of old = 0.8 × old
Step 3: Decrease = 1 – 0.8 = 0.2 → 20%
✅ Time decreases by 20%
```
> 🧠 **This is exactly the "more/less reversal" from [Percentage](02-percentage.md):** speed up x% → time down `x/(100+x) × 100 %`. Here: 25/125 × 100 = 20%.

**Handy table:**
| Speed increases by | Time decreases by |
|---|---|
| 20% | 16.67% |
| 25% | 20% |
| 50% | 33.33% |
| 100% | 50% |

## 📖 PART 4 — AVERAGE SPEED ⭐⭐

```
                    Total distance
Average speed  =  ──────────────────
                    Total time
```

⚠️ **NEVER average the two speeds** — unless the *times* are equal.

| The question says | Use |
|---|---|
| same **distance** at x and at y | **2xy / (x + y)** |
| same **time** at x and at y | **(x + y) / 2** |
| three equal **distances** | **3xyz / (xy + yz + zx)** |

### 🔍 Example 5
*A man goes from A to B at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h. Find the average speed for the whole journey.*
```
Step 1: "Goes and returns" → the DISTANCE is the same both ways.
        So use 2xy/(x+y).

Step 2: = 2 × 60 × 40 / (60 + 40)
Step 3: = 4800 / 100
Step 4: = 48 km/h

✅ 48 km/h  — NOT 50 km/h

Why? He spends more TIME at the slower speed, so 40 gets more weight.
The answer is always pulled toward the slower speed.
```

### 🔍 Example 6 — Read carefully!
*A man travels for 2 hours at 40 km/h and then for 2 hours at 60 km/h. Find the average speed.*
```
Step 1: This time the TIMES are equal, not the distances.
Step 2: So it IS the simple average: (40 + 60)/2 = 50 km/h
✅ 50 km/h

Compare with Example 5 — same numbers-style question, different answer,
purely because "equal time" vs "equal distance" changed.
ALWAYS check which one is equal.
```

## 📖 PART 5 — THE "LATE / EARLY" PATTERN ⭐⭐ (very commonly asked)

*"At speed x he is late by t₁; at speed y he is early by t₂. Find the distance."*

**Method: the distance is the same, so write both times and subtract.**

### 🔍 Example 7
*Walking at 4 km/h, a man reaches his office 10 minutes late. Walking at 5 km/h, he reaches 5 minutes early. Find the distance to his office.*

```
Step 1: Let the distance = d km.

Step 2: Write the time taken at each speed.
        At 4 km/h → time = d/4 hours
        At 5 km/h → time = d/5 hours

Step 3: What's the difference between these two times?
        One arrival is 10 min LATE, the other is 5 min EARLY.
        Total gap = 10 + 5 = 15 minutes = 15/60 = 1/4 hour

        ⚠️ Add them because they're on opposite sides of the on-time moment.

Step 4: The slower speed takes longer, so:
        d/4 – d/5 = 1/4

Step 5: Solve. LCM of 4 and 5 is 20.
        5d/20 – 4d/20 = 1/4
        d/20 = 1/4
        d = 5

✅ Distance = 5 km

VERIFY: at 4 km/h → 1.25 hr = 75 min.  At 5 km/h → 1 hr = 60 min.
        Difference = 15 min ✔  (And the on-time duration is 65 min.)
```

> 🔑 **The rule for combining the times:**
> - one **late**, one **early** → **ADD** them
> - both **late** (or both **early**) → **SUBTRACT** them

## 📖 PART 6 — RELATIVE SPEED ⭐⭐

This is the foundation of Trains and Boats.

```
Moving in OPPOSITE directions (towards or away)  →  ADD the speeds
Moving in the SAME direction (one chasing)       →  SUBTRACT the speeds
```

### 🔍 Example 8 — Opposite directions
*Two trains are 100 km apart and move towards each other at 40 km/h and 60 km/h. When will they meet?*
```
Step 1: Opposite directions → relative speed = 40 + 60 = 100 km/h
Step 2: Time = Distance / Relative speed = 100 / 100 = 1 hour
✅ 1 hour
```

### 🔍 Example 9 — Same direction (a chase)
*A thief runs at 8 km/h. A policeman, 1 km behind him, chases at 10 km/h. How long until the policeman catches him?*
```
Step 1: Same direction → relative speed = 10 – 8 = 2 km/h
        (The gap closes at only 2 km/h, even though both are moving fast.)

Step 2: The gap to close = 1 km

Step 3: Time = 1 / 2 = 0.5 hour = 30 minutes

✅ 30 minutes
```
> 💡 **Think of it as: freeze the thief, and let the policeman run at 2 km/h.** That mental picture makes every relative-speed question easy.

## 📖 PART 7 — RACES (quick coverage)

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "A beats B by 20 m" | when A finishes, B is 20 m from the finish line |
| "A gives B a start of 20 m" | B begins 20 m ahead; B runs only (100 – 20) m |
| "A beats B by 5 seconds" | B needs 5 more seconds to finish |
| "dead heat" | they finish at exactly the same time |

### 🔍 Example 10
*In a 100 m race, A beats B by 20 m. Find the ratio of their speeds.*
```
Step 1: In the same TIME, A covers 100 m while B covers only 80 m.
Step 2: Same time → speeds are in the ratio of the distances.
Step 3: Ratio = 100 : 80 = 5 : 4
✅ 5 : 4
```

## ⚡ SHORTCUTS & SPEED TRICKS

1. **Convert units FIRST, before anything else.** Write the conversion at the top of your rough work. Most errors in this topic are unit errors, not method errors.

2. **km/h → m/s: × 5/18.** Memorize 36 = 10, 54 = 15, 72 = 20, 90 = 25.

3. **Same distance → speed and time flip.** Ratio 3:4 becomes 4:3.

4. **Average speed always leans toward the SLOWER speed.** Use this to kill MCQ options instantly — if the speeds are 40 and 60, the answer must be below 50.

5. **Late/early:** one late + one early → **add** the times. Both same side → **subtract**.

6. **Relative speed:** opposite → **add**; same direction → **subtract**. Then treat it as one object moving and one standing still.

7. **When no distance is given, assume a convenient one** — usually the LCM of the speeds. For speeds 40 and 60, assume 120 km. All the arithmetic becomes clean.

8. **Watch the "minutes vs hours" trap.** 15 minutes is 1/4 hour, not 15.

## 🎯 QUICK RECAP — say these out loud

- **D = S × T.** Match your units before you calculate.
- **km/h → m/s: × 5/18.** (36 = 10, 54 = 15, 72 = 20, 90 = 25)
- Same distance → **speed and time are inverse** (flip the ratio)
- Average speed = **total distance / total time**, never the average of the speeds
- Equal **distances** → **2xy/(x+y)**. Equal **times** → **(x+y)/2**
- Speed up x% → time down **x/(100+x) × 100 %**
- Late + early → **ADD** the time gaps. Both late → **SUBTRACT**.
- Relative speed: opposite → **add**, same direction → **subtract**
