# 1️⃣9️⃣ READING COMPREHENSION 🟢

⏱️ **Study time: 20 minutes** | 🎯 **Typical questions: 4–5 (one passage)**

> 🟢 **You don't "study" comprehension — you learn a TECHNIQUE.** Every answer is already printed in the passage. The only skills being tested are (a) finding it fast and (b) not falling for the trap options. This file is about technique, not knowledge.

---

## 📖 PART 1 — THE GOLDEN RULE

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  THE ANSWER IS ALWAYS IN THE PASSAGE.                        │
│                                                              │
│  Never use your own knowledge, opinions, or common sense.    │
│  If the passage doesn't say it, it isn't the answer —        │
│  even if you know it's true in real life.                    │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

This single rule eliminates most wrong answers. Exam writers deliberately include options that are **factually true but not stated in the passage**. Those options are wrong.

---

## 📖 PART 2 — THE METHOD ⭐⭐ (read questions first)

```
STEP 1:  Read the QUESTIONS first (30 seconds).       ← counter-intuitive, but it works
         Don't read the options yet — just the question stems.
         Now you know what you're hunting for.

STEP 2:  Skim the passage (60–90 seconds).
         Read the FIRST and LAST sentence of each paragraph carefully.
         Skim the middle.

STEP 3:  Answer the fact-based questions first (they're findable).
         Underline the exact line in the passage that proves your answer.

STEP 4:  Then do the inference and tone questions.

STEP 5:  Do the vocabulary-in-context question last.
```

> 🔑 **Why read the questions first?** Because then your skim becomes a targeted search instead of general reading. You'll spot the relevant sentence as you pass it, instead of having to re-read the whole passage 5 times.

---

## 📖 PART 3 — THE 5 QUESTION TYPES AND HOW TO ATTACK EACH

### ① FACT / DETAIL questions — *"According to the passage, X is…"*
```
✔ EASIEST TYPE. The answer is written almost word-for-word in the passage.
✔ Method: find the keyword from the question in the passage, read that
  sentence AND the one after it.
⚠️ Trap: an option that changes ONE word (e.g. "always" instead of "often",
  "all" instead of "most"). Compare word by word.
```

### ② MAIN IDEA / TITLE questions — *"The best title for this passage is…"*
```
✔ Method: the main idea is usually in the FIRST or LAST paragraph.
✔ The right answer covers the WHOLE passage.
⚠️ Trap: an option that's true but only covers ONE paragraph. Too narrow.
⚠️ Trap: an option that's much broader than the passage. Too wide.

Ask: "Does this title fit every paragraph, or just one?"
```

### ③ INFERENCE questions — *"It can be inferred that…"*
```
✔ Method: the answer is NOT stated directly, but must follow LOGICALLY
  and NECESSARILY from what IS stated.
⚠️ Trap: options that go one step too far. If the passage says sales "grew
  slowly", you can infer growth — you CANNOT infer the company is profitable.

Rule: choose the SMALLEST logical step, never the biggest.
```

### ④ TONE / ATTITUDE questions — *"The author's tone is…"*
```
✔ Method: look at the ADJECTIVES the author chooses.
  Positive words → approving. Negative words → critical.
  No emotional words at all → neutral/objective/informative.
⚠️ Most placement-test passages are NEUTRAL or INFORMATIVE.
  Extreme options (furious, sarcastic, contemptuous) are usually wrong.
```
**Common tone words:**
| Positive | Neutral | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| optimistic, appreciative, enthusiastic, sympathetic | objective, informative, analytical, descriptive, factual | critical, pessimistic, sceptical, ironic, dismissive |

### ⑤ VOCABULARY-IN-CONTEXT — *"The word 'X' in the passage means…"*
```
✔ Method: COVER the word. Read the sentence. Guess what fits the blank.
  Then pick the option closest to your guess.
⚠️ Trap: the dictionary's most common meaning, when the passage uses a
  secondary meaning. CONTEXT WINS over the dictionary.
```

## 📖 PART 4 — WORKED EXAMPLE (do this with me)

### 📄 Passage
> Remote work, once considered a rare privilege, has become a standard arrangement for millions of employees worldwide. The shift was accelerated dramatically by the global disruptions of 2020, which forced organisations to adopt distributed working models almost overnight. Many companies discovered, somewhat to their surprise, that productivity did not collapse. In several documented cases, output actually rose modestly.
>
> However, the transition has not been without cost. Employees frequently report a blurring of the boundary between professional and personal life, leading to longer working hours and, in some cases, burnout. Junior staff in particular appear to suffer, as they lose the informal mentoring that occurs naturally in a shared office. Organisations are now experimenting with hybrid models in an attempt to capture the flexibility of remote work while preserving the collaborative benefits of physical presence.

---

### 🔍 Q1 (FACT) — According to the passage, what happened to productivity when companies shifted to remote work?
```
Step 1: Keyword is "productivity". Find it.
        → "productivity did not collapse. In several documented cases,
           output actually rose modestly."

Step 2: Read it precisely. It says productivity did NOT collapse, and in
        SEVERAL cases rose MODESTLY.

Step 3: Check the wording of the options carefully.
        ✘ "Productivity increased dramatically"   → the passage says MODESTLY
        ✘ "Productivity fell sharply"             → the opposite
        ✔ "Productivity did not fall, and in some cases rose slightly"

✅ Productivity did not decline; in some cases it rose slightly.
```
> 🚨 **Note how "dramatically" vs "modestly" decides the question.** This is the single most common trap in RC. **Read the qualifying adverbs.**

### 🔍 Q2 (INFERENCE) — It can be inferred that junior employees are disadvantaged by remote work because…
```
Step 1: Find the junior staff sentence.
        → "Junior staff in particular appear to suffer, as they lose the
           informal mentoring that occurs naturally in a shared office."

Step 2: The passage gives ONE reason: loss of informal mentoring.

Step 3: Evaluate options.
        ✘ "They are less skilled with technology"  → never mentioned. Outside knowledge!
        ✘ "They are paid less"                     → never mentioned
        ✔ "They miss out on informal learning from colleagues"

✅ They lose the informal mentoring that a shared office provides.
```

### 🔍 Q3 (MAIN IDEA) — The best title for this passage would be:
```
Step 1: What does paragraph 1 cover? The rise of remote work and its
        surprising productivity results.
        What does paragraph 2 cover? The costs, and the move to hybrid models.

Step 2: A good title must cover BOTH.
        ✘ "The Productivity Miracle of Remote Work"  → only paragraph 1. Too narrow.
        ✘ "The Dangers of Burnout"                   → only part of paragraph 2. Too narrow.
        ✘ "The History of Employment"                → far too broad.
        ✔ "Remote Work: Benefits, Costs and the Hybrid Response"

✅ Remote Work: Benefits, Costs and the Hybrid Response
```

### 🔍 Q4 (TONE) — The author's tone in this passage is best described as:
```
Step 1: Look for emotional language. The author presents positives
        ("output actually rose") AND negatives ("burnout") without
        taking a side.

Step 2: There are no angry, mocking, or celebratory words anywhere.
        ✘ "Enthusiastic"  → too positive
        ✘ "Critical"      → too negative
        ✔ "Balanced / objective"

✅ Objective and balanced
```

### 🔍 Q5 (VOCABULARY) — In the passage, the word "blurring" most nearly means:
```
Step 1: Cover the word and read: "a ______ of the boundary between
        professional and personal life."

Step 2: What fits? Something like "the line becoming unclear / merging".

Step 3: ✘ "Strengthening"  → the opposite
        ✘ "Removal"        → too strong; the boundary is unclear, not gone
        ✔ "Becoming indistinct"

✅ Becoming indistinct / less clear
```

## ⚡ SHORTCUTS & SPEED TRICKS

1. **Read the QUESTIONS first**, then skim the passage. This is the biggest single time-saver.

2. **Underline the proof.** For every answer, physically mark the line in the passage that justifies it. If you can't find a line, your answer is probably wrong.

3. **Watch the QUALIFYING WORDS.** These decide most questions:
   ```
   always / never / all / every / only        ← ABSOLUTE (usually wrong)
   often / some / may / might / generally     ← QUALIFIED (usually right)
   ```
   > 💡 **Options with absolute words are wrong far more often than not**, because passages rarely make absolute claims. If an option says "always" and the passage says "often", reject it.

4. **Eliminate, don't select.** Cross out the clearly wrong options first. Usually 2 die immediately, leaving a coin-flip you can reason through.

5. **For inference, choose the SMALLEST step.** The most cautious-sounding option is usually correct.

6. **Never use outside knowledge.** If it's not in the passage, it's not the answer.

7. **Don't read the whole passage word-by-word twice.** Skim once, then re-read only the relevant sentences.

8. **Do the passage LAST** if it's long, but **do not skip it** — 4–5 marks are sitting there and no calculation is required.

9. **If two options seem equally right, one of them says something slightly stronger.** Pick the weaker one.

## ✍️ PRACTICE

### 📄 Passage
> The introduction of the bicycle in the late nineteenth century had social consequences that its inventors never anticipated. For the first time, ordinary people could travel several miles from home cheaply and without depending on animals or railway timetables. Historians have argued that this modest machine did more to widen the social horizons of the working classes than any political reform of the period.
>
> Its effect on women was particularly striking. Cycling required practical clothing, which contributed to the decline of restrictive Victorian dress. It also allowed unmarried women to travel unaccompanied, something that had previously been difficult. Some contemporary commentators were alarmed, publishing warnings about the supposed medical dangers of cycling for women. These claims had no scientific basis, but they reveal how threatening the new mobility appeared to those invested in the existing social order.

**Answer these (10 min):**

1. According to the passage, what did the bicycle allow ordinary people to do for the first time?
2. What effect did cycling have on women's clothing?
3. Why did some commentators publish warnings about women cycling?
4. What is the author's attitude towards the medical warnings mentioned in the passage?
5. In the passage, the word "striking" most nearly means:
6. What would be the best title for this passage?

---

### ✅ ANSWERS

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal</summary>

1. **Travel several miles from home cheaply, without depending on animals or railway timetables.** (Stated directly in paragraph 1. Note the three specific elements — cheap, no animals, no timetables.)

2. **It contributed to the decline of restrictive Victorian dress**, because cycling required practical clothing. (Direct fact, paragraph 2.)

3. **Because the new mobility for women felt threatening to those who benefited from the existing social order.** (This is the author's explanation in the final sentence. ⚠️ Note the trap: the warnings were *presented* as medical concerns, but the passage tells us the real reason was social anxiety.)

4. **Dismissive of their validity, but interested in what they reveal.** The author states plainly that the claims "had no scientific basis" — so he rejects them as science — yet treats them as useful historical evidence. (An option saying merely "the author agrees with them" or "the author ignores them" would both be wrong.)

5. **Notable / remarkable / pronounced.** Cover the word: "Its effect on women was particularly ______." The context needs a word meaning "significant", not the literal "hitting" sense of strike. **Context beats the dictionary.**

6. **"The Bicycle and Social Change in the Nineteenth Century"** — it covers both paragraphs (general social effects + the specific effect on women).
   - ✘ "Victorian Women's Fashion" → too narrow, only part of paragraph 2
   - ✘ "The History of Transport" → too broad, the passage is only about the bicycle's social effects
   - ✘ "Medical Myths of the 1890s" → a detail, not the main idea

</details>

## 🎯 QUICK RECAP — say these out loud

- **The answer is ALWAYS in the passage.** Never use outside knowledge.
- **Read the QUESTIONS first**, then skim the passage
- **Underline the line that proves** each answer
- Watch **qualifying words**: "always/all/never/only" options are usually **wrong**; "often/some/may" options are usually **right**
- Fact questions → find the keyword, read that sentence **and the next one**
- Main idea → must cover the **whole** passage, not one paragraph
- Inference → take the **smallest** logical step
- Tone → usually **neutral/objective** in placement tests; extreme options are wrong
- Vocabulary → **cover the word, guess the blank**, then match. Context beats the dictionary.
- **Eliminate wrong options** rather than hunting for the right one
