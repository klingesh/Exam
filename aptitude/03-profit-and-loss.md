# 3️⃣ PROFIT & LOSS ⭐⭐

⏱️ **Study time: 45 minutes** | 🎯 **Typical questions: 3–5**

> 📌 The syllabus lists "Profit and loss" twice — that's a strong hint it carries decent weight. Good news: it's just [percentage](02-percentage.md) applied to buying and selling.

---

## 📖 PART 1 — THE VOCABULARY (get this right and half the battle is won)

| Term | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost Price** | CP | What the seller **paid** |
| **Selling Price** | SP | What the seller **sold it for** |
| **Marked Price** | MP | The **label/list price** before discount |
| **Profit** | — | SP – CP (when SP > CP) |
| **Loss** | — | CP – SP (when CP > SP) |
| **Discount** | — | MP – SP |
| **Overheads** | — | Extra costs (repair, transport) — **add these to CP** |

### 🚨 The single biggest rule in this topic
```
Profit % and Loss %      are ALWAYS calculated on  CP
Discount %               is ALWAYS calculated on  MP
```
Mixing these up is the #1 reason students lose marks here. Write it on your formula sheet.

---

## 📖 PART 2 — CORE FORMULAS

```
Profit    = SP – CP                    Loss    = CP – SP

Profit %  = (Profit / CP) × 100        Loss %  = (Loss / CP) × 100

Discount  = MP – SP                    Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100
```

### The multiplier forms (use these — much faster)

```
Profit of x%   →   SP = CP × (100 + x)/100        e.g. 25% profit → SP = 1.25 × CP
Loss of x%     →   SP = CP × (100 – x)/100        e.g. 20% loss   → SP = 0.80 × CP

Going backwards (find CP from SP):
CP = SP × 100/(100 + x)   for profit
CP = SP × 100/(100 – x)   for loss

Discount of d% →   SP = MP × (100 – d)/100
```

> 💡 **Always ask yourself: "which one am I solving for?"** If the question gives SP and profit%, you must **divide** to get CP, not multiply. This trips up more people than any formula.

## 📖 PART 3 — WORKED EXAMPLES, STEP BY STEP

### 🔍 Example 1 — Basic profit %
*An article costing ₹400 is sold for ₹500. Find the profit percent.*
```
Step 1: Profit = SP – CP = 500 – 400 = ₹100
Step 2: Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100        ← divide by CP, which is 400
Step 3:          = (100 / 400) × 100
Step 4:          = 0.25 × 100 = 25%
✅ Profit = 25%
```

### 🔍 Example 2 — Find SP from CP and profit %
*CP = ₹1,250. The trader wants a 16% profit. Find SP.*
```
Step 1: Profit of 16% → multiplier is (100 + 16)/100 = 1.16
Step 2: SP = 1250 × 1.16
Step 3:    = 1450
✅ SP = ₹1,450
```

### 🔍 Example 3 — Find CP from SP (the reverse — go slow here)
*An article is sold for ₹1,200 at a profit of 20%. Find the CP.*
```
Step 1: DON'T do 1200 – 20% . That is wrong, because 20% is on CP, not SP.

Step 2: Set it up properly.
        SP = CP × 1.20
        1200 = CP × 1.20

Step 3: CP = 1200 / 1.20 = 1000

✅ CP = ₹1,000
   Check: profit = 200, and 200/1000 = 20% ✔

   (The wrong method would have given 1200 – 240 = ₹960. Note how different that is.)
```

### 🔍 Example 4 — Marked price, discount and profit together ⭐
*A shopkeeper marks an article 25% above its cost price of ₹500 and then gives a 12% discount. Find his profit percent.*
```
Step 1: Find MP. Marked 25% ABOVE CP → MP = 500 × 1.25 = ₹625

Step 2: Apply the discount to MP (not to CP!).
        Discount 12% → SP = 625 × 0.88 = ₹550

Step 3: Now compare SP with CP.
        Profit = 550 – 500 = ₹50

Step 4: Profit % = (50 / 500) × 100 = 10%

✅ Profit = 10%
```
> ⚡ **Faster route for this pattern:** just multiply the multipliers.
> `1.25 × 0.88 = 1.10` → the final SP is 1.10 × CP → **10% profit**. No need to know the actual ₹500 at all.

### 🔍 Example 5 — The "had he sold it for ₹X more" pattern ⭐⭐
*A man sells an article at a 10% loss. Had he sold it for ₹90 more, he would have gained 5%. Find the cost price.*
```
Step 1: Write BOTH selling prices in terms of CP.
        At 10% loss  → SP₁ = 0.90 × CP
        At 5% gain    → SP₂ = 1.05 × CP

Step 2: The problem says SP₂ is ₹90 more than SP₁.
        SP₂ – SP₁ = 90

Step 3: Substitute.
        1.05CP – 0.90CP = 90
        0.15CP = 90

Step 4: CP = 90 / 0.15 = 600

✅ CP = ₹600
   Check: SP₁ = 540 (loss 60 = 10% ✔), SP₂ = 630 (gain 30 = 5% ✔), difference 90 ✔
```
> 🧠 **The pattern:** the ₹ difference divided by the **% difference** gives you the CP.
> Here: 90 ÷ 15% = 600. The total swing was from –10% to +5%, i.e. **15 percentage points**.

### 🔍 Example 6 — Chain of sellers
*A sells to B at 20% profit, B sells to C at 25% profit. C pays ₹1,500. What did A pay?*
```
Step 1: Chain the multipliers from A's cost.
        1500 = A's CP × 1.20 × 1.25

Step 2: 1.20 × 1.25 = 1.50

Step 3: 1500 = A's CP × 1.50
        A's CP = 1500 / 1.50 = 1000

✅ A paid ₹1,000
```

## 📖 PART 4 — THE 4 SPECIAL FORMULAS ⭐⭐

These are pure mark-grabbers. Each one turns a 3-minute problem into 10 seconds.

### ① Two articles at the same SP, one at x% profit and one at x% loss
```
There is ALWAYS a net LOSS of  x²/100 %
```
*Two shirts sold at ₹960 each, one at 20% profit, one at 20% loss. Overall?*
```
Net loss = 20²/100 = 400/100 = 4% loss
```
> ⚠️ Students almost always answer "no profit no loss." **It is always a loss.** Never zero.

### ② Dishonest shopkeeper using a false weight
```
                    Error
Gain % =  ───────────────────────  × 100
           True weight – Error
```
*A shopkeeper claims to sell at cost price but uses a 900 g weight for 1 kg. Find his gain %.*
```
Step 1: Error = 1000 – 900 = 100 g
Step 2: Gain % = 100 / (1000 – 100) × 100
Step 3:        = 100/900 × 100 = 11.11%
✅ Gain = 11.11%
```
> 💡 Simpler way to think about it: he gives 900 g but charges for 1000 g. So he gains 100 on a cost of 900 → 100/900 = **1/9 = 11.11%**.

### ③ "SP of m articles = CP of n articles"
```
Profit % = (n – m)/m × 100
```
*The SP of 12 articles equals the CP of 15 articles. Find the profit %.*
```
Profit % = (15 – 12)/12 × 100 = 3/12 × 100 = 25%
✅ 25% profit
```

### ④ Successive discounts (same as successive percentage)
```
Equivalent single discount = d₁ + d₂ – (d₁ × d₂)/100
```
*Discounts of 20% and 10%:*  `20 + 10 – 200/100 = 28%` — **not 30%.**

## ⚡ SHORTCUTS & SPEED TRICKS

1. **Assume CP = 100 whenever no actual value is given.** This turns almost every "find the profit %" question into mental arithmetic. It is the single best habit in this topic.

2. **Multiply the multipliers.** Marked up 40%, then 25% discount?
   `1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05` → **5% profit.** Done in 5 seconds.

3. **Profit % > Discount %** happens when the markup is high. Don't be suspicious of it — it's normal.

4. **When the answer must be a "nice" number,** work with fractions:
   - 25% profit → SP/CP = 5/4
   - 20% loss → SP/CP = 4/5
   - 12.5% profit → SP/CP = 9/8

5. **Overheads/repairs are added to CP** before computing profit %. If a question mentions transport or repair charges, they are part of the cost.

6. **For the "₹X more/less" pattern:** `CP = (₹ difference) ÷ (percentage-point difference)`.

## 🎯 QUICK RECAP — say these out loud

- **Profit% and Loss% are on CP. Discount% is on MP.** Never mix them.
- SP = CP × (100 ± x)/100 &nbsp;&nbsp;|&nbsp;&nbsp; CP = SP × 100/(100 ± x)
- Assume **CP = 100** when no numbers are given
- **Multiply the multipliers** for markup-then-discount chains
- Same SP, +x% and –x% → **always x²/100 % LOSS**
- False weight → **Error / (True – Error) × 100**
- SP of m = CP of n → **(n–m)/m × 100**
- "₹X more" pattern → **CP = X ÷ (percentage-point gap)**
