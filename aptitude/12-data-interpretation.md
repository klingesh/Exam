# 1️⃣2️⃣ DATA INTERPRETATION

⏱️ **Study time: 30–45 minutes** | 🎯 **Typical questions: 4–5 (usually one set of 4–5 questions on a single chart)**

> 📌 **DI is not a maths topic — it's a READING topic.** You already know the maths (percentage, ratio, average). The only skill being tested is whether you can read a table correctly and calculate fast. That means DI is one of the most reliably scorable sections, **if** you don't panic at the wall of numbers.

---

## 📖 PART 1 — THE 5-STEP APPROACH (use this every single time)

```
STEP 1:  Read the TITLE and the UNITS.        ← 10 seconds, saves you the whole set
STEP 2:  Read the row and column headings.
STEP 3:  Read the QUESTION carefully.
STEP 4:  Pick out ONLY the numbers you need. Ignore everything else.
STEP 5:  Approximate wherever the options allow it.
```

### 🚨 THE UNITS TRAP — this is where most marks are lost
If the table says **"Production (in '000 tonnes)"** and the value shown is 45, the real value is **45,000 tonnes**.

> ✅ **Good news:** for **ratios and percentages, the units cancel out** — you can ignore them entirely.
> ⚠️ **But** if the question asks *"how many tonnes?"*, you must multiply by 1,000.
>
> **So: circle the unit line before you start.**

---

## 📖 PART 2 — THE ONLY 5 FORMULAS DI EVER USES

```
1. Total          →  add the row or column

2. Average        →  Total ÷ Number of items

3. Percentage     →  (Part / Whole) × 100

4. % change       →  [(New – Old) / Old] × 100        ← divide by the OLD value

5. Ratio          →  A : B  (then divide both by their HCF)
```

That's it. **There is no sixth formula.** Every DI question is one of these five.

## 📖 PART 3 — TYPE 1: TABLE DI ⭐ (most common)

### 📊 Data Set A — Car production by four companies (in thousands of units)

| Company | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 40 | 50 | 60 | **150** |
| **B** | 30 | 45 | 45 | **120** |
| **C** | 50 | 40 | 55 | **145** |
| **D** | 20 | 25 | 30 | **75** |
| **Total** | **140** | **160** | **190** | **490** |

> 💡 **Pro move:** the moment you see a table, **write the row totals and column totals in the margin.** It takes 20 seconds and about half the questions in the set will need them.

---

### 🔍 Q1 — What was A's total production over the three years?
```
Step 1: Read across row A: 40, 50, 60
Step 2: Add → 40 + 50 + 60 = 150
✅ 150 thousand units (= 1,50,000 units)
```

### 🔍 Q2 — What is the percentage increase in B's production from 2021 to 2022?
```
Step 1: Identify the two values. B in 2021 = 30, B in 2022 = 45
Step 2: Change = 45 – 30 = 15
Step 3: Divide by the OLD value (2021 = 30), not the new one.
        15 / 30 = 0.5
Step 4: × 100 → 50%
✅ 50% increase
```

### 🔍 Q3 — Which company had the highest total production?
```
Step 1: Compare the row totals: A = 150, B = 120, C = 145, D = 75
Step 2: Highest is 150
✅ Company A
```

### 🔍 Q4 — What is D's average annual production?
```
Step 1: D's total = 20 + 25 + 30 = 75
Step 2: Number of years = 3
Step 3: Average = 75 / 3 = 25
✅ 25 thousand units per year
```

### 🔍 Q5 — What is the ratio of A's 2023 production to D's 2023 production?
```
Step 1: A in 2023 = 60, D in 2023 = 30
Step 2: Ratio = 60 : 30
Step 3: Divide both by 30 → 2 : 1
✅ 2 : 1
```

### 🔍 Q6 — A's 2022 production is what percent of the total 2022 production?
```
Step 1: A in 2022 = 50. Total in 2022 = 160 (the column total).
Step 2: (50 / 160) × 100
Step 3: 50/160 = 0.3125
Step 4: × 100 = 31.25%
✅ 31.25%
```
> ⚡ **Speed tip:** notice 50/160 = 5/16, and 1/16 = 6.25%, so 5/16 = 5 × 6.25 = **31.25%**. The [fraction table](02-percentage.md) pays off here.

## 📖 PART 4 — TYPE 2: PIE CHART DI ⭐

A pie chart shows how a **whole** splits into **parts**. It's given either as percentages or as degrees.

```
Total circle = 360° = 100%

So:   1% = 3.6°        and        1° = 1/3.6 %  ≈  0.278%

To convert:   Degrees = Percentage × 3.6
              Percentage = Degrees / 3.6
```

### 📊 Data Set B — Monthly expenditure of a family, total income ₹36,000

| Category | Percentage |
|---|---|
| Food | 25% |
| Rent | 20% |
| Savings | 18% |
| Education | 15% |
| Clothing | 12% |
| Transport | 10% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

> ✅ **First thing to check on any pie chart: do the percentages add to 100?** (Here they do.) If a segment is missing, its value is `100 – (sum of the others)`. That's a very common Q1.

### 🔍 Q1 — How much is spent on food?
```
Step 1: Food = 25% of the total income.
Step 2: 25% = 1/4  →  36,000 / 4 = 9,000
✅ ₹9,000
```

### 🔍 Q2 — By how much does the rent exceed the transport cost?
```
Step 1: Work with the PERCENTAGE difference first — it's faster.
        Rent 20% – Transport 10% = 10%

Step 2: 10% of 36,000 = 3,600

✅ ₹3,600
```
> 💡 **Always subtract the percentages first, then convert once.** Don't compute ₹7,200 and ₹3,600 separately and then subtract — that's twice the work and twice the error risk.

### 🔍 Q3 — What is the ratio of expenditure on education to savings?
```
Step 1: Education = 15%, Savings = 18%
Step 2: Ratio = 15 : 18
Step 3: Both divide by 3 → 5 : 6
✅ 5 : 6

(Notice: we never needed the ₹36,000 at all. In a RATIO question the total cancels out.)
```

### 🔍 Q4 — What is the central angle of the food segment?
```
Step 1: Food = 25%
Step 2: Degrees = 25 × 3.6 = 90°
✅ 90°   (which makes sense — a quarter of the circle)
```

### 🔍 Q5 — How much is spent on food and rent together?
```
Step 1: Add the percentages: 25% + 20% = 45%
Step 2: 45% of 36,000 = 0.45 × 36,000 = 16,200
✅ ₹16,200
```

## 📖 PART 5 — TYPE 3: BAR / LINE GRAPH DI

A bar or line graph is just a table drawn with rectangles. **Read the values off the axis and treat it as a table.**

### 📊 Data Set C — Revenue and Expenditure of a company (₹ crore)

| Year | Revenue | Expenditure | Profit (Rev – Exp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 120 | 100 | 20 |
| 2020 | 150 | 120 | 30 |
| 2021 | 180 | 140 | 40 |
| 2022 | 200 | 150 | 50 |
| 2023 | 250 | 200 | 50 |

> 🔑 **In any revenue/expenditure graph, IMMEDIATELY write the profit column.** Almost every question needs it.

### 🔍 Q1 — In which year was the profit highest?
```
Step 1: Profits are 20, 30, 40, 50, 50
Step 2: The highest value (50) occurs in BOTH 2022 and 2023.
✅ 2022 and 2023 (tied)
```

### 🔍 Q2 — In which year was the percentage profit (on expenditure) highest?
```
Step 1: % profit = (Profit / Expenditure) × 100. Compute each:

        2019:  20/100 × 100 = 20.00%
        2020:  30/120 × 100 = 25.00%
        2021:  40/140 × 100 = 28.57%
        2022:  50/150 × 100 = 33.33%
        2023:  50/200 × 100 = 25.00%

Step 2: The highest is 33.33% in 2022.

✅ 2022
```
> 🚨 **This is the most important trap in bar-graph DI.** 2022 and 2023 have the **same** profit (₹50 cr), but 2022 has the **higher percentage** profit because its expenditure base was smaller. **"Highest profit" ≠ "highest profit percentage."** Read which one is being asked.

### 🔍 Q3 — What is the total profit over the five years?
```
Step 1: 20 + 30 + 40 + 50 + 50 = 190
✅ ₹190 crore
```

### 🔍 Q4 — What is the percentage increase in revenue from 2019 to 2023?
```
Step 1: Old = 120 (2019), New = 250 (2023)
Step 2: Change = 250 – 120 = 130
Step 3: 130 / 120 = 1.0833
Step 4: × 100 = 108.33%
✅ About 108.33% increase
```

### 🔍 Q5 — What is the average annual revenue?
```
Step 1: Total revenue = 120 + 150 + 180 + 200 + 250 = 900
Step 2: Average = 900 / 5 = 180
✅ ₹180 crore
```

## ⚡ SHORTCUTS & SPEED TRICKS

1. **Approximate boldly when the options are far apart.**
   `4,983 / 2,014` → think `5,000 / 2,000` = **2.5**. Done in 2 seconds.
   Only compute exactly when two options are close together.

2. **Fractions beat long division.** Learn these percentage-to-fraction equivalents:
   | % | Fraction |
   |---|---|
   | 50% | 1/2 |
   | 33.33% | 1/3 |
   | 25% | 1/4 |
   | 20% | 1/5 |
   | 16.67% | 1/6 |
   | 12.5% | 1/8 |
   | 6.25% | 1/16 |

3. **Ratio and percentage questions don't need the units or the grand total.** They cancel. Don't waste time converting.

4. **Write row and column totals in the margin immediately.**

5. **Don't compute what you don't need.** If a question asks about only company B, ignore A, C and D completely. DI sets are deliberately overloaded with data to slow you down.

6. **For "which is highest/lowest" questions, eyeball first.** You rarely need to compute all five options — usually only the top 2 candidates are close enough to matter.

7. **Percentage change: divide by the OLD value.** Say it every time.

8. **Do the DI set LAST** in the exam if it's a long one. Each set takes 4–6 minutes but is worth 4–5 marks, so it's a good deal — just not at the cost of quick single-mark questions elsewhere.

9. **Beware "profit" vs "profit %"**, and **"increase" vs "increase %"**. These pairs are different questions and both appear in the same set on purpose.

## ✍️ PRACTICE

### 📊 Data Set D — Marks obtained by 5 students in 4 subjects (out of 100 each)

| Student | Maths | Physics | Chemistry | English | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amit** | 80 | 70 | 75 | 65 | **290** |
| **Bina** | 90 | 85 | 60 | 70 | **305** |
| **Chetan** | 60 | 75 | 80 | 85 | **300** |
| **Deepa** | 70 | 65 | 90 | 75 | **300** |
| **Esha** | 85 | 80 | 70 | 60 | **295** |
| **Total** | **385** | **375** | **375** | **355** | **1490** |

**Answer these (12 min):**

1. Which student scored the highest total marks?
2. What is the average score in Mathematics across all five students?
3. What is Amit's overall percentage?
4. What is the ratio of Bina's Maths marks to Chetan's Maths marks?
5. Which subject has the lowest total marks?
6. What is Deepa's average score per subject?
7. Chetan's Chemistry marks are what percent of his total marks?
8. By what percent are Bina's Maths marks higher than Deepa's Maths marks?
9. What is the combined total of all students in Physics and Chemistry?
10. If the pass mark is 70 in each subject, how many subjects did Amit pass?

---

### ✅ ANSWERS

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal</summary>

1. **Bina** — 305, the highest row total
2. **77** — 385 / 5 = 77
3. **72.5%** — 290 / 400 × 100 = 72.5%  (4 subjects × 100 = 400 max)
4. **3 : 2** — 90 : 60, divide both by 30
5. **English** — 355, the lowest column total
6. **75** — 300 / 4 = 75
7. **26.67%** — 80 / 300 × 100 = 26.67%
8. **28.57%** — Bina 90, Deepa 70. Change = 20; 20/70 × 100 = 28.57% (divide by the OLD/base value, 70)
9. **750** — Physics 375 + Chemistry 375 = 750
10. **3 subjects** — Maths 80 ✔, Physics 70 ✔ (70 is the pass mark, so it passes), Chemistry 75 ✔, English 65 ✘

</details>

## 🎯 QUICK RECAP — say these out loud

- Read the **TITLE and UNITS** first. Circle the unit line.
- Only 5 formulas: **total, average, percentage, % change, ratio**
- **% change divides by the OLD value**
- Write **row and column totals** in the margin straight away
- **Ratios and percentages ignore the units** — they cancel
- **Approximate** when the options are far apart
- Use the **fraction table** instead of long division
- **"Highest profit" ≠ "highest profit %"** — read the question
- **Skip the data you don't need.** DI sets are padded on purpose.
