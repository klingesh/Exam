# 1️⃣8️⃣ ENGLISH GRAMMAR 🟢

⏱️ **Study time: 40 minutes** | 🎯 **Typical questions: 8–12 (grammar + vocabulary together — a big chunk of the paper)**

> 🟢 **HIGH VALUE.** English is usually ~20% of the paper and needs the least preparation. You already speak the language — you just need to know the specific **rules examiners test**. This file covers exactly those rules and nothing else.

---

## 📖 PART 1 — SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT ⭐⭐ (the most tested rule)

**The basic rule:** singular subject → singular verb. Plural subject → plural verb.

### The 8 rules examiners actually test

#### ① Words between the subject and verb don't change the verb
```
✔ The BOX of chocolates IS on the table.        (subject = box, not chocolates)
✘ The box of chocolates ARE on the table.
```
> 🔑 **Trick: mentally delete the "of ___" phrase.** "The box ~~of chocolates~~ is..." Now it's obvious.

#### ② "Each, every, either, neither, everyone, someone, nobody, anyone" are ALWAYS SINGULAR
```
✔ EACH of the students HAS a book.
✔ EVERYONE IS present.
✔ NEITHER of them WAS available.
✘ Each of the students have a book.
```

#### ③ Two subjects joined by "and" → PLURAL
```
✔ Ram and Shyam ARE friends.
```
**Exception:** if the two nouns describe one single thing, keep it singular.
```
✔ Bread and butter IS my breakfast.       (one dish)
✔ The rise and fall of the empire WAS dramatic.
```

#### ④ "Either/or", "neither/nor", "not only/but also" → the verb agrees with the NEAREST subject
```
✔ Neither the teacher nor the STUDENTS ARE present.     (students is nearest → plural)
✔ Neither the students nor the TEACHER IS present.      (teacher is nearest → singular)
```

#### ⑤ "As well as", "along with", "together with", "besides", "in addition to" → ignore them; use the FIRST subject
```
✔ The MANAGER, as well as his assistants, IS attending.
✘ The manager, as well as his assistants, are attending.
```
> 🔑 These phrases are NOT the same as "and". Cross them out mentally.

#### ⑥ Collective nouns (team, jury, committee, family, government, crowd) → usually SINGULAR
```
✔ The team IS playing well.
✔ The committee HAS decided.
```

#### ⑦ Subjects that LOOK plural but are SINGULAR
```
✔ News IS good.                    ✔ Mathematics IS difficult.
✔ Physics IS my favourite.         ✔ Politics IS complicated.
✔ Measles IS contagious.
```
And distances / amounts / periods of time treated as one unit:
```
✔ Twenty kilometres IS a long walk.
✔ Ten years IS a long time.
✔ Five hundred rupees IS not enough.
```

#### ⑧ "A number of" vs "The number of"
```
✔ A NUMBER OF students ARE absent.     ("a number of" = many → PLURAL)
✔ THE NUMBER OF students IS fifty.     ("the number" itself is one thing → SINGULAR)
```
> 🚨 This exact pair appears constantly. Learn both.

## 📖 PART 2 — TENSES ⭐⭐

### The 12 tenses at a glance

| | **Simple** | **Continuous** | **Perfect** | **Perfect Continuous** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | I write | I am writing | I have written | I have been writing |
| **Past** | I wrote | I was writing | I had written | I had been writing |
| **Future** | I will write | I will be writing | I will have written | I will have been writing |

### The 5 tense rules examiners test

#### ① "Since" and "for" → use PRESENT PERFECT (or perfect continuous), never simple present
```
✔ I HAVE BEEN living here SINCE 2010.
✘ I am living here since 2010.
```
- **since** + a point in time (since Monday, since 2010, since morning)
- **for** + a duration (for two years, for three hours)

#### ② Two past actions → the EARLIER one takes PAST PERFECT (had + verb)
```
✔ The train HAD LEFT before I REACHED the station.
   (leaving happened first → had left)
✘ The train left before I had reached the station.
```

#### ③ After "if" in a second conditional → use "were", and "would" in the other half
```
✔ If I WERE rich, I WOULD travel the world.
✘ If I was rich, I will travel the world.
```
> ⚠️ **Never put "will" in the "if" clause.** ✔ *If it **rains**, I **will** stay home.* ✘ *If it will rain...*

#### ④ Universal truths and habitual actions → SIMPLE PRESENT
```
✔ The sun RISES in the east.
✔ Water BOILS at 100°C.
```

#### ⑤ Time markers fix the tense
| Marker | Tense |
|---|---|
| yesterday, last week, ago, in 1990 | simple past |
| now, at present, currently, look! | present continuous |
| already, just, yet, ever, never, so far | present perfect |
| always, usually, every day, often | simple present |
| tomorrow, next week, soon | future |

## 📖 PART 3 — ARTICLES (a / an / the) ⭐

### A vs AN — it's about the SOUND, not the letter
```
Use AN before a VOWEL SOUND.  Use A before a CONSONANT SOUND.

✔ AN hour, AN honest man, AN heir        (silent H → vowel sound)
✔ A university, A useful book, A European   ("yoo" sound → consonant)
✔ AN MBA, AN MLA, AN hour                 ("em-bee-ay" starts with a vowel sound)
✔ A one-rupee note                        ("wun" → consonant sound)
```
> 🚨 **These 6 are the exam favourites: an hour, an honest, a university, a useful, an MBA, a one-rupee.** Memorize them as a block.

### THE — use it when:
| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Something already mentioned / specific | I saw **a** dog. **The** dog was barking. |
| Unique things | **the** sun, **the** moon, **the** earth |
| Superlatives | **the** best, **the** tallest |
| Rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges | **the** Ganga, **the** Himalayas |
| Musical instruments | play **the** guitar |
| Ordinals | **the** first, **the** second |

### Do NOT use "the" before:
```
✘ the India        → India (names of countries, mostly)
✘ the breakfast    → breakfast (meals)
✘ the football     → football (games and sports)
✘ the English      → English (languages)
✘ the Mount Everest → Mount Everest (single mountains)
```

## 📖 PART 4 — PREPOSITIONS ⭐ (learn the fixed pairs)

There's no logic here — these are just fixed combinations. **Memorize the list.**

| Word | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **afraid** | of | afraid **of** dogs |
| **angry** | with (a person) / at (a thing) | angry **with** him |
| **good / bad** | at | good **at** maths |
| **interested** | in | interested **in** music |
| **married** | to | married **to** her |
| **depend** | on | depends **on** you |
| **consist** | of | consists **of** three parts |
| **congratulate** | on | congratulate him **on** his success |
| **listen** | to | listen **to** me |
| **belong** | to | belongs **to** me |
| **prefer** | to | prefer tea **to** coffee |
| **superior / inferior** | to | superior **to** the others |
| **die** | of (disease) / from (injury) | died **of** cancer |
| **look forward** | to | look forward **to** meeting you |
| **capable** | of | capable **of** winning |
| **aware** | of | aware **of** the risk |
| **compare** | with (similar) / to (metaphor) | compared **with** last year |
| **suffer** | from | suffering **from** fever |
| **insist** | on | insisted **on** paying |
| **succeed** | in | succeeded **in** his attempt |

### 🚨 Common preposition traps
```
✘ discuss ABOUT the matter   →  ✔ DISCUSS the matter        (no preposition!)
✘ order FOR a book            →  ✔ ORDER a book
✘ reach AT the station        →  ✔ REACH the station
✘ resemble WITH his father    →  ✔ RESEMBLE his father
✘ marry WITH her              →  ✔ MARRY her  /  be married TO her
✘ superior THAN               →  ✔ superior TO
✘ prefer THAN                 →  ✔ prefer TO
```
> 🔑 **"Discuss", "reach", "resemble", "order", "marry", "enter" take NO preposition.** This is one of the most frequently tested error-spotting items.

## 📖 PART 5 — ACTIVE & PASSIVE VOICE

```
ACTIVE:   Subject + Verb + Object          Ram wrote a letter.
PASSIVE:  Object + be + V₃ + by + Subject  A letter was written by Ram.
```

### The conversion recipe
```
STEP 1: Move the OBJECT to the front.
STEP 2: Use the correct form of "BE" (matching the original tense).
STEP 3: Use the THIRD form of the verb (past participle).
STEP 4: Add "by + original subject".
```

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple present | He writes a letter | A letter **is written** by him |
| Present continuous | He is writing a letter | A letter **is being written** by him |
| Simple past | He wrote a letter | A letter **was written** by him |
| Past continuous | He was writing a letter | A letter **was being written** by him |
| Present perfect | He has written a letter | A letter **has been written** by him |
| Past perfect | He had written a letter | A letter **had been written** by him |
| Future | He will write a letter | A letter **will be written** by him |
| Modal | He can write a letter | A letter **can be written** by him |

### 🔍 Worked example
*Convert to passive: "The teacher is teaching the lesson."*
```
Step 1: Object "the lesson" moves to the front.
Step 2: Tense is present continuous → "is being"
Step 3: Third form of teach = taught
Step 4: Add "by the teacher"
✅ The lesson IS BEING TAUGHT by the teacher.
```

## 📖 PART 6 — DIRECT & INDIRECT SPEECH

### The 4 changes to make
```
1. Remove the quotation marks; add "that" (for statements)
2. Shift the TENSE one step back        (is → was, will → would, have → had)
3. Change the PRONOUNS to match the reporting speaker
4. Change the TIME and PLACE words
```

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tomorrow | the next day |
| yesterday | the previous day |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| ago | before |

### The reporting verb changes by sentence type
| Type | Reporting verb |
|---|---|
| Statement | said that |
| Question | asked **if / whether** (yes-no) or asked + question word |
| Command | ordered / told + **to** + verb |
| Request | requested + **to** + verb |
| Advice | advised + **to** + verb |

### 🔍 Worked examples
```
Direct:   He said, "I am busy."
Indirect: He said that HE WAS busy.                  (I→he, am→was)

Direct:   She said, "I will come tomorrow."
Indirect: She said that SHE WOULD come THE NEXT DAY.

Direct:   He said to me, "Are you ready?"
Indirect: He asked me IF I WAS ready.                (question → "if", no question mark)

Direct:   The teacher said, "Open your books."
Indirect: The teacher ORDERED us TO OPEN our books.   (command → ordered + to)
```
> ⚠️ **Exception:** if the quote is a universal truth, do NOT shift the tense.
> *He said, "The sun rises in the east."* → *He said that the sun **rises** in the east.*

## 📖 PART 7 — COMMON ERROR-SPOTTING ITEMS ⭐⭐

These exact errors appear again and again. **Learn the right-hand column.**

| ✘ Wrong | ✔ Right |
|---|---|
| One of my friend | One of my **friends** (always plural after "one of") |
| He is my cousin brother | He is my **cousin** |
| Return back | **Return** |
| Repeat again | **Repeat** |
| Discuss about | **Discuss** |
| Two twins | **Twins** |
| More better | **Better** |
| Most cheapest | **Cheapest** |
| I am having a car | I **have** a car (possession is never continuous) |
| He is knowing the answer | He **knows** the answer |
| Each of the boys were | Each of the boys **was** |
| The scenery**s** are beautiful | The **scenery is** beautiful (uncountable) |
| Give me some informations | Some **information** (uncountable) |
| My all books | **All my** books |
| Very much beautiful | **Very** beautiful |
| Comprises of | **Comprises** |
| Since three years | **For** three years |
| Lesser than | **Less** than |
| Between you and I | Between you and **me** |
| Neither of them are | Neither of them **is** |

### 🚨 Uncountable nouns — never add "s", never use "a"
```
information, advice, furniture, luggage, baggage, scenery, machinery,
equipment, news, money, work, bread, water, poetry, traffic, evidence
```
✘ *informations, advices, furnitures, luggages*

## ⚡ SHORTCUTS & SPEED TRICKS

1. **For subject-verb agreement, find the REAL subject.** Mentally delete every "of ___", "as well as ___", "along with ___" phrase. Then the verb is obvious.

2. **Read the sentence aloud in your head.** Your ear catches most errors faster than your rulebook. Trust it — then confirm with a rule.

3. **In error-spotting, check these four first:**
   - subject-verb agreement
   - tense consistency
   - preposition
   - singular/plural noun

4. **"One of the ___" is ALWAYS followed by a plural noun** and a singular verb. *One of the **boys** **is** absent.*

5. **"Since" needs a perfect tense. "For" needs a duration.**

6. **Never "will" after "if"** in a conditional.

7. **Don't over-correct.** Many sentences in error-spotting questions have **no error** — that's usually an option, and it's the right answer more often than students expect.

8. **In fill-in-the-blanks, read the WHOLE sentence first.** The clue is often after the blank, not before.

## 🎯 QUICK RECAP — say these out loud

- **Each / every / either / neither / everyone → SINGULAR**
- Delete the **"of ___"** phrase to find the real subject
- **Neither/nor, either/or** → verb agrees with the **NEAREST** subject
- **"As well as", "along with"** → ignore; use the **first** subject
- **"A number of" = plural. "The number of" = singular.**
- **"Since" + point in time, "for" + duration**, both with perfect tense
- Earlier of two past actions → **past perfect (had + V₃)**
- **Never "will" after "if"**
- **An hour, a university, an MBA, a one-rupee note**
- **Discuss / reach / resemble / order / marry / enter take NO preposition**
- **"One of the ___" → plural noun, singular verb**
- Uncountable: **information, advice, furniture, luggage, scenery, news** — no "s"
- **"No error" is sometimes the right answer.** Don't over-correct.
