# 1️⃣5️⃣ BLOOD RELATIONS 🟢

⏱️ **Study time: 20 minutes** | 🎯 **Typical questions: 2–3**

> 🟢 **THE BEST RETURN ON TIME IN THE ENTIRE SYLLABUS.** 20 minutes of study and you'll get essentially every question right. There are no formulas — just a diagram method and one habit: **read the sentence BACKWARDS.**

---

## 📖 PART 1 — THE FAMILY TREE METHOD ⭐⭐

**Never solve these in your head. Draw a tree.** Use these symbols consistently:

```
SYMBOLS:
    +    male
    –    female
    =    married couple  (draw them side by side)
    │    parent → child  (vertical line going DOWN)
    ──   siblings        (horizontal line, same level)


LAYOUT — put each GENERATION on its own row:

    Generation 1  (grandparents)      A(+) ═══ B(–)
                                            │
    Generation 2  (parents)          C(+) ─── D(–)
                                       │
    Generation 3  (children)         E(+)
```

> 🔑 **The single most important rule: keep generations on separate horizontal levels.** Siblings go side by side; children go one row below. If your diagram respects levels, you cannot get the answer wrong.

---

## 📖 PART 2 — THE RELATIONSHIP DICTIONARY

| Relationship | In plain terms |
|---|---|
| Father's/Mother's father | **Grandfather** |
| Father's/Mother's mother | **Grandmother** |
| Son's/Daughter's son | **Grandson** |
| Son's/Daughter's daughter | **Granddaughter** |
| Father's brother | **Uncle** (paternal) |
| Father's sister | **Aunt** (paternal) |
| Mother's brother | **Uncle** (maternal) |
| Mother's sister | **Aunt** (maternal) |
| Uncle's/Aunt's son or daughter | **Cousin** |
| Brother's son | **Nephew** |
| Brother's daughter | **Niece** |
| Sister's son | **Nephew** |
| Sister's daughter | **Niece** |
| Husband's/Wife's father | **Father-in-law** |
| Husband's/Wife's mother | **Mother-in-law** |
| Husband's/Wife's brother | **Brother-in-law** |
| Husband's/Wife's sister | **Sister-in-law** |
| Sister's husband | **Brother-in-law** |
| Brother's wife | **Sister-in-law** |
| Son's wife | **Daughter-in-law** |
| Daughter's husband | **Son-in-law** |

### 🚨 Two things examiners exploit
1. **"Cousin" has no gender.** A cousin can be male or female — so if a question asks "how is X related to Y" and the answer is cousin, don't assume "cousin brother."
2. **Paternal vs maternal.** *Father's* side = paternal. *Mother's* side = maternal. Some questions specifically test this distinction.

## 📖 PART 3 — THE GOLDEN RULE: READ BACKWARDS ⭐⭐⭐

For long chained statements like *"the daughter of the only son of my grandmother"*, **start from the END and work towards the front.**

### 🔍 Example 1 — The classic photograph question
*Pointing to a photograph, a man said, "She is the daughter of the only son of my grandmother." How is the woman related to him?*

```
Step 1: Break the phrase into pieces and read from the BACK.

        "...of my grandmother"          ← START HERE
        "the only son of my grandmother"
        "the daughter of [that son]"

Step 2: Work forwards through your pieces.

        My grandmother's only son
        → My grandmother has one son. Who is her son?
        → My own FATHER.  (If she had other sons they'd be my uncles,
                           but she has only ONE son, so it must be my father.)

Step 3: Now: "the daughter of my father"
        → My father's daughter = MY SISTER.

✅ She is his SISTER.

DIAGRAM CHECK:
        Grandmother(–)
             │
          Father(+)          ← "the only son"
             │
        ┌────┴────┐
       Man(+)   She(–)       ← same level = siblings ✔
```

### 🔍 Example 2 — The "only daughter" trap ⭐
*Introducing a man, a woman said, "His wife is the only daughter of my father." How is the man related to the woman?*

```
Step 1: Read from the back.
        "the only daughter of my father"
        → The woman's father has exactly ONE daughter.
        → Who is that one daughter? It must be THE WOMAN HERSELF.

Step 2: So the statement becomes: "His wife is ME."

Step 3: If his wife is her, then he is her HUSBAND.

✅ He is her HUSBAND

⚠️ THIS IS THE #1 TRAP IN THIS TOPIC. Many people answer "brother-in-law".
   Whenever you see "the only son/daughter of my father/mother",
   ALWAYS check whether it refers to the SPEAKER themselves.
```

### 🔍 Example 3 — Cousin
*Pointing to a photo, Ravi said, "He is the son of my mother's only brother." How is he related to Ravi?*
```
Step 1: My mother's only brother → Ravi's maternal UNCLE.
Step 2: The son of Ravi's uncle → Ravi's COUSIN.
✅ Cousin

DIAGRAM:
    Grandparents
         │
    ┌────┴────┐
  Mother(–)  Uncle(+)
      │          │
    Ravi(+)    He(+)     ← same level = cousins ✔
```

## 📖 PART 4 — MULTI-PERSON PUZZLES ⭐

### 🔍 Example 4
*P is Q's brother. R is Q's father. S is R's sister. T is S's mother. How is Q related to T?*

```
Step 1: Take the statements ONE AT A TIME and build the tree upwards.

  "R is Q's father"       →  R is one generation ABOVE Q
  "P is Q's brother"      →  P is on the SAME level as Q
  "S is R's sister"       →  S is on the SAME level as R
  "T is S's mother"       →  T is one generation ABOVE S (and therefore above R too)

Step 2: Draw it with levels.

    Level 1:            T(–)
                          │
    Level 2:      ┌───────┴───────┐
                 R(+)           S(–)
                  │
    Level 3:  ┌───┴───┐
             Q      P(+)

Step 3: Read off the answer. T is two levels above Q.
        T is R's mother, and R is Q's father.
        So T is Q's GRANDMOTHER  →  therefore Q is T's GRANDCHILD.

✅ Q is T's grandchild (grandson or granddaughter — Q's gender isn't given)
```
> 💡 **Notice we could NOT say "grandson"** — the puzzle never tells us Q's gender. (P is called Q's *brother*, which tells us about P, not Q.) If an option says "grandson", check whether the gender is actually established. Exams love this.

### 🔍 Example 5
*A is B's father. B is C's mother. How is A related to C?*
```
Step 1:  A(+)
           │
         B(–)
           │
         C

Step 2: A is two generations above C, and A is male.
✅ A is C's GRANDFATHER
```

## 📖 PART 5 — CODED BLOOD RELATIONS ⭐

Here symbols replace relationship words. **Method: decode the symbols into a diagram, then read it.**

### Typical code set
```
A + B   means   A is the FATHER of B
A – B   means   A is the WIFE of B
A × B   means   A is the BROTHER of B
A ÷ B   means   A is the MOTHER of B
```

### 🔍 Example 6
*In the expression `P × Q + R`, how is P related to R?*
```
Step 1: Decode each symbol, left to right.
        P × Q  →  P is the BROTHER of Q
        Q + R  →  Q is the FATHER of R

Step 2: Draw it.
              P(+) ─── Q(+)
                        │
                       R

Step 3: P is the brother of R's father.
        Your father's brother is your UNCLE.

✅ P is R's UNCLE
```
> 🔑 **Coded questions are easy once you convert to a diagram.** Don't try to reason in symbols. Decode → draw → read.

## ⚡ SHORTCUTS & SPEED TRICKS

1. **ALWAYS DRAW THE TREE.** With generations on separate levels. This is non-negotiable.

2. **READ CHAINED PHRASES BACKWARDS.** Start from the last "of my ___" and work forwards.

3. **"Only son/daughter of my father/mother" is usually the SPEAKER or the speaker's sibling.** Check this first — it's the most common trap.

4. **Mark gender as you go (+ / –).** Half the questions hinge on gender, and half the wrong answers come from assuming it.

5. **Watch for un-stated gender.** If the puzzle never says whether Q is male or female, the answer is "child/grandchild", not "son/grandson".

6. **Count generations, not names.** Two levels up = grandparent. One level up, sideways = uncle/aunt. Same level, one level up sideways = cousin.

7. **"How is X related to Y" — get the DIRECTION right.** "How is A related to C" asks for A's role (grandfather). "How is C related to A" asks for C's role (grandchild). These are different answers to the same diagram. **Read which way round it's asked.**

8. **In-law = through marriage.** If the connection passes through a spouse, the answer almost certainly ends in "-in-law".

## ✍️ PRACTICE (do these — 10 min)

1. Pointing to a photograph, a man said, "She is the daughter of the only son of my grandmother." How is she related to him?
2. A is B's father and B is C's mother. How is A related to C?
3. Introducing a man, a woman said, "His wife is the only daughter of my father." How is the man related to the woman?
4. P is Q's brother, R is Q's father, S is R's sister, T is S's mother. How is Q related to T?
5. X is Y's sister and Y is Z's mother. How is X related to Z?
6. A is the son of B, and C is the mother of B. How is C related to A?
7. Pointing to a photo, Ravi said, "He is the son of my mother's only brother." How is he related to Ravi?
8. M is the daughter of N, and N is the son of O. How is O related to M?
9. P's father is Q's only son. How is P related to Q?
10. In the code `A + B` (A is the father of B) and `B ÷ C` (B is the mother of C), how is A related to C?
11. In the expression `P × Q + R` where × means "is the brother of" and + means "is the father of", how is P related to R?
12. A woman said, "The boy in the photo is my mother's daughter's son." How is the boy related to her?

---

### ✅ ANSWERS

<details>
<summary>Click to reveal</summary>

1. **Sister** — grandmother's only son = his father; father's daughter = his sister
2. **Grandfather** — A is male and two generations above C
3. **Husband** — the only daughter of her father is the woman herself, so her husband
4. **Grandchild** — T is R's mother, R is Q's father, so T is Q's grandmother. (Not "grandson" — Q's gender is never given.)
5. **Aunt** — X is the sister of Z's mother, i.e. Z's maternal aunt
6. **Grandmother** — C is B's mother and B is A's parent
7. **Cousin** — the son of Ravi's maternal uncle
8. **Grandfather** — N is M's parent, and O is N's parent, so O is two generations above M. (Strictly, "N is the son of O" fixes N as male but leaves O's gender open, so "grandparent" is the airtight answer — exams normally key this one as **grandfather**.)
9. **Grandchild** — P's father is Q's son, so Q is P's grandfather and P is Q's grandchild
10. **Grandfather** — A is B's father; B is C's mother; so A is C's maternal grandfather
11. **Uncle** — P is the brother of R's father
12. **Nephew** — her mother's daughter is her sister (or herself); taken as her sister, the sister's son is her nephew

## 🎯 QUICK RECAP — say these out loud

- **DRAW A TREE with generations on separate levels.** Always.
- Mark gender with **+ (male)** and **– (female)** as you read
- **READ CHAINED PHRASES BACKWARDS**, from the last "of my ___"
- **"Only son/daughter of my father"** → check if it means the SPEAKER
- **Two levels up** = grandparent. **Same level** = sibling/cousin. **One up, sideways** = uncle/aunt.
- If gender isn't stated, answer **child/grandchild/cousin**, not son/grandson
- Check the **direction** of "how is X related to Y"
- Connection through a spouse → **"-in-law"**
- Coded questions: **decode → draw → read.** Never reason in symbols.
