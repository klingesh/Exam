# 2️⃣ PERCENTAGE ⭐⭐⭐

⏱️ **Study time: 45 minutes** | 🎯 **Typical questions: 4–6 direct, plus it powers Profit & Loss, Interest, and DI**

> 🔥 **If you only study one topic today, study this one.** Percentage is hidden inside Profit & Loss, Simple & Compound Interest, Data Interpretation, and Averages. Getting this right lifts your score in 5 topics at once.

---

## 📖 PART 1 — THE CORE IDEA

**Percent = "per hundred".** 25% just means 25 out of 100, i.e. 25/100.

```
To convert % → number:   divide by 100      45%  = 45/100  = 0.45
To convert number → %:   multiply by 100    0.6  = 0.6×100 = 60%
```

### The 3 questions that cover 90% of basic percentage

| Question form | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **"x% of N"** | N × x/100 | 25% of 480 = 480 × 25/100 = **120** |
| **"A is what % of B"** | (A/B) × 100 | 45 is what % of 250? → 45/250 × 100 = **18%** |
| **"x% of N is A, find N"** | A × 100/x | 40% of a number is 160 → 160 × 100/40 = **400** |

> 💡 **Read the word "of" as ×, and "is" as =.** "30% of what number is 60" becomes `30/100 × ? = 60`. This one habit prevents most percentage errors.

## 📖 PART 2 — THE FRACTION TABLE ⭐ (memorize — this is pure speed)

Converting % into a fraction turns ugly multiplication into simple division.

| Fraction | Percentage | | Fraction | Percentage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 50% | | 1/9 | 11.11% |
| 1/3 | 33.33% | | 1/10 | 10% |
| 1/4 | 25% | | 1/11 | 9.09% |
| 1/5 | 20% | | 1/12 | 8.33% |
| 1/6 | 16.67% | | 1/16 | 6.25% |
| 1/7 | 14.28% | | 1/20 | 5% |
| 1/8 | 12.5% | | 1/25 | 4% |

**Also worth knowing:** 2/3 = 66.67%, 3/4 = 75%, 2/5 = 40%, 3/5 = 60%, 5/8 = 62.5%, 1/40 = 2.5%

### 🔍 Why this saves you time
```
Find 12.5% of 656.

SLOW way:  656 × 12.5 / 100 = 8200/100 = 82
FAST way:  12.5% = 1/8  →  656 ÷ 8 = 82        ← 3 seconds, no pen needed
```

```
Find 16.67% of 342.
16.67% = 1/6  →  342 ÷ 6 = 57
```

## 📖 PART 3 — PERCENTAGE CHANGE

```
                   Change            New – Old
% Change  =  ─────────────── × 100 = ───────── × 100
                Original value          Old
```

⚠️ **Always divide by the ORIGINAL (old) value.** This is the most common mistake in the whole topic.

### 🔍 Worked example — A price rises from ₹80 to ₹100. What is the % increase?
```
Step 1: Change = 100 – 80 = 20
Step 2: Divide by the ORIGINAL, which is 80 (not 100)
        20 / 80 = 0.25
Step 3: × 100 → 25%
✅ 25% increase
```

### 🔍 Now reverse it — price falls from ₹100 to ₹80. What is the % decrease?
```
Step 1: Change = 20
Step 2: Original is now 100
        20 / 100 = 0.20
✅ 20% decrease
```

> 🚨 **Notice: 80 → 100 is +25%, but 100 → 80 is –20%.** Same ₹20 change, different percentages, because the base changed. Examiners test this constantly.

### The multiplier method (use this, it's faster)
```
Increase by 15%  →  multiply by 1.15
Decrease by 15%  →  multiply by 0.85
```
*60 increased by 15% → 60 × 1.15 = **69*** (no need for two steps)

## 📖 PART 4 — SUCCESSIVE PERCENTAGE CHANGE ⭐⭐

When two changes happen one after another, **you cannot just add them.**

```
                        a × b
Net % change = a + b + ───────
                         100

Use + for increase, – for decrease.
```

### 🔍 Worked example — A salary is increased by 20%, then by 30%. Net increase?
```
Step 1: a = +20, b = +30
Step 2: Net = 20 + 30 + (20 × 30)/100
Step 3:     = 50 + 600/100
Step 4:     = 50 + 6 = 56%

✅ Net increase = 56%, NOT 50%.

Verify: ₹100 → +20% → ₹120 → +30% → ₹156.  Yes, 56% ✔
```

### 🔍 Worked example — Two successive discounts of 20% and 10%. Single equivalent discount?
```
Step 1: Both are decreases → a = –20, b = –10
Step 2: Net = –20 – 10 + (–20 × –10)/100
Step 3:     = –30 + 200/100
Step 4:     = –30 + 2 = –28%

✅ Equivalent single discount = 28%, NOT 30%.

Verify: ₹100 → –20% → ₹80 → –10% → ₹72.  A drop of 28 ✔
```

### 🔍 The classic trap — increase by 10%, then decrease by 10%
```
Net = +10 – 10 + (10 × –10)/100 = 0 – 1 = –1%

✅ You end up 1% WORSE off, not back where you started.

Verify: ₹100 → +10% → ₹110 → –10% → ₹99 ✔
```

> ⚡ **Shortcut to remember:** an increase of x% followed by a decrease of x% **always** gives a net loss of **x²/100 %**.
> Up 20% then down 20% → loss of 400/100 = **4%**.

## 📖 PART 5 — THE "MORE THAN / LESS THAN" REVERSAL ⭐⭐

This pattern appears in almost every placement paper.

```
If A is x% MORE than B,  then B is LESS than A by:      x/(100 + x) × 100 %

If A is x% LESS than B,  then B is MORE than A by:      x/(100 – x) × 100 %
```

### 🔍 Worked example — A's salary is 25% more than B's. By what % is B's salary less than A's?
```
Step 1: x = 25, and it's "more", so use 100 + x
Step 2: 25 / (100 + 25) × 100
Step 3: = 25/125 × 100
Step 4: = 0.2 × 100 = 20%

✅ B's salary is 20% less than A's.

Verify with numbers: B = 100, A = 125.
Difference = 25. As a % of A → 25/125 × 100 = 20% ✔
```

> 💡 **If you forget the formula, just plug in B = 100.** It works every time and takes 15 seconds. Honestly, in the exam, do this instead of memorizing.

### 🔍 The consumption / expenditure version
*The price of sugar rises by 25%. By what % must a family reduce consumption so the expenditure stays the same?*
```
Step 1: Expenditure = Price × Quantity. Price went up by a factor of 1.25.
Step 2: So quantity must fall to 1/1.25 = 0.8 of what it was.
Step 3: Reduction = 1 – 0.8 = 0.2 → 20%

Or straight from the formula: 25/(100+25) × 100 = 20%
✅ Reduce consumption by 20%
```

**Handy pre-solved values for this pattern:**

| Price rises by | Reduce consumption by |
|---|---|
| 10% | 9.09% (1/11) |
| 20% | 16.67% (1/6) |
| 25% | 20% (1/5) |
| 50% | 33.33% (1/3) |
| 100% | 50% (1/2) |

## 📖 PART 6 — EXAM MARKS / PASSING MARKS PROBLEMS ⭐

A very common, very scorable pattern. **Always set up "pass mark" twice and equate.**

### 🔍 Worked example
*A student scores 30% and fails by 20 marks. Another scores 40% and gets 15 marks more than the pass mark. Find the total marks and the pass mark.*

```
Step 1: Let total marks = T. Write the PASS MARK from each student.

        Student 1 scored 0.30T and was 20 SHORT of passing.
        → Pass mark = 0.30T + 20

        Student 2 scored 0.40T and was 15 ABOVE passing.
        → Pass mark = 0.40T – 15

Step 2: Both expressions equal the same pass mark, so equate them.
        0.30T + 20 = 0.40T – 15

Step 3: Collect terms.
        20 + 15 = 0.40T – 0.30T
        35 = 0.10T

Step 4: T = 35 / 0.10 = 350

Step 5: Pass mark = 0.30(350) + 20 = 105 + 20 = 125

✅ Total = 350 marks, Pass mark = 125 (which is 35.7% — sensible ✔)
```

> 🧠 **The rule:** "fails by" → **ADD** to their score. "scores more than needed by" → **SUBTRACT** from their score. Get this direction right and the problem solves itself.

## 📖 PART 7 — CHAIN PERCENTAGE ("A is x% of B, B is y% of C")

Just multiply the decimals.

### 🔍 Worked example — A is 40% of B, and B is 25% of C. A is what % of C?
```
Step 1: A = 0.40 × B
Step 2: B = 0.25 × C
Step 3: Substitute → A = 0.40 × (0.25 × C) = 0.10 × C
Step 4: 0.10 → 10%
✅ A is 10% of C
```

## 📖 PART 8 — POPULATION / GROWTH

```
Value after n years of r% growth  =  P (1 + r/100)ⁿ
Value after n years of r% decline =  P (1 – r/100)ⁿ
```
*(Yes — this is the same formula as Compound Interest. One formula, two topics.)*

### 🔍 Worked example — Population is 10,000. It grows 10% in year 1 and 20% in year 2. Find the population.
```
Step 1: After year 1 → 10,000 × 1.10 = 11,000
Step 2: After year 2 → 11,000 × 1.20 = 13,200
✅ 13,200
```

## ⚡ SHORTCUTS & SPEED TRICKS

1. **x% of y = y% of x.** So 40% of 25 = 25% of 40 = **10**. Use whichever is easier.

2. **Break awkward percentages into friendly pieces.**
   ```
   35% of 240 = 25% of 240  +  10% of 240
              = 60          +  24         = 84
   ```

3. **10% is your anchor.** 10% of anything = move the decimal one place left. Then build:
   - 5% = half of 10%
   - 20% = double 10%
   - 15% = 10% + 5%
   - 1% = move decimal two places

4. **Successive changes → use multipliers, not the formula.** Up 20%, down 10%, up 50%?
   `1.20 × 0.90 × 1.50 = 1.62` → **62% increase.** Much safer than applying the a+b+ab/100 formula twice.

5. **The x²/100 rule:** up x% then down x% → always a loss of x²/100 %.

6. **For "what % is A of B" questions,** if the answer looks ugly, check the fraction table — the answer is usually a familiar fraction.

## 🎯 QUICK RECAP — say these out loud

- % change is **always over the ORIGINAL** value
- Successive change: **a + b + ab/100** (signs matter) — or just multiply the multipliers
- Up x% then down x% → **loss of x²/100 %**, never zero
- A is x% more than B → B is less by **x/(100+x) × 100**
- A is x% less than B → B is more by **x/(100–x) × 100**
- Price up x% → cut consumption by **x/(100+x) × 100**
- "Fails by" → **ADD** to the score to get the pass mark
- **x% of y = y% of x**
- Learn the fraction table. 12.5% = 1/8 saves you 30 seconds every time it appears.
