# 6️⃣ PROBLEMS ON AGES

⏱️ **Study time: 30 minutes** | 🎯 **Typical questions: 1–3**

> ✅ This topic has **no formulas at all**. It's pure equation-setting. Learn the 3-step method below and you can solve every single one of them.

---

## 📖 PART 1 — THE UNIVERSAL 3-STEP METHOD

```
STEP 1:  Let the PRESENT age be x  (always start from the present)
STEP 2:  Translate every sentence into an equation
STEP 3:  Solve
```

### The translation table — learn this and you're done

| The question says… | You write… |
|---|---|
| present age | **x** |
| **after** / **hence** / **later** n years | **x + n** |
| **ago** / **before** n years | **x – n** |
| A is n years **older** than B | A = B + n |
| A is n years **younger** than B | A = B – n |
| A is **k times** as old as B | A = k × B |
| ages are in the **ratio** a : b | ages are **ax** and **bx** |
| **sum** of ages | A + B = … |

### 🚨 The #1 mistake in this topic
When the question says *"after 10 years, the father will be twice as old as the son"*, you must add 10 to **BOTH** people:
```
WRONG:   F + 10 = 2S
RIGHT:   F + 10 = 2(S + 10)
```
**Everybody ages together.** Circle the "+10" on both sides before you solve. This one habit will save you 2 marks.

## 📖 PART 2 — WORKED EXAMPLES, STEP BY STEP

### 🔍 Example 1 — The classic "times" problem
*The present age of a father is 3 times that of his son. After 12 years, the father's age will be twice the son's age. Find their present ages.*

```
Step 1: Name the present ages.
        Let the son's present age = S
        Then the father's present age = 3S          (given: 3 times)

Step 2: Translate the second sentence — add 12 to BOTH.
        Father after 12 years  = 3S + 12
        Son after 12 years     = S + 12
        The relation is: father = twice the son

        3S + 12 = 2(S + 12)

Step 3: Solve.
        3S + 12 = 2S + 24
        3S – 2S = 24 – 12
        S = 12

Step 4: Father = 3S = 36

✅ Son = 12 years, Father = 36 years

VERIFY (always do this — takes 5 seconds):
   After 12 years → Son = 24, Father = 48. Is 48 twice 24? YES ✔
```

### 🔍 Example 2 — Ratio type ⭐
*The ages of A and B are in the ratio 5 : 7. Four years from now, the ratio will be 3 : 4. Find their present ages.*

```
Step 1: Ratios → use a common multiplier x, NOT two separate variables.
        A = 5x,  B = 7x

Step 2: After 4 years → A = 5x + 4, B = 7x + 4
        New ratio is 3 : 4, so:

              5x + 4       3
             ─────────  =  ───
              7x + 4       4

Step 3: Cross-multiply.
        4(5x + 4) = 3(7x + 4)
        20x + 16  = 21x + 12

Step 4: Solve.
        16 – 12 = 21x – 20x
        x = 4

Step 5: A = 5x = 20,  B = 7x = 28

✅ A = 20 years, B = 28 years

VERIFY: After 4 years → 24 and 32. Is 24:32 = 3:4? Divide both by 8 → 3:4 YES ✔
```
> 🔑 **This is the key ratio insight:** two numbers in the ratio 5:7 are **5x and 7x**, not 5 and 7. Using one variable instead of two makes the algebra trivial.

### 🔍 Example 3 — Sum + past condition
*The sum of the ages of a father and son is 60 years. Six years ago, the father's age was 5 times the son's age. Find the son's age 6 years from now.*

```
Step 1: Let son = S, father = F.
        Given: F + S = 60          … (i)

Step 2: Six years AGO → subtract 6 from both.
        F – 6 = 5(S – 6)           … (ii)

Step 3: Expand (ii).
        F – 6 = 5S – 30
        F = 5S – 24

Step 4: Substitute into (i).
        (5S – 24) + S = 60
        6S = 84
        S = 14      →  F = 46

Step 5: The question asks for the son's age 6 years FROM NOW.
        ⚠️ Don't stop at S = 14. Read the question again!
        Son after 6 years = 14 + 6 = 20

✅ 20 years

VERIFY: 6 years ago → Son 8, Father 40. Is 40 = 5 × 8? YES ✔  Sum today = 60 ✔
```
> 🚨 **Read the final question carefully.** A huge number of marks are lost by solving correctly and then giving the present age when the question asked for the future age.

### 🔍 Example 4 — Two conditions, past and future
*Ten years ago, a mother was 3 times as old as her daughter. Ten years from now, she will be twice as old. Find their present ages.*

```
Step 1: Let mother = M, daughter = D (present ages).

Step 2: Ten years AGO:    M – 10 = 3(D – 10)      … (i)
        Ten years HENCE:  M + 10 = 2(D + 10)      … (ii)

Step 3: Simplify (i).
        M – 10 = 3D – 30
        M = 3D – 20

Step 4: Substitute into (ii).
        (3D – 20) + 10 = 2D + 20
        3D – 10 = 2D + 20
        3D – 2D = 20 + 10
        D = 30

Step 5: M = 3(30) – 20 = 70

✅ Daughter = 30 years, Mother = 70 years

VERIFY: 10 years ago → 20 and 60. Is 60 = 3 × 20? YES ✔
        10 years hence → 40 and 80. Is 80 = 2 × 40? YES ✔
```

### 🔍 Example 5 — Three people
*A is 2 years older than B. B is twice as old as C. The total of their ages is 27. Find B's age.*

```
Step 1: Express EVERYTHING in terms of ONE person.
        B is the middle link, so use B.

        A = B + 2
        B = 2C  →  so  C = B/2

Step 2: Write the sum.
        A + B + C = 27
        (B + 2) + B + B/2 = 27

Step 3: Combine. B + B + 0.5B = 2.5B
        2.5B + 2 = 27
        2.5B = 25
        B = 10

✅ B = 10 years   (A = 12, C = 5)

VERIFY: 12 + 10 + 5 = 27 ✔  B is twice C ✔  A is 2 more than B ✔
```
> 🧠 **With three people, always express all of them in terms of the one in the middle of the chain.** Here C was defined via B, and A was defined via B — so B is the natural variable.

## ⚡ SHORTCUTS & SPEED TRICKS

1. **USE THE OPTIONS.** This is an MCQ. Ages problems are the *easiest* topic to back-solve. Take each option, plug it into the conditions, see which one fits. Often 3× faster than algebra.
   > *Father is 3× son; after 12 years father is 2× son. Options for son: (a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 14 (d) 16*
   > Try 12: father = 36. After 12 → 24 and 48. 48 = 2 × 24 ✔ **Answer is (b).** Solved in 10 seconds.

2. **The age DIFFERENCE never changes.** If a father is 30 years older than his son today, he will still be 30 years older in 20 years. This is a powerful shortcut:
   > *A father is 30 years older than his son. In 12 years he will be 3 times as old. Find the son's age.*
   > In 12 years: F = 3S, and F – S = 30 → 3S – S = 30 → 2S = 30 → S = 15 **at that time**.
   > So today the son is 15 – 12 = **3 years old.** (Check: father is 33 today; in 12 years 45 and 15 → 3× ✔)

3. **Ratios → one variable (ax, bx), never two.**

4. **Always add/subtract the years to EVERY person** in the equation.

5. **Verify by plugging back.** Ages problems are self-checking — it takes 5 seconds and catches nearly every error.

6. **Sanity-check the answer.** Nobody's age is negative or 300. If you get a fraction for a "how many years old" question, you probably mis-set an equation.

7. **"n years ago the ratio was a:b" plus "now the ratio is c:d"** → set up ages as present values and subtract n. Don't set up the past as ax, bx unless the past ratio is the only ratio given.

## 🎯 QUICK RECAP — say these out loud

- Always let the **PRESENT** age be the variable
- "After n years" → **add n to EVERYONE**. "n years ago" → **subtract n from EVERYONE**
- Ratio a : b → write **ax and bx** (one variable, not two)
- The **age difference never changes** — use it as a shortcut
- **Back-solve from the options** — fastest method in this topic
- **Verify** by plugging your answer back into both conditions
- **Re-read what's actually asked** — present age? future age? the sum?
