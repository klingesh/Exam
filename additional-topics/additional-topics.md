## 1. BANKING

### What is a Bank?
A **bank** is a financial institution that **accepts deposits** from the public and **lends money**, while providing payment and other financial services. It acts as an intermediary between savers and borrowers.

### Functions of a Commercial Bank
**Primary functions:**
- **Accepting deposits:** Savings, Current, Fixed (Term), Recurring deposits.
- **Lending/Advances:** loans, cash credit, overdraft, discounting bills.

**Secondary functions:**
- **Agency services:** collecting cheques, paying bills, fund transfers.
- **General utility services:** lockers, demand drafts, foreign exchange, letters of credit.
- **Credit creation:** banks create money by lending out a multiple of their deposits.

### Types of Banks
- **Central Bank** (RBI) — regulator of the banking system.
- **Commercial Banks** — Public sector (SBI, PNB), Private (HDFC, ICICI), Foreign.
- **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)** — rural credit.
- **Cooperative Banks** — member-owned, local.
- **Small Finance & Payments Banks** — financial inclusion.
- **Development Banks** — NABARD, SIDBI (long-term/sector finance).

### Key Banking Concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)** | % of deposits banks must keep as cash with RBI. |
| **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)** | % of deposits banks keep in liquid assets (gold, govt securities). |
| **Repo Rate** | Rate at which RBI lends to banks. |
| **Reverse Repo Rate** | Rate at which RBI borrows from banks. |
| **NPA (Non-Performing Asset)** | A loan on which interest/principal is overdue (usually 90+ days). |
| **NEFT/RTGS/IMPS/UPI** | Electronic fund transfer systems. |
| **Basel Norms** | International capital-adequacy standards for banks. |
| **KYC** | "Know Your Customer" — customer verification. |

## 2. INSURANCE & RISK MANAGEMENT

### What is Insurance?
**Insurance** is a contract (**policy**) where the **insurer** promises to compensate the **insured** for a specified loss, in exchange for a **premium**. It's a tool to **transfer risk** from an individual to a pool.

### Key Principles of Insurance
1. **Utmost Good Faith** — both parties disclose all material facts honestly.
2. **Insurable Interest** — insured must benefit from the subject's existence / suffer from its loss.
3. **Indemnity** — compensation only for actual loss (no profit from insurance); applies to general insurance, not life.
4. **Subrogation** — after paying a claim, insurer gets the insured's right to recover from a third party.
5. **Contribution** — if insured with multiple insurers, they share the loss.
6. **Proximate Cause** — the nearest/main cause of loss determines liability.

### Types of Insurance
- **Life Insurance** — covers life (term plans, endowment, ULIPs, whole life).
- **General (Non-life) Insurance** — health, motor, fire, marine, property, travel.

### Risk Management
**Risk** = uncertainty of loss. **Risk management** is identifying, assessing, and controlling risks.

**Risk handling strategies (4 T's):**
- **Avoidance** — don't undertake the risky activity.
- **Reduction/Mitigation** — reduce likelihood/impact (safety measures).
- **Retention** — accept/bear the risk yourself (self-insure).
- **Transfer** — shift to another party (insurance, hedging).

**Types of risk:** pure risk (only loss possible — insurable) vs speculative risk (gain or loss — not insurable).

## 3. BASICS OF FINANCIAL MARKETS & INSTRUMENTS

### Financial Market
A **financial market** is where **buyers and sellers trade financial assets** (shares, bonds, currencies). It channels savings into investments.

### Structure
| Market | What it does |
|--------|--------------|
| **Money Market** | Short-term funds (< 1 year): Treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, call money. |
| **Capital Market** | Long-term funds (> 1 year): shares, bonds, debentures. |
| → **Primary Market** | New securities issued first time (IPO — Initial Public Offering). |
| → **Secondary Market** | Existing securities traded (Stock Exchanges — NSE, BSE). |

### Financial Instruments
- **Equity Shares** — ownership; dividend + voting rights; high risk/return.
- **Preference Shares** — fixed dividend, priority over equity, usually no voting.
- **Debentures / Bonds** — debt instruments; fixed interest; no ownership.
- **Derivatives** — value derived from an underlying asset:
  - **Futures & Forwards** — contract to buy/sell at a future date at a set price.
  - **Options** — right (not obligation) to buy (call) or sell (put).
  - **Swaps** — exchange of cash flows (e.g., interest rate swaps).
- **Mutual Funds** — pool money from investors, invest in a diversified portfolio.
- **Money-market instruments** — T-bills, commercial paper, CDs.

### Key terms
- **IPO/FPO**, **Demat account**, **Sensex/Nifty** (stock indices), **Bull vs Bear market**, **Blue-chip stocks**.

## 4. TAXATION — INCOME TAX & GST

### A. Income Tax (Direct Tax)
A **direct tax** levied on the **income** of individuals and entities. Governed by the **Income Tax Act, 1961**; administered by the **CBDT** (Central Board of Direct Taxes).

**Five Heads of Income:**
1. **Salaries**
2. **Income from House Property**
3. **Profits & Gains of Business or Profession**
4. **Capital Gains** (from sale of assets — short-term / long-term)
5. **Income from Other Sources** (interest, dividends, lottery)

**Key terms:**
- **Assessee** — person liable to pay tax.
- **Assessment Year (AY)** — year in which income is taxed.
- **Previous Year (PY)** — year in which income is earned.
- **Gross Total Income → Deductions (e.g., Sec 80C) → Total Income → Tax.**
- **TDS** — Tax Deducted at Source.
- **PAN** — Permanent Account Number.
- **Old vs New Tax Regime** — slab-based; new regime has lower rates but fewer deductions.

### B. GST — Goods and Services Tax (Indirect Tax)
An **indirect tax** on the **supply of goods and services**, introduced **1 July 2017**. It replaced many taxes (VAT, excise, service tax) — "**One Nation, One Tax**." It is a **destination-based, multi-stage** tax with **input tax credit** (tax paid on inputs is set off against tax on output, avoiding cascading/"tax on tax").

**Types of GST:**
| Type | Applies to | Collected by |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| **CGST** | Intra-state (within a state) | Central Govt |
| **SGST** | Intra-state | State Govt |
| **IGST** | Inter-state (between states) & imports | Central Govt (shared) |
| **UTGST** | Union Territories | UT |

**Key facts:**
- **GST Slabs:** commonly 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%.
- **GSTIN** — GST identification number.
- **Input Tax Credit (ITC)** — core feature avoiding cascading effect.
- Governed by the **GST Council** (headed by the Union Finance Minister).

**Direct vs Indirect Tax:**
| Direct Tax | Indirect Tax |
|------------|--------------|
| On income/wealth | On goods & services |
| Paid directly by the person | Collected via seller, borne by consumer |
| e.g., Income Tax | e.g., GST |
| Burden cannot be shifted | Burden shifts to consumer |

## 5. BASICS OF AUDIT & ASSURANCE

### What is Auditing?
**Auditing** is the **independent examination of financial records** of an organization to express an **opinion on whether they show a true and fair view**. Governed in India by **Auditing Standards** and the **Companies Act**.

### Purpose
- Verify accuracy & fairness of financial statements.
- Detect & prevent errors and fraud.
- Build stakeholder confidence & ensure compliance.

### Types of Audit
- **Statutory Audit** — legally required (e.g., company audit under Companies Act).
- **Internal Audit** — conducted by the organization's own staff for control.
- **External Audit** — by independent external auditors.
- **Cost Audit, Tax Audit, Management Audit, Government Audit (CAG).**

### Assurance
**Assurance** is a professional service that **improves the quality/credibility of information** for decision-makers. Audit is the **highest level** of assurance (reasonable assurance). Levels:
- **Reasonable assurance** (audit — positive opinion).
- **Limited assurance** (review — negative opinion: "nothing came to our attention").

### Key terms
- **Auditor's Report** — Unqualified (clean), Qualified, Adverse, or Disclaimer of opinion.
- **Vouching** — checking transactions against evidence.
- **Verification** — confirming existence/ownership/value of assets & liabilities.
- **Internal Control** — systems to safeguard assets and ensure accuracy.

## 6. REGULATORY BODIES (very common MCQs)

| Body | Full Form | Regulates | Key facts |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **RBI** | Reserve Bank of India | Banking & monetary policy | India's **central bank**, est. **1935**. Controls money supply, inflation, currency issue, repo rate, CRR/SLR. |
| **SEBI** | Securities and Exchange Board of India | Securities/stock market | Est. **1988**, statutory in **1992**. Protects investors, regulates stock exchanges, IPOs, mutual funds, brokers. |
| **IRDAI** | Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India | Insurance sector | Est. **1999**. Regulates insurers, protects policyholders, licenses agents. |
| **ICAI** | Institute of Chartered Accountants of India | Accounting/audit profession | Est. **1949**. Sets accounting & auditing standards, regulates **Chartered Accountants (CA)**. |
| **PFRDA** | Pension Fund Regulatory & Development Authority | Pensions (NPS) | Regulates pension funds. |
| **ICSI / ICMAI** | Company Secretaries / Cost Accountants | CS & CMA professions | Professional bodies. |
| **CBDT / CBIC** | Central Boards of Direct Taxes / Indirect Taxes & Customs | Income tax / GST & customs | Under Ministry of Finance. |

### Quick memory hooks
- **RBI → Banks & money.** **SEBI → Share/stock market.** **IRDAI → Insurance.** **ICAI → Chartered Accountants/Audit.**

## ✅ Quick Recap (Additional Topics)
- **Banking:** deposits + lending; CRR/SLR/Repo; NPA; RBI is the central bank.
- **Insurance:** transfers risk for a premium; principles — utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity, subrogation, contribution, proximate cause. Risk handling: Avoid, Reduce, Retain, Transfer.
- **Financial Markets:** Money (short-term) vs Capital (long-term); Primary (IPO) vs Secondary (exchanges). Instruments: equity, debt, derivatives, mutual funds.
