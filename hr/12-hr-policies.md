# HR — Topic 12: HR Policies

Developing and Implementing Policies, Types of HR Policies, Communicating and Enforcing, Review & Updates, and Documentation.

---

## 1. What is an HR Policy? (Basic Idea)

An **HR policy** is a **written statement of the organisation's intent and general guideline for decision-making** on people matters — it tells managers and employees in advance how a situation will be handled.

**Everyday analogy:** A policy is the **rulebook of a game**. Without one, every decision becomes an argument and depends on who is refereeing that day. With one, everyone knows the call before the ball is kicked.

### Policy vs Procedure vs Rule vs Practice ⭐⭐ (a very common distinction question)

| | **Policy** | **Procedure** | **Rule** | **Practice** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | General **guideline** for decisions | **Step-by-step method** to execute it | **Specific do/don't** | What **actually happens** |
| Discretion | Allows managerial judgement | Limited — a defined sequence | **None** | Informal, undocumented |
| Answers | *What is our stand?* | *How is it done?* | *What is forbidden/required?* | *What do we really do?* |
| Example | "The company supports employee development" | "Apply via the LMS; manager approves within 5 working days; finance releases funds" | "No smoking on the premises" | "Nobody actually applies because approvals never come" |

> 🔑 **The exam insight:** the danger zone is the gap between **policy** and **practice**. A written policy that is routinely ignored is worse than having none — it destroys credibility and, in a dispute, becomes evidence against the employer. **What you tolerate becomes your real policy.**

## 2. Purpose and Importance of HR Policies

1. **Consistency** — similar cases are decided similarly, across departments and over time
2. **Fairness** — removes arbitrariness and favouritism
3. **Speed** — routine decisions do not need to be escalated or re-thought
4. **Legal compliance and protection** — documented policy is the employer's first line of defence
5. **Clarity of expectations** — employees know their rights and obligations
6. **Delegation** — managers can decide within known limits
7. **Reflects values** — a policy signals what the organisation stands for
8. **Reduces conflict and grievances** — most grievances stem from unclear or inconsistently applied rules *(see [Topic 9](09-grievance-handling.md))*
9. **Onboarding tool** — new joiners learn "how things work here"
10. **Continuity** — survives changes of manager

## 3. Characteristics of a Good HR Policy ⭐⭐

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| **Clear and unambiguous** | Written in plain language, not legal jargon |
| **In writing** | An unwritten policy cannot be enforced or proved |
| **Consistent** | Does not contradict other policies |
| **Fair and equitable** | Applies to everyone, including senior management |
| **Legally compliant** | Meets or exceeds every statutory requirement |
| **Aligned to strategy and values** | Supports what the business is trying to be |
| **Flexible** | Allows reasonable judgement in genuine exceptions |
| **Stable but not rigid** | Reliable enough to be trusted; revisable when conditions change |
| **Communicated** | Known to everyone it applies to |
| **Practical / enforceable** | Can actually be implemented with available resources |
| **Participative in origin** | Employees and managers consulted during drafting → higher acceptance |
| **Reviewed periodically** | Has an owner and a review date |

> ⚠️ **The most common design failure:** writing an aspirational policy the organisation has no intention or capacity to enforce (e.g. a "zero-tolerance" clause nobody applies to a top performer). A policy must be **enforceable in every case**, or it should not be written.

## 4. Types of HR Policies ⭐⭐

### By coverage
| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| **Recruitment & staffing** | Recruitment and selection, internal job posting, employee referral, background verification, relative/nepotism policy, probation and confirmation |
| **Attendance & time** | **Attendance**, working hours, shift, punctuality, work-from-home / hybrid, flexible hours, overtime |
| **Leave** | **Leave policy** (casual, sick, earned/privilege), maternity, paternity, bereavement, sabbatical, leave encashment, holiday calendar |
| **Compensation & benefits** | Salary structure, increment and promotion, bonus and incentive, reimbursement, travel and expense, insurance, provident fund, ESOP |
| **Performance & development** | Performance management, appraisal cycle, PIP, training and development, education/certification reimbursement, succession |
| **Conduct & discipline** | **Code of conduct**, disciplinary and misconduct, dress code, conflict of interest, moonlighting, gifts and hospitality, social media, anti-bribery |
| **Employee relations** | Grievance redressal, open door, whistle-blower, rewards and recognition, employee engagement |
| **Health, safety & wellbeing** | Workplace safety, first aid and emergency, substance abuse, EAP/mental health, medical check-up |
| **Diversity & respect** | Equal opportunity, **POSH / anti-sexual-harassment**, anti-discrimination, anti-bullying, accessibility |
| **Information & assets** | IT and acceptable use, data privacy and confidentiality, intellectual property, asset issue and return, BYOD |
| **Separation** | Resignation and notice period, exit and clearance, full & final settlement, retirement, termination, retrenchment |
| **Travel & mobility** | Domestic and international travel, relocation, transfer, deputation |

### The three policies every syllabus names explicitly

**① Attendance policy** — should cover: working hours and shifts; marking and recording (biometric/app); grace period; late-coming and half-day rules; unauthorised absence and its consequences; work-from-home eligibility; overtime approval; consequences of habitual absenteeism.

**② Leave policy** — should cover: types and annual entitlement; accrual and pro-rating for mid-year joiners; carry-forward limits and lapse; encashment rules; application and approval process and notice required; sandwich-leave treatment; medical certificate requirements; statutory leave (maternity, paternity, national holidays); leave without pay; the leave year.

**③ Code of conduct** — should cover: core values and expected behaviour; integrity, honesty and anti-bribery; conflict of interest and outside employment; confidentiality; use of company property and IT; harassment, discrimination and bullying; dress and workplace decorum; social media conduct; substance use; reporting violations (whistle-blowing) and non-retaliation; disciplinary consequences.

## 5. Developing HR Policies ⭐

### Sources of policy content
| Source | Contribution |
|--------|-------------|
| **Legislation** | The compulsory minimum — statute always overrides |
| **Organisational strategy and values** | What the policy must support |
| **Past practice and precedent** | What has actually been done so far |
| **Industry and competitor benchmarking** | What is normal in the market |
| **Employee feedback, surveys, grievances** | Where the current gaps are |
| **Union agreements / settlements** | Negotiated commitments |
| **Management judgement** | Balancing cost, risk and culture |
| **Incidents** | Many policies are written after something goes wrong |

### The development process
```
1. IDENTIFY THE NEED
   A recurring problem, a legal change, a strategic shift, or an incident.

2. RESEARCH
   Study the law, benchmark peers, review internal precedent and grievance data.

3. CONSULT STAKEHOLDERS
   Line managers, employees, legal, finance, union where applicable.
   (Consultation is what converts compliance into acceptance.)

4. DRAFT
   Purpose · Scope (who it applies to) · Definitions · The policy statement ·
   Procedure · Roles and responsibilities · Exceptions and approval authority ·
   Consequences of breach · Effective date · Owner · Review date · Version number.

5. REVIEW FOR LEGAL COMPLIANCE
   Legal/compliance sign-off. Check it does not contradict other policies.

6. APPROVE
   Formal sign-off by management / board as appropriate.

7. COMMUNICATE AND TRAIN
   Publish, explain, train managers who must apply it.

8. IMPLEMENT
   Effective from a stated date; handle transition cases.

9. MONITOR AND REVIEW
   Track how it works in practice; revise on schedule.
```

### The standard structure of a policy document
```
1. Policy title, number and version
2. Purpose / objective          — why this policy exists
3. Scope / applicability        — who and where it applies
4. Definitions                  — key terms
5. Policy statement             — the organisation's position
6. Procedure                    — the steps to follow
7. Roles and responsibilities   — employee / manager / HR
8. Exceptions and authority     — who may approve a deviation
9. Consequences of non-compliance
10. Related policies and documents
11. Effective date · Owner · Approver · Review date · Revision history
```

## 6. Communicating HR Policies ⭐

**A policy not communicated is a policy not in force.** In a dispute, an employer who cannot show the employee was informed will usually lose.

### Channels
| Channel | Use |
|---------|-----|
| **Employee handbook** | The consolidated reference |
| **HRIS / intranet portal** | Single source of truth, always current |
| **Induction and onboarding** | First exposure for new joiners |
| **Manager briefings and cascades** | Managers must be trained *before* employees are told |
| **Email and notice boards** | Announcing new or revised policies |
| **Training sessions** | For policies needing understanding, not just awareness (POSH, code of conduct, data privacy) |
| **Acknowledgement / sign-off** | **Recorded confirmation** that the employee has read and understood |
| **FAQs** | Reduces repeated queries and misinterpretation |

### Principles of effective communication
1. **Explain the "why"**, not only the rule — compliance without understanding is fragile
2. **Train the managers first** — they are the ones who will apply and be asked about it
3. Use **plain language**; keep legalese in an annexure
4. Provide **examples** of what is and is not acceptable
5. **Translate** where the workforce needs it
6. **Record acknowledgement** — signature or digital confirmation
7. **Repeat** — one email is not communication
8. Say clearly what **happens if it is breached**

## 7. Enforcing HR Policies ⭐⭐

### Why consistency is the whole game
Inconsistent enforcement is legally more dangerous than having no policy. If two employees commit the same breach and only one is punished, the punished employee has a strong claim of **discrimination or victimisation** — and the organisation's own inconsistency is the evidence.

> 🔑 **The rule:** apply the policy to the **star performer and the senior manager** exactly as to the junior employee. Selective enforcement destroys the policy and the culture with it.

### Progressive discipline ⭐
Escalating response, so the penalty matches the seriousness and repetition:
```
1. Verbal counselling / informal warning
2. Written warning
3. Final written warning
4. Suspension / withholding of increment / demotion
5. Termination
```
**Exception:** **gross misconduct** (theft, violence, serious harassment, fraud, safety endangerment) may justify moving straight to suspension and dismissal.

### Due process before punitive action (India) ⭐
```
1. Preliminary enquiry           — is there a case at all?
2. SHOW-CAUSE NOTICE             — put the allegation to the employee
3. CHARGE SHEET                  — formal, specific charges
4. Employee's written reply
5. DOMESTIC ENQUIRY              — impartial enquiry officer; employee may be
                                   assisted; witnesses examined and cross-examined
6. Enquiry report and findings
7. Proportionate punishment      — considering past record and severity
8. Written order with reasons; right of appeal
```
> ⚠️ Skipping the domestic enquiry, or having the complainant conduct it, breaches **natural justice** *(see [Topic 9](09-grievance-handling.md))* and is the most common reason employers lose dismissal cases.

### Principles of enforcement
| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Consistency** | Like cases treated alike |
| **Proportionality** | The penalty fits the offence |
| **Natural justice** | Hear both sides; no one judges their own case |
| **Documentation** | Every step recorded |
| **Timeliness** | Act promptly; delay implies condonation |
| **Confidentiality** | Discipline is not a public spectacle |
| **Corrective intent** | Aim to change behaviour, not merely to punish |
| **No retaliation** | Especially where a complaint or whistle-blowing is involved |

## 8. Policy Review and Updates ⭐

### Triggers for review
| Trigger | Example |
|---------|---------|
| **Change in law** | New Labour Codes; DPDP Act; POSH amendments |
| **Scheduled review** | Annual or biennial cycle |
| **Business change** | Merger, new location, new country, rapid growth |
| **Repeated grievances or disputes** | The policy itself is the problem |
| **Market/practice shift** | Hybrid working, moonlighting, AI tools |
| **Technology change** | New HRIS, biometric attendance, monitoring tools |
| **Audit or inspection finding** | Compliance gap identified |
| **Employee feedback** | Survey results show a policy is unworkable |
| **An incident** | A crisis exposes a gap |

### The review process
```
1. Owner initiates review at the scheduled date (or on a trigger)
2. Gather evidence — usage data, exceptions granted, grievances, queries, audit findings
3. Check legal currency against the latest statute
4. Consult stakeholders
5. Amend; assign a NEW VERSION NUMBER; record what changed and why
6. Legal review and re-approval
7. RE-COMMUNICATE the change — highlight what is different, not just reissue the document
8. Set the next review date
9. Archive the superseded version (do NOT delete — you may need to prove
   which version applied on a past date)
```

> 🔑 **Never delete an old policy version.** If a dispute concerns something that happened two years ago, you must be able to produce the policy **as it stood then**. Archive, don't overwrite.

## 9. Policy Documentation ⭐

### The employee handbook
The consolidated, employee-facing compilation of policies. Should include: welcome and organisational overview · values and code of conduct · employment terms · attendance, hours and leave · compensation and benefits · performance management · conduct and discipline · grievance and POSH procedures · health and safety · IT, confidentiality and data privacy · separation process · **acknowledgement page**.

> ⚠️ **A drafting caution:** many handbooks state that the handbook "is not a contract" and that policies may be amended at the employer's discretion — so that a discretionary practice is not accidentally converted into an enforceable contractual entitlement. Whether such a disclaimer holds depends on the jurisdiction, so legal review matters.

### Documentation hygiene
| Practice | Why |
|----------|-----|
| **Version control** | Know exactly which version applied when |
| **Unique policy number** | Easy referencing and cross-linking |
| **Named owner** | Someone is accountable for keeping it current |
| **Effective date and review date** | Prevents silent obsolescence |
| **Revision history table** | Shows what changed, when, and by whose approval |
| **Central repository** | One authoritative source; no conflicting copies circulating |
| **Access control** | Employee-facing vs internal HR/manager guidance separated |
| **Acknowledgement records** | Proof of communication |
| **Archived superseded versions** | Legal defensibility |
| **Language versions** | Where the workforce requires it |

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 12)

- **Policy** = general guideline (allows judgement). **Procedure** = the steps. **Rule** = rigid do/don't. **Practice** = what actually happens.
- ⭐ **The gap between policy and practice is the real risk.** *What you tolerate becomes your real policy.*
- Purpose: **consistency, fairness, speed, compliance, clarity, delegation**.
- A good policy is **written, clear, consistent, fair, legally compliant, aligned, flexible, communicated, enforceable and reviewed** — and drafted with consultation.
- **Never write a policy you will not enforce** in every case, including for senior people.
- Types: recruitment · **attendance** · **leave** · compensation · performance · **code of conduct** · grievance · safety · POSH/EEO · IT and data privacy · separation.
- Development: identify need → research → **consult** → draft → legal review → approve → communicate and train → implement → monitor and review.
- Policy structure: purpose · scope · definitions · statement · procedure · responsibilities · exceptions · consequences · dates · owner · **version**.
- **A policy not communicated is not in force.** Train managers first; record acknowledgement.
- **Enforcement:** consistency above all; **progressive discipline** (verbal → written → final → suspension → termination), with gross misconduct as the exception.
- **Due process in India:** show-cause notice → charge sheet → reply → **domestic enquiry** → findings → proportionate penalty → written order → appeal.
- **Review** on legal change, business change, recurring grievances, technology or incident. Use **version control** and **archive old versions** — never delete.
