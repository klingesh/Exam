# HR — Topic 6: Compensation & Benefits

Compensation Philosophies, Job Evaluation, Salary Structures, Types of Pay, Benefits, Legal Aspects and Pay Equity.

---

## 1. What is Compensation? (Basic Idea)

**Compensation** is the **total reward** — monetary and non-monetary — that an employee receives in exchange for work.

**Everyday analogy:** Compensation is not just the number on the payslip. It is the whole "deal": the salary, plus the health cover, the provident fund, the annual bonus, the cab facility and the paid leave. Employees compare **deals**, not just salaries.

### The two halves ⭐

| **Direct compensation** (paid in cash to the employee) | **Indirect compensation** (benefits and services) |
|---|---|
| Basic salary / wages | Health insurance |
| Dearness allowance (DA) | Provident fund, gratuity, pension |
| House rent allowance (HRA) | Paid leave, holidays |
| Bonus, commission, incentives | Canteen, transport, crèche |
| Overtime pay | Company car, accommodation, club membership |

### Objectives of a compensation system
1. **Attract** capable talent
2. **Retain** existing employees
3. **Motivate** higher performance
4. Ensure **internal and external equity**
5. **Comply** with statutory requirements
6. **Control labour cost** within the ability to pay
7. Support the **organisation's strategy** and culture

## 2. Compensation Philosophies and Strategies ⭐⭐

A **compensation philosophy** is the organisation's formal stance on how it will pay relative to the market.

### The three market positioning strategies ⭐

| Strategy | Pay level | Rationale | Risk |
|----------|-----------|-----------|------|
| **Lead the market** (pay above market, e.g. 75th percentile) | Highest | Attracts and retains the best; reduces attrition; can demand higher performance | High cost |
| **Match / meet the market** (at median, 50th percentile) | Average | Stays competitive at a sustainable cost — the commonest choice | No special pull |
| **Lag the market** (below market) | Lowest | Suits low-margin businesses; compensated by non-pay factors (job security, brand, learning) | High attrition, weaker talent |

> 💡 Organisations often **mix** strategies — lead for scarce, business-critical roles; match for the majority; lag for easily replaced roles.

### Other strategic choices
| Choice | Options |
|--------|---------|
| **Basis of pay** | Job-based (pay the position) vs **skill/competency-based** (pay the person's capability) vs performance-based |
| **Fixed vs variable mix** | High fixed = security; high variable = pay-for-performance |
| **Pay for performance vs pay for tenure** | Merit increases vs seniority increments |
| **Secrecy vs transparency** | Confidential pay vs published bands |
| **Centralised vs local** | One global structure vs market-adjusted by location |
| **Egalitarian vs hierarchical** | Narrow gap between levels vs wide differentials |

### The three types of equity ⭐⭐ (a very common question)

| Type | Comparison being made | Achieved through |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|
| **Internal equity** | *My pay vs other jobs inside this company* | **Job evaluation** |
| **External equity** | *My pay vs the same job in other companies* | **Salary surveys / market benchmarking** |
| **Individual equity** | *My pay vs my colleague doing the same job* | **Performance appraisal**, clear increment rules |

> 🔑 **Perceived inequity is more damaging than low pay.** An employee paid well but less than a weaker peer will be more demotivated than one paid modestly but fairly. This links directly to **Adams' Equity Theory** in [Topic 8](08-employee-relations.md).

## 3. Components of Compensation — the Indian CTC structure ⭐⭐

**CTC (Cost to Company)** = everything the employer spends on the employee in a year, including contributions the employee never sees in hand.

```
CTC
├── FIXED PAY
│   ├── Basic salary            (usually 40–50% of CTC; base for PF, gratuity, HRA)
│   ├── Dearness Allowance (DA) (inflation-linked; common in PSUs/government)
│   ├── House Rent Allowance    (typically 40–50% of Basic)
│   ├── Conveyance / transport allowance
│   ├── Medical allowance
│   └── Special allowance       (the balancing figure)
├── VARIABLE PAY
│   ├── Performance bonus / annual incentive
│   ├── Sales commission
│   └── Retention / joining bonus
├── EMPLOYER CONTRIBUTIONS (part of CTC, not of take-home)
│   ├── Employer PF (12% of Basic)
│   ├── ESI (where applicable)
│   └── Gratuity provision (≈ 4.81% of Basic)
└── PERQUISITES / BENEFITS IN KIND
    ├── Insurance premium, car, fuel, accommodation, meal cards
```

### Worked example — CTC to take-home ⭐

An offer of **CTC ₹8,00,000 per annum**. Basic is 40% of CTC; HRA is 50% of Basic; professional tax ₹2,400 p.a.

```
Step 1: Basic                    = 40% of 8,00,000        = ₹3,20,000
Step 2: HRA                      = 50% of 3,20,000        = ₹1,60,000
Step 3: Employer PF              = 12% of 3,20,000        =   ₹38,400
Step 4: Gratuity provision       = 4.81% of 3,20,000      =   ₹15,392
Step 5: Sub-total so far         = 3,20,000 + 1,60,000 + 38,400 + 15,392
                                                          = ₹5,33,792
Step 6: Special allowance (balance) = 8,00,000 − 5,33,792 = ₹2,66,208
        ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        CTC check                                          = ₹8,00,000 ✓

Step 7: GROSS SALARY (on the payslip; excludes employer contributions)
        = Basic + HRA + Special = 3,20,000 + 1,60,000 + 2,66,208
        = ₹7,46,208

Step 8: Deductions
        Employee PF (12% of Basic)   = ₹38,400
        Professional tax             =  ₹2,400
                                        ───────
                                       ₹40,800

Step 9: NET (take-home) before income tax = 7,46,208 − 40,800 = ₹7,05,408
        Monthly take-home ≈ ₹58,784
```

> 🎯 **The point of this example:** a ₹8 lakh CTC is **not** ₹8 lakh in hand. Roughly ₹94,000 of it is employer contributions and deductions. This gap is the single biggest cause of new-hire dissatisfaction, which is why HR must explain CTC at offer stage.

### Key definitions
> **Gross salary** = Basic + all allowances (before deductions)
> **Net / take-home salary** = Gross salary − deductions (PF, professional tax, TDS, other)
> **CTC** = Gross salary + employer contributions + benefits cost

## 4. Job Evaluation ⭐⭐⭐

**Job evaluation** determines the **relative worth of different jobs** in an organisation, in order to build a rational pay structure.

> ⚠️ **Critical distinction:** **Job evaluation rates the JOB. Performance appraisal rates the PERSON.** Getting these the wrong way round is a classic exam mistake.

### The four methods

| | Method | Type | How it works |
|---|--------|------|-------------|
| 1 | **Ranking** | Non-analytical, non-quantitative | Jobs ranked as whole jobs from most to least valuable. Simple; subjective; impractical beyond ~30 jobs |
| 2 | **Job classification / Grading** | Non-analytical, non-quantitative | Grades defined first, then jobs slotted into grades. Used in government service |
| 3 | **Point / Point rating method** | **Analytical, quantitative** — most widely used | Jobs broken into factors; factors weighted; points assigned and totalled |
| 4 | **Factor comparison** | Analytical, quantitative | Jobs compared factor by factor against benchmark jobs; monetary values assigned |

### Worked example — the point method ⭐

Four factors are chosen and weighted, with a 1,000-point maximum:

| Factor | Weight | Max points | Job A's score |
|--------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Skill & qualification | 40% | 400 | 300 |
| Responsibility | 30% | 300 | 210 |
| Effort (mental + physical) | 20% | 200 | 120 |
| Working conditions | 10% | 100 | 60 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **1,000** | **690** |

```
Job A = 690 points
```

Points are then mapped to pay grades:

| Points range | Grade | Salary band (₹ p.a.) |
|---|---|---|
| 0–299 | G1 | 3,00,000 – 4,50,000 |
| 300–549 | G2 | 4,50,000 – 6,50,000 |
| 550–749 | G3 | 6,50,000 – 9,00,000 |
| 750–1,000 | G4 | 9,00,000 – 13,00,000 |

✅ **Job A (690 points) falls in Grade G3.**

> 💡 **Why the point method dominates:** it is systematic, defensible in a dispute, and treats jobs analytically rather than as a matter of opinion — which is exactly what you need if pay is ever legally challenged.

## 5. Salary Structures ⭐⭐

A **salary structure** is the framework of **grades** and **pay ranges** into which all jobs are placed.

### Key terms and formulas

> **Midpoint = (Minimum + Maximum) ÷ 2**
>
> **Range Spread (%) = ((Maximum − Minimum) ÷ Minimum) × 100**
>
> **Compa-ratio (%) = (Employee's actual salary ÷ Midpoint of the range) × 100**

### Worked example ⭐

Grade G3 has a range of **₹40,000 – ₹60,000** per month. An employee in G3 earns **₹45,000**.

```
Step 1: Midpoint     = (40,000 + 60,000) ÷ 2 = ₹50,000
Step 2: Range spread = ((60,000 − 40,000) ÷ 40,000) × 100 = 50%
Step 3: Compa-ratio  = (45,000 ÷ 50,000) × 100 = 90%
```

**Interpreting the compa-ratio:**

| Compa-ratio | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **< 100%** | Paid **below** midpoint — typically newer or still developing in the role |
| **= 100%** | Paid **at** midpoint — fully competent performer |
| **> 100%** | Paid **above** midpoint — high performer or long-tenured; approaching the range ceiling |

✅ At **90%**, this employee sits below midpoint — reasonable for someone new to the grade, and a signal there is room for increments.

### Structural concepts
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Pay grade** | Group of jobs of roughly equal worth, paid within one range |
| **Broadbanding** | Collapsing many narrow grades into a few wide bands — supports flat structures and lateral moves |
| **Red circle rate** | An employee paid **above** the range maximum (often after a downgrade or long service) |
| **Green circle rate** | An employee paid **below** the range minimum |
| **Pay compression** | Gap between junior and senior pay becomes too small — usually because new hires are recruited at market rates while existing staff get modest increments |
| **Salary survey / benchmarking** | Collecting market pay data to set competitive ranges |
| **Increment** | Periodic rise within a grade |
| **Promotion increase** | Rise on moving to a higher grade |

## 6. Types of Compensation

### 6.1 Base pay
The fixed, guaranteed amount for the job — the foundation for PF, gratuity, HRA and most benefits. Provides **security**.

### 6.2 Variable pay
Pay that **depends on performance** and is not guaranteed.

| Form | Basis |
|------|-------|
| **Performance bonus** | Individual and/or company performance |
| **Sales commission** | Percentage of sales value |
| **Profit sharing** | Share of company profit |
| **Gainsharing** | Share of measurable cost savings/productivity gains |
| **ESOP** | Right to buy company shares at a set price |
| **Team/group incentive** | Team output |
| **Spot bonus / recognition award** | Immediate reward for a specific achievement |

### 6.3 Incentive schemes with formulas ⭐

**Taylor's differential piece rate:** below-standard output paid at a **low** piece rate; at-or-above standard paid at a **high** piece rate. Harsh but strongly motivating.

**Halsey Plan** — worker gets a fixed share (usually 50%) of the value of time saved:
> **Total wage = (Time taken × Rate) + 50% × (Time saved × Rate)**

**Rowan Plan** — bonus is proportionate to the fraction of time saved:
> **Total wage = (Time taken × Rate) + (Time saved ÷ Time allowed) × Time taken × Rate**

**Worked example:** Standard (allowed) time = **10 hours**; actual time taken = **8 hours**; rate = **₹50/hour**.

```
Time saved = 10 − 8 = 2 hours
Basic wage for time taken = 8 × 50 = ₹400

HALSEY:
  Bonus = 50% × (2 × 50) = 50% × 100 = ₹50
  Total = 400 + 50       = ₹450

ROWAN:
  Bonus = (2 ÷ 10) × 8 × 50 = 0.2 × 400 = ₹80
  Total = 400 + 80          = ₹480
```
✅ **Halsey ₹450, Rowan ₹480**

> 🔑 **The comparison rule (worth stating in an exam):**
> - Time saved **less than 50%** of time allowed → **Rowan pays more**
> - Time saved **exactly 50%** → **both pay the same**
> - Time saved **more than 50%** → **Halsey pays more**
>
> Rowan therefore protects the employer against over-generous bonuses from loose time standards.

## 7. Employee Benefits

### 7.1 Statutory benefits in India (legally compulsory)

| Benefit | Governing Act | Key point |
|---------|--------------|-----------|
| **Provident Fund (EPF)** | EPF & MP Act, 1952 | Employee 12% of Basic + employer 12% |
| **ESI** (medical) | ESI Act, 1948 | Employee 0.75%, employer 3.25% of wages |
| **Gratuity** | Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 | Payable after **5 years** continuous service |
| **Bonus** | Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 | Minimum **8.33%**, maximum **20%** of salary |
| **Maternity benefit** | Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (amended 2017) | **26 weeks** paid leave for the first two children |
| **Minimum wages** | Minimum Wages Act, 1948 | Government-notified floor wages |
| **Leave & working hours** | Factories Act, 1948 / Shops & Establishments Acts | Earned leave, weekly off, hours limits |

> **Gratuity formula**
> **Gratuity = Last drawn salary (Basic + DA) × (15 ÷ 26) × Years of service**
>
> *(15 days' wages per completed year; 26 = working days in a month)*

**Worked example:** Last drawn Basic + DA = **₹52,000/month**; service = **10 years**.
```
Gratuity = 52,000 × (15 ÷ 26) × 10
         = 52,000 × 15 = 7,80,000;  7,80,000 ÷ 26 = 30,000
         = 30,000 × 10
         = ₹3,00,000
```
✅ **Gratuity = ₹3,00,000**

### 7.2 Voluntary benefits

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Health & wellness** | Group health insurance, family cover, annual health check, gym, EAP/counselling |
| **Retirement** | Superannuation, NPS, voluntary PF |
| **Paid time off (PTO)** | Casual, sick, earned/privilege leave; sabbatical; paternity leave; bereavement leave |
| **Work–life** | Flexible hours, remote/hybrid work, crèche, transport |
| **Financial** | Meal cards, salary advances, loans at concessional rates, relocation support |
| **Development** | Tuition reimbursement, certification sponsorship |
| **Recognition** | Awards, long-service awards, referral bonus |
| **Insurance** | Group term life, accident cover |

> 💡 **Flexible/cafeteria benefits:** employees choose from a menu within a fixed value. A 25-year-old may want learning sponsorship; a 45-year-old may want family health cover. Same cost, far higher perceived value.

## 8. Legal and Regulatory Aspects

| Requirement | Implication for HR |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Minimum wages** | Cannot pay below the notified rate for that category and state |
| **Timely payment** | Wages must be paid within the prescribed period (Payment of Wages Act, 1936) |
| **Equal pay for equal work** | Equal Remuneration Act, 1976 — no gender-based pay difference for the same work |
| **Overtime** | Payable at a premium (commonly **twice** the ordinary rate under the Factories Act) |
| **Statutory deductions** | PF, ESI, professional tax and **TDS** must be deducted and deposited on time |
| **Bonus** | Statutory minimum 8.33% for eligible employees |
| **Gratuity** | Payable on exit after 5 years' service |
| **Full and final settlement** | Dues must be cleared within the prescribed period after exit |
| **Record keeping** | Registers of wages, attendance and deductions must be maintained |
| **The four Labour Codes** | Consolidate and modernise these obligations — see [Topic 11](11-labour-law-and-legal-requirements.md) |

> ⚠️ Rates, wage ceilings and thresholds are revised periodically. Quote them as *"currently around…"* rather than as permanent facts.

## 9. Pay Equity and Fairness ⭐⭐

**Pay equity** means people are paid fairly relative to the **value of their work**, free of bias.

### Forms of pay inequity
| Issue | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Gender pay gap** | Difference in average pay between men and women |
| **Equal pay for equal work** | Same role, same output, different pay — usually unlawful |
| **Equal pay for work of equal value** | Different jobs of comparable worth paid differently (e.g. a female-dominated role paid less than an equivalent male-dominated one) |
| **Pay compression** | New hires paid near existing seniors' levels |
| **Pay inversion** | New hires paid **more** than existing experienced staff — highly damaging to morale |
| **Favouritism** | Increments driven by relationships rather than performance |

### How HR builds fairness
1. **Job evaluation** for internal equity
2. **Market benchmarking** for external equity
3. **Documented, criteria-based** increment and promotion rules
4. **Pay audits** to detect unexplained gaps by gender, location or group
5. **Calibration** of appraisal ratings before pay decisions *(see [Topic 7](07-performance-management.md))*
6. **Manager training** so pay decisions are explained, not just announced
7. **Appropriate transparency** — publishing bands and the basis of increases, even if individual pay stays confidential

### Pay secrecy vs pay transparency

| Basis | **Secrecy** | **Transparency** |
|-------|------------|-----------------|
| Perceived fairness | Low — people assume the worst | Higher — decisions are visible |
| Conflict | Fewer open comparisons | More short-term challenges |
| Negotiating power | Favours the employer | Favours the employee |
| Pressure on HR | Low | High — every difference must be justifiable |
| Trend | Declining | **Rising**, driven by regulation and employee expectation |

> 🎯 **A strong closing line for a pay-equity question:** *"Pay must not only be fair — it must be visibly explainable. Employees accept differences they understand and resent differences they cannot."*

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 6)

**Formulas:**
```
Gross salary   = Basic + all allowances
Net salary     = Gross − deductions (PF, professional tax, TDS)
CTC            = Gross + employer contributions + benefits cost

Midpoint       = (Min + Max) ÷ 2
Range spread   = ((Max − Min) ÷ Min) × 100
Compa-ratio    = (Actual salary ÷ Midpoint) × 100

Gratuity       = Last drawn (Basic + DA) × 15/26 × Years of service

Halsey         = (Time taken × Rate) + 50% × (Time saved × Rate)
Rowan          = (Time taken × Rate) + (Time saved ÷ Time allowed) × Time taken × Rate
```

- **Direct** compensation = cash; **indirect** = benefits and services.
- Three market strategies: **lead / match / lag**.
- Three equities: **internal** (job evaluation), **external** (salary survey), **individual** (appraisal).
- **Job evaluation rates the JOB; appraisal rates the PERSON.**
- Four job-evaluation methods: **ranking, classification, point (most used), factor comparison**.
- **Compa-ratio < 100% = below midpoint; > 100% = above midpoint.**
- **Halsey vs Rowan:** Rowan pays more below 50% time saved, equal at 50%, Halsey more above 50%.
- Statutory in India: **PF (12%+12%), ESI, gratuity after 5 years, bonus 8.33%–20%, 26 weeks maternity**.
- **CTC ≠ take-home.** Employer contributions and deductions create the gap.
- **Perceived inequity damages morale more than low pay does.**
