# HR — Topic 11: Legal Requirements & Labour Law

Employment Laws, EEO & Discrimination, Workplace Safety, Unions & Collective Bargaining, Employment Contracts and Data Privacy in HR.

---

## 1. Why HR Must Know the Law (Basic Idea)

Labour law sets the **minimum floor** below which no employer may go, whatever the employee agrees to.

**Everyday analogy:** Labour law is the guard-rail on a mountain road. Most of the time you drive nowhere near it. But it is not optional, and the one time you need it, its absence is catastrophic.

> 🔑 **The principle that surprises people:** an employee **cannot contract out of a statutory right**. If a contract says "the employee waives gratuity", that clause is void — the law overrides the agreement. This is why HR compliance is not negotiable.

### Areas regulated by employment law
| Area | Typical regulation |
|------|-------------------|
| **Wages** | Minimum wage, timely payment, lawful deductions, equal pay, bonus |
| **Working hours** | Maximum hours, overtime premium, rest intervals, weekly off |
| **Leave** | Earned/annual, sick, casual, maternity, paternity, national holidays |
| **Safety & health** | Safe premises, equipment, hazardous processes, welfare facilities |
| **Social security** | Provident fund, insurance, gratuity, pension, compensation for injury |
| **Non-discrimination** | Protected characteristics, equal opportunity, harassment |
| **Employment security** | Notice, layoff, retrenchment, closure, unfair dismissal |
| **Industrial relations** | Union registration, recognition, bargaining, strikes, disputes |
| **Contract & data** | Terms of employment, confidentiality, personal data protection |

## 2. The US Statutes Named in the Syllabus ⭐⭐

The PDF syllabus uses **US** legislation, so learn these first.

| Act | Year | What it does | Key figures |
|-----|------|-------------|-------------|
| **FLSA** — Fair Labor Standards Act | 1938 | **Minimum wage, overtime, child labour, record-keeping** | Overtime at **1.5×** beyond **40 hours/week** |
| **Equal Pay Act** | 1963 | Equal pay for equal work regardless of sex | — |
| **Title VII, Civil Rights Act** | 1964 | Prohibits discrimination on **race, colour, religion, sex, national origin**; created the **EEOC** | Applies to employers with **15+** employees |
| **ADEA** — Age Discrimination in Employment Act | 1967 | Protects workers aged **40 and above** | 20+ employees |
| **OSHA** — Occupational Safety and Health Act | 1970 | Requires a **safe and healthy workplace**; created the OSHA agency | **General Duty Clause** |
| **ADA** — Americans with Disabilities Act | 1990 | Prohibits disability discrimination; requires **reasonable accommodation** | 15+ employees |
| **FMLA** — Family and Medical Leave Act | 1993 | **Up to 12 weeks unpaid, job-protected leave** for family/medical reasons | Employers with **50+** employees |
| **NLRA** — National Labor Relations Act (Wagner Act) | 1935 | Protects the right to **organise and bargain collectively**; created the NLRB | — |

### Key US EEO concepts ⭐
| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Disparate treatment** | **Intentional** discrimination against an individual because of a protected characteristic |
| **Disparate impact** | A **neutral-looking** policy that in effect disadvantages a protected group (e.g. a height requirement that screens out most women) |
| **Reasonable accommodation** | Adjustment enabling a qualified person with a disability to do the job (ramp, screen reader, flexible hours) — required unless it causes *undue hardship* |
| **Bona fide occupational qualification (BFOQ)** | A narrow, legitimate exception where a protected characteristic genuinely is essential to the role |
| **Affirmative action** | Positive steps to improve representation of under-represented groups |
| **Hostile work environment** | Harassment severe or pervasive enough to alter the conditions of employment |
| **Retaliation** | Punishing someone for complaining or participating in an investigation — itself unlawful |

## 3. Indian Labour Law ⭐⭐⭐

> 🇮🇳 If your exam is India-focused, this is the section that matters most. India historically had ~29 central labour laws, now consolidated into **four Labour Codes**.

### 3.1 The major individual Acts

| Act | Year | Core provision | Commonly asked figures |
|-----|------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Employees' Compensation Act** | 1923 | Compensation for injury/death arising out of employment | — |
| **Trade Unions Act** | 1926 | Registration and rights of trade unions | Min **7** members to apply; **10% or 100** workmen (whichever less) for registration |
| **Payment of Wages Act** | 1936 | Timely payment; only **authorised deductions** | — |
| **Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act** | 1946 | Written, certified conditions of employment | **100+** workers (raised to **300** under the IR Code) |
| **Industrial Disputes Act** | 1947 | Dispute machinery, layoff, retrenchment, closure, strikes | Works Committee **100+**; Grievance Committee **20+**; retrenchment comp. **15 days' pay per year** |
| **Factories Act** | 1948 | Safety, health, welfare, hours | **10+** workers with power / **20+** without; max **48 hrs/week, 9 hrs/day**; overtime at **2×** |
| **Minimum Wages Act** | 1948 | Government-notified floor wages by category and state | — |
| **ESI Act** | 1948 | Medical and cash benefits | Employee **0.75%**, employer **3.25%**; wage ceiling around **₹21,000/month** |
| **EPF & MP Act** | 1952 | Provident fund, pension, insurance | **20+** employees; **12% + 12%**; wage ceiling around **₹15,000** |
| **Apprentices Act** | 1961 | Regulation of apprenticeship training | — |
| **Maternity Benefit Act** | 1961 (amd. 2017) | Paid maternity leave | **26 weeks** for the first two children, **12 weeks** thereafter; crèche where **50+** employees |
| **Payment of Bonus Act** | 1965 | Statutory annual bonus | Min **8.33%**, max **20%**; eligibility wage ceiling around **₹21,000**; **30 days'** work required |
| **Contract Labour (Regulation & Abolition) Act** | 1970 | Regulates contract labour, principal employer duties | **20+** contract workers |
| **Payment of Gratuity Act** | 1972 | Gratuity on exit | **10+** employees; **5 years'** continuous service; **15/26** formula; ceiling around **₹20 lakh** |
| **Equal Remuneration Act** | 1976 | Equal pay for same or similar work regardless of gender | — |
| **Child Labour (Prohibition & Regulation) Act** | 1986 (amd. 2016) | Prohibits employment of children | Below **14** prohibited; **14–18** restricted from hazardous work |
| **POSH Act** | 2013 | Prevention of sexual harassment at the workplace | See below |
| **Shops & Establishments Acts** | state-wise | Hours, leave, holidays for shops and offices | Varies by state |

### 3.2 The four Labour Codes ⭐⭐⭐

India consolidated **29 central labour laws** into **four Codes**:

| Code | Year | Subsumes |
|------|------|----------|
| **Code on Wages** | 2019 | Payment of Wages Act 1936, Minimum Wages Act 1948, Payment of Bonus Act 1965, Equal Remuneration Act 1976 |
| **Industrial Relations Code** | 2020 | Trade Unions Act 1926, Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946, Industrial Disputes Act 1947 |
| **Code on Social Security** | 2020 | EPF, ESI, Maternity Benefit, Gratuity, Employees' Compensation and other social-security laws |
| **Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions (OSH) Code** | 2020 | Factories Act 1948, Contract Labour Act 1970, Mines Act, Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act and others |

**Key changes introduced by the Codes:**
- A **uniform statutory definition of "wages"** across all laws (a major payroll change)
- **National floor wage** below which no state may set minimum wages
- Threshold for **standing orders** raised from 100 → **300** workers
- Threshold for prior government permission for **layoff, retrenchment and closure** raised from 100 → **300** workers
- **14 days' strike notice extended to all** industrial establishments (previously only public utilities)
- **Fixed-Term Employment** formally recognised, with **pro-rata gratuity** and parity of benefits
- Social security extended toward **gig and platform workers**
- Single registration, single licence and web-based inspection ("inspector-cum-facilitator")

> ⚠️ **Important honesty point for your answer:** the Codes were **enacted** in 2019–2020, but their **coming into force has been phased** and depends on central and state rules being notified. Say *"enacted and being brought into force in phases — states must notify rules"* rather than asserting a single date. **Verify the current status before the exam**, because an examiner may expect either the old Acts or the Codes depending on when the paper was set.
>
> ⚠️ Wage ceilings, contribution rates and thresholds are **revised periodically**. Quote them as *"currently around…"*.

### 3.3 The POSH Act, 2013 ⭐⭐
*Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act*

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Internal Committee (IC)** | Mandatory where **10 or more** employees |
| **Local Committee (LC)** | At district level, for smaller establishments and for complaints against the employer |
| **Presiding Officer** | Must be a **woman employed at a senior level** |
| **Composition** | At least 2 members from employees committed to the cause of women or with legal knowledge, plus **1 external member** from an NGO/association; **at least half the members must be women** |
| **Complaint time limit** | Within **3 months** of the incident (extendable by a further 3 months) |
| **Inquiry** | To be completed within **90 days** |
| **Report** | Submitted within **10 days** of completing the inquiry |
| **Action** | Employer to act within **60 days** of the recommendation |
| **Other duties** | Written policy, awareness training, display of penal consequences, annual report |

## 4. Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) ⭐

**EEO** means every person gets a fair and equal chance in recruitment, pay, training, promotion and every other employment decision, judged only on **merit and job requirements**.

### Where discrimination can creep in
| Stage | Risk |
|-------|------|
| **Job advert** | "Young, energetic male candidates preferred" — directly discriminatory wording |
| **Screening** | Rejecting on marital status, pregnancy, caste, region, age |
| **Interview** | Questions about family plans, religion or disability unrelated to the job |
| **Selection tests** | Tests that are not job-related and disadvantage a group (**disparate impact**) |
| **Pay** | Different pay for the same work |
| **Promotion** | A **glass ceiling** for women or minorities |
| **Training** | Development opportunities offered selectively |
| **Termination** | Redundancy selection that targets a protected group |

### How HR builds an EEO-compliant system
1. **Job-related criteria only** — everything traceable to the **job specification**
2. **Structured interviews** and scoring rubrics *(see [Topic 5](05-recruitment-selection-and-training.md))*
3. **Validated selection tests** — job-related and non-discriminatory
4. **Diverse interview panels**
5. **Documented reasons** for every hiring, pay and promotion decision
6. **Pay-equity audits** *(see [Topic 6](06-compensation-and-benefits.md))*
7. **Anti-harassment policy + IC** with a safe reporting channel
8. **Reasonable accommodation** process for disability
9. **Training** on bias and lawful interviewing
10. **Non-retaliation** guarantee, actually enforced

## 5. Workplace Safety and Health ⭐

### The employer's core duty
Provide a workplace **free from recognised hazards** — the substance of OSHA's **General Duty Clause** in the US and the Factories Act / OSH Code in India.

### The hierarchy of hazard controls ⭐ (most to least effective)
```
1. ELIMINATION            Remove the hazard entirely
2. SUBSTITUTION           Replace it with something safer
3. ENGINEERING CONTROLS   Isolate people from it (guards, ventilation, interlocks)
4. ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROLS Change how people work (rotation, training, permits, signage)
5. PPE                    Personal protective equipment — the LAST resort
```
> 🔑 **The exam point:** PPE is **last**, not first. Handing out helmets is the weakest control because it depends entirely on human compliance and does nothing to the hazard itself.

### Statutory safety requirements (India — Factories Act / OSH Code)
**Health:** cleanliness, waste disposal, ventilation and temperature, dust and fume control, lighting, drinking water, latrines
**Safety:** fencing of machinery, safe hoists and lifts, precautions against fire, pressure plant, protection of eyes, safety officers in large factories
**Welfare:** washing facilities, first aid, canteens, rest rooms, crèches, welfare officers
**Hours:** max **48 hours/week**, **9 hours/day**, weekly holiday, rest interval, overtime at **twice** the ordinary rate
**Committees:** **Safety Committee** required in factories with hazardous processes

### HR's role in safety
Induction safety training · maintaining accident and near-miss records · statutory returns and registers · running the safety committee · investigating incidents · organising health check-ups · liaising with inspectors · ensuring PPE availability and use · **building a safety culture** where reporting hazards is welcomed rather than punished

### Safety metrics worth knowing
> **Frequency Rate = (Number of lost-time injuries ÷ Man-hours worked) × 1,000,000**
> **Severity Rate = (Man-days lost ÷ Man-hours worked) × 1,000,000**
> **Incidence Rate = (Number of injuries ÷ Average number of workers) × 1,000**

## 6. Labour Unions and Collective Bargaining ⭐⭐

### What a trade union is
A **trade union** is a formally organised association of workers formed to **protect and promote their common interests** in wages, working conditions and job security.

### Functions of a trade union
| Type | Functions |
|------|-----------|
| **Militant / protective** | Collective bargaining, strikes, resisting unfair treatment, safeguarding jobs |
| **Fraternal / welfare** | Member welfare, education, cooperative stores, financial assistance |
| **Political** | Influencing labour legislation and policy |
| **Social** | Community and social activity |

### Union structure in India
- **Plant/enterprise-level union** → **Industry federation** → **National centre** (central trade union organisations such as INTUC, AITUC, BMS, CITU, HMS)
- Registration under the **Trade Unions Act, 1926**: minimum **7** members to apply; **10% or 100** workmen (whichever is less) required for registration
- **Recognition** (the right to bargain) is distinct from **registration** and has historically been governed by state law and practice; the **IR Code, 2020** introduces the concept of a **negotiating union/council**, with sole negotiating rights for a union having the support of a specified proportion of workers

### Collective bargaining ⭐

**Collective bargaining** is negotiation between the employer and the workers' representatives over terms and conditions of employment, resulting in a **written settlement/agreement (CBA)**.

**Features:** collective · voluntary · continuous · bipartite · flexible · a process of compromise, not a single event

**Levels:** plant/enterprise → industry → national/sectoral

**Types** *(covered in [Topic 10](10-dispute-resolution.md))*: distributive · integrative · attitudinal structuring · intra-organisational

**Process:**
```
1. Preparation      — data on wages, productivity, ability to pay, comparable settlements
2. Charter of demands — submitted by the union
3. Negotiation      — meetings, positions, concessions
4. Agreement        — settlement drawn up and signed
5. Implementation   — terms applied
6. Administration   — interpretation of the agreement, handling disputes over it
```

**Prerequisites for success:** a **recognised, representative union** · genuine willingness on both sides · **good faith** and mutual trust · accurate shared data · negotiators with real authority to settle · a written agreement · effective grievance machinery to interpret it afterwards

### Unfair labour practices (both sides commit them)
| By the employer | By the union |
|-----------------|--------------|
| Interfering with the right to organise | Coercing workers to join |
| Victimising union office-bearers | Intimidation, **gherao**, violence |
| Refusing to bargain in good faith | Refusing to bargain in good faith |
| Discriminating against union members | Illegal strikes and go-slow |
| Setting up a puppet/company union | Damaging property |

## 7. Employment Contracts and Agreements ⭐⭐

### Offer letter vs Appointment letter
| | **Offer letter** | **Appointment letter** |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Offers the role; may be conditional (BGV, medical, documents) | **Confirms employment** and sets out full terms |
| Timing | Before joining | On or before joining |
| Legal weight | Preliminary; conditional | The primary **contract of employment** |

### Types of employment arrangement
Permanent/regular · **probationary** · **fixed-term** · part-time · temporary/casual · **contract labour** (via a contractor) · consultant/professional (not an employee) · apprentice/trainee · **gig/platform worker**

> 🔑 **Employee vs independent contractor** — a heavily litigated distinction. Courts look at **substance, not the label**: degree of **control** over how work is done, integration into the organisation, exclusivity, who supplies tools, whether payment is a salary or per assignment, and who bears business risk. Calling someone a "consultant" does not make them one if they are managed like an employee.

### Essential clauses in an employment contract
```
1.  Job title, reporting line and duties
2.  Place of work; transferability
3.  Date of joining and duration (if fixed-term)
4.  Remuneration and benefits (CTC breakdown)
5.  Working hours, leave and holidays
6.  Probation period and confirmation
7.  Confidentiality / NDA
8.  Intellectual property assignment
9.  Notice period (both sides) and termination conditions
10. Non-solicitation / non-compete (subject to enforceability)
11. Code of conduct, policies incorporated by reference
12. Conflict of interest and moonlighting
13. Governing law, jurisdiction and dispute resolution
```

### Restrictive covenants — the Indian position ⭐
| Covenant | Enforceability in India |
|----------|------------------------|
| **Confidentiality / NDA** | **Generally enforceable**, during and after employment |
| **Non-solicitation** (of clients/employees) | Often enforceable if reasonable |
| **Non-compete during employment** | Enforceable |
| **Post-employment non-compete** | **Generally VOID** under **Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872**, which makes agreements in restraint of trade void |
| **Employment bond** | Enforceable only to the extent of **reasonable, actual, provable costs** (e.g. genuine training expenditure) — not as a penalty |

> 🎯 **A high-value exam fact:** unlike in many jurisdictions, **post-employment non-compete clauses are largely unenforceable in India** because of Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act. Employers therefore rely on **confidentiality and non-solicitation** clauses instead. Mentioning Section 27 by name will distinguish your answer.

## 8. Data Privacy in HR ⭐⭐

HR holds the most sensitive data in any organisation: identity documents, bank details, salary, health records, performance ratings, disciplinary files, background checks and biometric attendance.

### The core principles (common to GDPR and India's DPDP Act)
| Principle | Meaning for HR |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Lawful basis / consent** | Collect only with a lawful basis; notice must explain the purpose |
| **Purpose limitation** | Data collected for payroll cannot be repurposed for surveillance |
| **Data minimisation** | Collect only what is genuinely needed — do not ask for what you will never use |
| **Accuracy** | Keep records correct and updated |
| **Storage limitation / retention** | Delete when the lawful purpose ends and the retention period expires |
| **Security safeguards** | Access control, encryption, restricted permissions, audit logs |
| **Accountability** | Documented policies, records of processing, breach response plan |
| **Rights of the individual** | Access, correction, erasure; grievance redressal |
| **Breach notification** | Notify the regulator and affected individuals promptly |

### The relevant laws
- **GDPR** (EU, in force 2018) — applies extraterritorially where EU residents' data is processed; the global benchmark
- **DPDP Act — Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** (India) — introduces **Data Fiduciary** (the organisation) and **Data Principal** (the individual), consent and notice requirements, security safeguards, breach reporting and a **Data Protection Board**
- **IT Act, 2000** and the SPDI Rules, 2011 — earlier Indian framework for sensitive personal data

> ⚠️ DPDP Act implementation is being rolled out through subordinate rules. Treat the detail as "in force with rules being notified" and verify current status.

### Practical HR safeguards
Role-based access to HRIS · encrypt and restrict payroll and medical data · **written consent** for background verification · a published **retention schedule** for ex-employee records · vendor/processor agreements with payroll and background-check providers · **anonymise** data used for analytics · a **breach response plan** · **train HR staff** — most breaches are human error, not hacking · transparent policy on any monitoring of employees

### The recurring ethical tension
| Employer's legitimate interest | Employee's privacy interest |
|------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Monitoring productivity and system use | Freedom from constant surveillance |
| Background verification | Proportionality; consent; relevance |
| Biometric attendance | Sensitive biometric data security |
| Wellness and health programmes | Confidentiality of health information |
| Investigating misconduct | Fair process; no fishing expeditions |

> 💡 The test HR should apply: is the intrusion **proportionate** to the legitimate purpose, is it **transparent**, and is it **the minimum necessary**?

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 11)

- **An employee cannot contract out of a statutory right.** Law overrides the contract.
- **US Acts:** **FLSA** (wages/overtime, 1.5× beyond 40 hrs) · **Title VII 1964** (race, colour, religion, sex, national origin; created **EEOC**) · **ADEA** (40+) · **ADA** (disability, reasonable accommodation) · **FMLA** (**12 weeks** unpaid) · **OSHA** (safe workplace) · **NLRA** (right to organise).
- **Disparate treatment = intentional; disparate impact = neutral policy, unequal effect.**
- **Indian Acts:** Trade Unions 1926 · Payment of Wages 1936 · Standing Orders 1946 · **ID Act 1947** · **Factories 1948** · Minimum Wages 1948 · ESI 1948 · **EPF 1952** · Maternity Benefit 1961 (**26 weeks**) · Bonus 1965 (**8.33%–20%**) · Contract Labour 1970 · **Gratuity 1972** (5 years, 15/26) · Equal Remuneration 1976 · **POSH 2013**.
- **Four Labour Codes** consolidate 29 laws: **Wages 2019**, **Industrial Relations 2020**, **Social Security 2020**, **OSH 2020**. Key changes: uniform "wages" definition, national floor wage, thresholds raised **100 → 300**, 14-day strike notice for all, fixed-term employment with pro-rata gratuity. **Implementation is phased — verify current status.**
- **POSH:** IC at **10+** employees · senior woman presiding officer · half the members women · one external NGO member · complaint in **3 months** · inquiry in **90 days**.
- **Hazard control hierarchy:** elimination → substitution → engineering → administrative → **PPE last**.
- **Factories Act:** 48 hrs/week, 9 hrs/day, overtime at **2×**.
- **Trade Unions Act:** min **7** members to apply; **10% or 100** for registration. **Registration ≠ recognition.**
- **Collective bargaining** = collective, voluntary, continuous, bipartite; needs a recognised union and good faith.
- **Contracts:** offer letter is conditional; **appointment letter is the contract**. Employee vs contractor is decided on **substance (control), not label**.
- ⭐ **Post-employment non-competes are generally VOID in India — Section 27, Indian Contract Act 1872.** NDAs and non-solicitation clauses survive; bonds recover only real, reasonable costs.
- **Data privacy:** consent, purpose limitation, **minimisation**, retention limits, security, breach notification. **GDPR** (EU) and **DPDP Act 2023** (India — Data Fiduciary/Data Principal).
