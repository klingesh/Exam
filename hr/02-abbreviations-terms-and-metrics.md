## 2. Key HR Terminology ⭐⭐

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Job Analysis** | Systematic study of a job to find out **what is done, how, and by whom**. Produces the JD and JS. |
| **Job Description** | Written statement of **duties, responsibilities, reporting and working conditions** of the job. |
| **Job Specification** | Statement of the **qualifications, experience, skills and traits** the person must have. |
| **Job Evaluation** | Determining the **relative worth of jobs** to fix pay grades (jobs, not people). |
| **Job Design** | Structuring the content of a job — rotation, enlargement, enrichment. |
| **Job Enlargement** | Adding **more tasks at the same level** (horizontal expansion). |
| **Job Enrichment** | Adding **more responsibility and autonomy** (vertical expansion). |
| **Job Rotation** | Moving an employee **between jobs** to build variety and skills. |
| **Competency** | A cluster of knowledge, skill, behaviour and attitude that causes superior performance. |
| **Competency Mapping** | Identifying the competencies needed for a role and assessing people against them. |
| **Succession Planning** | Identifying and developing internal people to fill **key positions** in future. |
| **Talent Management** | End-to-end strategy for attracting, developing, engaging and retaining high-value employees. |
| **Talent Acquisition** | The strategic, long-term view of recruitment (employer brand, pipeline), vs day-to-day recruiting. |
| **Onboarding** | Full process of integrating a new hire — longer and broader than induction. |
| **Induction / Orientation** | Formal introduction to the organisation in the first days. |
| **Attrition / Turnover** | Rate at which employees leave. |
| **Voluntary vs Involuntary attrition** | Employee chooses to leave vs organisation ends employment. |
| **Retention** | Keeping employees from leaving. |
| **Absenteeism** | Unauthorised or unscheduled absence from work. |
| **Employee Engagement** | Emotional commitment an employee has to the organisation and its goals. |
| **Employee Value Proposition (EVP)** | The total deal — pay, growth, culture, purpose — offered in exchange for work. |
| **Employer Branding** | Managing the organisation's reputation as a place to work. |
| **Moonlighting** | Holding a second job alongside primary employment. |
| **Bench** | Employees on payroll but not currently assigned to billable work (IT/ITES). |
| **Notice Period** | Contractual time an employee must serve after resigning. |
| **Full and Final Settlement** | Final clearing of dues at exit. |
| **Exit Interview** | Structured discussion with a leaving employee to learn the real reason. |
| **Garden Leave** | Employee is asked to stay away during notice period but remains paid. |
| **Golden Handshake** | Large severance payment on early exit. |
| **Whistle-blower** | Employee who reports wrongdoing inside the organisation. |
| **Span of Control** | Number of subordinates reporting to one manager. |
| **Attrition of critical talent** | Loss of employees in hard-to-replace roles — tracked separately from overall attrition. |

## 3. HR Metrics & Analytics ⭐⭐⭐ (the formula section)

### 3.1 Attrition / Turnover

> **Attrition Rate (%) = (Number of separations during the period ÷ Average number of employees) × 100**
>
> **Average number of employees = (Opening headcount + Closing headcount) ÷ 2**

**Worked example:**
A company began the year with **480** employees and ended with **520**. During the year **75** employees left.

```
Step 1: Average employees = (480 + 520) ÷ 2 = 500
Step 2: Attrition rate    = (75 ÷ 500) × 100
Step 3:                   = 15%
```
✅ **Attrition = 15%**

### 3.2 Retention

> **Retention Rate (%) = (Number of employees who remained for the whole period ÷ Number of employees at the start) × 100**

**Example:** Of the 480 employees present on 1 April, 420 were still employed on 31 March.
```
Retention = (420 ÷ 480) × 100 = 87.5%
```
> ⚠️ **Retention rate and attrition rate do not always add to 100%** — retention counts only the original group, while attrition uses the average headcount and includes people who joined *and* left within the period.

### 3.3 Absenteeism

> **Absenteeism Rate (%) = (Number of man-days lost ÷ (Average employees × Scheduled working days)) × 100**

**Example:** 500 employees, 25 working days in the month, 375 man-days lost.
```
Scheduled days = 500 × 25 = 12,500
Absenteeism    = (375 ÷ 12,500) × 100 = 3%
```

### 3.4 Recruitment metrics

> **Cost per Hire = (Internal recruiting costs + External recruiting costs) ÷ Number of hires**
>
> **Time to Fill** = days from **requisition approved** → **offer accepted**
> **Time to Hire** = days from **candidate applied** → **offer accepted**
>
> **Offer Acceptance Rate (%) = (Offers accepted ÷ Offers made) × 100**
>
> **Yield Ratio (%) = (Candidates moving to the next stage ÷ Candidates at the current stage) × 100**
>
> **Selection Ratio = Number selected : Number of applicants**

**Worked example — cost per hire:**
Internal costs (recruiter salaries, ATS) = ₹2,00,000; external costs (job boards, agency fees) = ₹6,00,000; hires made = 40.
```
Cost per hire = (2,00,000 + 6,00,000) ÷ 40 = 8,00,000 ÷ 40 = ₹20,000
```

**Worked example — recruitment funnel and yield ratios:**

| Stage | Number | Yield from previous stage |
|-------|--------|---------------------------|
| Applications received | 1,000 | — |
| Shortlisted | 250 | 250/1,000 = **25%** |
| Interviewed | 100 | 100/250 = **40%** |
| Offers made | 25 | 25/100 = **25%** |
| Offers accepted (joined) | 20 | 20/25 = **80%** (offer acceptance rate) |

```
Selection ratio = 20 : 1,000 = 1 : 50
```
> 💡 **Interpretation:** a low selection ratio (1:50) means the organisation is selective — good quality, but expensive sourcing. A high offer acceptance rate (80%) means the offer is competitive.

### 3.5 Productivity & efficiency metrics

> **Revenue per Employee = Total revenue ÷ Number of employees**
>
> **HR-to-Employee Ratio = Number of HR staff ÷ Total employees** (often stated per 100 employees)
>
> **Overtime Percentage (%) = (Overtime hours ÷ Total regular hours) × 100**
>
> **Training ROI (%) = ((Monetary benefit of training − Cost of training) ÷ Cost of training) × 100**

**Example — revenue per employee:** Revenue ₹50 crore, 500 employees → `₹50,00,00,000 ÷ 500 = ₹10,00,000 per employee`

**Example — HR-to-employee ratio:** 5 HR staff for 500 employees → `5 ÷ 500 = 1%`, i.e. **1 HR person per 100 employees (1:100)**

**Example — training ROI:** Training cost ₹2,00,000; measured benefit ₹5,00,000.
```
ROI = ((5,00,000 − 2,00,000) ÷ 2,00,000) × 100 = (3,00,000 ÷ 2,00,000) × 100 = 150%
```

### 3.6 The four levels of HR Analytics ⭐

| Level | Question it answers | HR example |
|-------|--------------------|-----------|
| **Descriptive** | What happened? | Attrition was 15% last year |
| **Diagnostic** | Why did it happen? | Attrition was highest among 2–3 year tenure staff in one location, driven by pay |
| **Predictive** | What will happen? | These 40 employees have a high probability of resigning next quarter |
| **Prescriptive** | What should we do? | Offer this cohort a targeted retention bonus and a career conversation |

> 🎯 **Exam tip:** this is the **same four-level framework** used in Systems/analytics papers. One framework, two subjects.

**Leading vs lagging indicators**
- **Lagging** = tells you what already happened (attrition rate, cost per hire)
- **Leading** = predicts what will happen (engagement score, absenteeism trend, internal mobility rate)

## 4. Legal & Compliance Terms

### From the syllabus (US)
| Term | What it does |
|------|-------------|
| **FLSA** (1938) | Sets **minimum wage, overtime pay (1.5× beyond 40 hrs/week), record-keeping and child-labour** standards |
| **ADA** (1990) | Prohibits discrimination against people with **disabilities**; requires **reasonable accommodation** |
| **FMLA** (1993) | Entitles eligible employees to **up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave** for specified family/medical reasons |
| **OSHA** (1970) | Requires employers to provide a **safe and healthy workplace**; sets and enforces safety standards |
| **EEO / EEOC** | Principle and enforcing agency for **non-discrimination** in employment |
| **Title VII, Civil Rights Act (1964)** | Prohibits discrimination on **race, colour, religion, sex, national origin** |

### General compliance vocabulary
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Statutory compliance** | Obligations imposed by law (PF, ESI, minimum wages, gratuity) |
| **Due diligence** | Reasonable steps an employer must take to verify and protect |
| **Vicarious liability** | Employer held responsible for acts of its employees |
| **Constructive dismissal** | Employee resigns because conditions were made intolerable — treated as dismissal |
| **Wrongful termination** | Ending employment in breach of law or contract |
| **Disparate treatment** | Intentional discrimination against an individual |
| **Disparate impact** | A neutral-looking policy that in effect disadvantages a protected group |
| **Reasonable accommodation** | Adjustment enabling a person with a disability to perform the job |
| **At-will employment** | (US) Either party may end employment at any time, with limited exceptions |
| **Data privacy in HR** | Lawful, limited, secure handling of employee personal data |

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 2)

**Must-know formulas:**
```
Attrition Rate      = (Separations ÷ Average employees) × 100
Average employees   = (Opening + Closing) ÷ 2
Retention Rate      = (Employees who stayed ÷ Employees at start) × 100
Absenteeism Rate    = (Man-days lost ÷ (Employees × Working days)) × 100
Cost per Hire       = (Internal + External recruiting costs) ÷ Number of hires
Offer Acceptance    = (Offers accepted ÷ Offers made) × 100
Yield Ratio         = (Candidates to next stage ÷ Candidates at stage) × 100
Selection Ratio     = Selected : Applicants
Revenue per Employee= Revenue ÷ Employees
Training ROI        = ((Benefit − Cost) ÷ Cost) × 100
```

- **JD = the job. JS = the person.** Job evaluation values **jobs**; performance appraisal values **people**.
- **Enlargement = horizontal** (more tasks). **Enrichment = vertical** (more authority).
- **Four analytics levels:** Descriptive → Diagnostic → Predictive → Prescriptive.
- **Leading** indicators predict; **lagging** indicators report.
- **FLSA** = wages/overtime, **ADA** = disability, **FMLA** = 12 weeks leave, **OSHA** = safety, **EEOC** = discrimination.
- Learn the abbreviation tables cold — they are the highest-yield marks in the paper.
