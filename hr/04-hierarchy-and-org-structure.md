# HR — Topic 4: Hierarchy & Organisational Structure

Organisational Structures and their HR Implications, Levels of HR Management, Reporting Relationships, Span of Control and Delegation.

---

## 1. What is Organisational Structure? (Basic Idea)

**Organisational structure** is the formal system that defines **who does what, who reports to whom, and how decisions flow** through the organisation.

**Everyday analogy:** A structure is the wiring diagram of a company. Two firms can have identical people and products, but if one has clear wiring and the other has tangled wiring, only one of them works.

### Why HR cares about structure
Structure directly determines:
- **How many managers** are needed (and therefore the salary bill)
- **Career paths** — a tall structure offers many promotions; a flat one offers few
- **Recruitment profiles** — flat organisations need self-directed people
- **Communication speed** and how much gets distorted
- **Who conducts appraisals** and who handles grievances
- **Training needs** — matrix structures demand negotiation and influencing skills

---

## 2. Types of Organisational Structure ⭐⭐

| Structure | How it works | HR implication |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Line** | Direct vertical authority; each person has one boss | Simple, clear accountability; overloads managers; limited specialist support |
| **Line and Staff** | Line managers run operations; staff experts (HR, legal, finance) advise | This is where **HR usually sits**; risk of line–staff conflict over authority |
| **Functional** | Departments by function — production, sales, HR, finance | Deep expertise, but silos; poor cross-functional coordination |
| **Divisional** | Units by product, geography or customer, each fairly self-contained | Duplication of HR effort in each division; better local responsiveness |
| **Matrix** | Employee reports to **both** a functional head **and** a project head | Dual reporting — needs clear appraisal rules; high conflict potential |
| **Flat** | Very few management levels; wide spans | Fewer promotions; needs empowered, self-managing staff |
| **Tall / Hierarchical** | Many levels; narrow spans | Clear promotion ladder; slow decisions; expensive |
| **Team-based** | Work organised around self-managed teams | Team-based rewards and appraisals needed |
| **Network / Virtual** | Small core, most work outsourced | Managing contractors and vendors becomes an HR/legal task |

### Matrix structure — know the trade-off ⭐

```
                    FUNCTIONAL HEADS
                 ┌────────┬────────┬────────┐
                 │Engineer│Finance │Marketing│
        ┌────────┼────────┼────────┼────────┤
PROJECT │Project A│   ●    │   ●    │   ●    │  ← each ● reports to BOTH
HEADS   │Project B│   ●    │   ●    │   ●    │     a functional head and
        │Project C│   ●    │   ●    │   ●    │     a project head
        └────────┴────────┴────────┴────────┘
```

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|-----------|---------------|
| Efficient sharing of scarce specialists | **Dual authority** → confusion and conflict |
| Fast response to project needs | Employee stress from two bosses with competing demands |
| Cross-functional learning | Slower decisions (need agreement from two sides) |
| Flexible resource deployment | Appraisal becomes complicated — who rates the employee? |

> 🎯 **Exam favourite:** *"What is the main problem with a matrix structure?"* → **Violation of the unity of command principle** (one employee, one boss), leading to conflicting instructions and accountability confusion.

## 3. Tall vs Flat Structures ⭐⭐ (very common comparison)

| Basis | **Tall (Hierarchical)** | **Flat** |
|-------|------------------------|----------|
| Number of levels | Many | Few |
| Span of control | **Narrow** | **Wide** |
| Supervision | Close and tight | Loose; relies on self-direction |
| Communication | Slow; more distortion as it passes levels | Fast; less distortion |
| Decision-making | Slow, centralised | Quicker, more decentralised |
| Cost | High (many managers) | Lower |
| Promotion opportunities | **Many** rungs | **Few** rungs → risk of career frustration |
| Employee autonomy | Low | High |
| Suits | Large, stable, standardised operations; manufacturing | Start-ups, knowledge and creative work |
| HR challenge | Bureaucracy, slow response, cost | Career pathing, retaining ambitious staff, manager overload |

> 💡 **Delayering / flattening** = deliberately removing management levels to cut cost and speed up decisions. HR consequence: fewer promotions, so organisations shift to **lateral moves, skill-based pay and enriched roles** to keep people motivated.

## 4. Span of Control ⭐⭐⭐

**Span of control** (span of management) = the **number of subordinates directly reporting to one manager**.

### The core relationship
> **Wide span → fewer levels → flatter organisation**
> **Narrow span → more levels → taller organisation**

For a fixed number of employees, span and number of levels move in **opposite** directions.

### Worked example — how span decides the shape ⭐

Take an organisation with **64 front-line workers**.

**Case A — span of 4:**
```
64 workers ÷ 4  = 16 supervisors
16 supervisors ÷ 4 =  4 managers
 4 managers ÷ 4    =  1 head

Levels: workers → supervisors → managers → head  =  4 LEVELS
Managers required: 16 + 4 + 1 = 21
```

**Case B — span of 8:**
```
64 workers ÷ 8 = 8 supervisors
 8 supervisors ÷ 8 = 1 head

Levels: workers → supervisors → head  =  3 LEVELS
Managers required: 8 + 1 = 9
```

✅ **Doubling the span cut one whole level and reduced managers from 21 to 9** — a huge saving in salary cost and a much faster flow of information.

### Factors that allow a WIDER span
- Work is **routine and standardised**
- Subordinates are **skilled, trained and experienced**
- Clear **rules, policies and SOPs** exist
- Strong **information systems** and dashboards
- Subordinates are **physically together**
- Manager is experienced and delegates well

### Factors that force a NARROWER span
- Work is **complex, varied or non-routine**
- Subordinates are **new or unskilled**
- Frequent **change** and unclear procedures
- Team is **geographically dispersed**
- Work requires **close supervision** (safety-critical, high value)

### Graicunas' theorem — why spans cannot grow indefinitely
V. A. Graicunas showed that relationships a manager must handle grow **exponentially**, not linearly:

> **Total relationships = n (2ⁿ⁄2 + n − 1)**, where n = number of subordinates

| Subordinates (n) | Total relationships |
|---|---|
| 4 | **44** |
| 5 | **100** |
| 6 | **222** |

Adding just two subordinates (4 → 6) multiplies the manager's relationship load **five times over**. That is the mathematical case for limiting span.

## 5. Delegation ⭐⭐

**Delegation** is the process by which a manager **assigns part of his work to a subordinate**, along with the authority needed to do it.

### The three elements ⭐ (the exam point is in the third)

| Element | Meaning | Can it be delegated? |
|---------|---------|---------------------|
| **Responsibility** | The duty/task to be performed | Yes — can be assigned |
| **Authority** | The right to take decisions and use resources | **Yes** — must be delegated along with the task |
| **Accountability** | Being answerable for the result | **NO — accountability can never be delegated** |

> 🎯 **The single most asked line on delegation:** *"Authority can be delegated, but accountability cannot."* A manager who delegates a task remains answerable for the outcome.

### Principles of delegation
1. **Authority must match responsibility** — never give a task without the power to do it
2. **Unity of command** — one subordinate should take orders from one superior
3. **Clarity** — the task, limits and expected result must be explicit
4. **Delegate by results expected**, not by activity
5. **Absoluteness of accountability** — the superior stays accountable

### Barriers to delegation
| From the manager | From the subordinate |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| "I can do it better myself" | Fear of criticism or failure |
| Fear of losing importance or control | Lack of confidence or skill |
| Lack of trust in subordinates | No incentive to take on more |
| Poor ability to instruct | Already overloaded |
| Fear the subordinate outshines them | Prefers to depend on the boss |

### Centralisation vs Decentralisation

| Basis | **Centralisation** | **Decentralisation** |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Decision authority | Held at the top | Pushed down the hierarchy |
| Speed | Slow | Fast |
| Uniformity | High | Lower; local variation |
| Control | Tight | Looser |
| Manager development | Limited | Strong — juniors learn to decide |
| Suits | Small firms, crisis, standard products | Large, diversified, geographically spread firms |

## 6. Levels of HR Management

| Level | Roles | Focus | Nature of work |
|-------|-------|-------|----------------|
| **Entry-level** | HR Executive, HR Assistant, Recruiter, HR Trainee | Transactions and data | Records, documentation, sourcing, scheduling, payroll inputs |
| **Mid-level** | HR Generalist, HR Specialist, HRBP, Asst./Deputy Manager | Programmes and processes | Running the recruitment, training, appraisal and engagement cycles; handling grievances |
| **Senior-level** | HR Manager, HR Head, VP HR, **CHRO** | Strategy and policy | Workforce planning, organisation design, culture, succession, HR budget, board reporting |

**The corresponding skill shift:** entry level needs **technical HR knowledge**; mid level needs **people and process skills**; senior level needs **business acumen and strategic thinking**.

## 7. Reporting Relationships and Communication Channels ⭐

### Formal communication — the four directions

| Direction | Flow | Typical content |
|-----------|------|----------------|
| **Downward** | Superior → subordinate | Instructions, policies, goals, feedback |
| **Upward** | Subordinate → superior | Reports, suggestions, grievances, survey responses |
| **Horizontal / Lateral** | Peer ↔ peer, same level | Coordination between departments |
| **Diagonal / Crosswise** | Across levels **and** departments | A junior analyst dealing directly with a senior manager in another function |

### Informal communication — the grapevine
Unofficial channel that runs on personal relationships. It is **fast** but **unreliable**.

| Grapevine pattern | How information travels |
|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Single strand** | A tells B, B tells C, C tells D — a chain (most distortion) |
| **Gossip chain** | One person tells everyone |
| **Probability chain** | Information passed randomly to anyone |
| **Cluster chain** | A tells a selected few, who each tell a selected few (most common in practice) |

> 💡 **HR's approach to the grapevine:** you cannot abolish it, so **monitor it and pre-empt it**. Rumours flourish where official communication is absent — the cure for a damaging grapevine is faster, more transparent formal communication.

### Related structural terms
- **Unity of command** — each employee should report to only **one** superior (violated by the matrix structure).
- **Chain of command / scalar chain** — the unbroken line of authority from top to bottom.
- **Gang plank** (Fayol) — a permitted **shortcut across the chain** for urgent matters, so information doesn't have to travel all the way up and down.

---

## 8. Barriers to Communication (and how HR removes them)

| Barrier type | Examples | Remedy |
|-------------|----------|--------|
| **Physical** | Noise, distance, poor connectivity | Better channels, quiet meeting spaces |
| **Semantic** | Jargon, technical terms, language differences | Plain language, translation |
| **Psychological** | Fear of the boss, distrust, prejudice, perception | Open-door policy, psychological safety |
| **Organisational** | Too many levels, rigid hierarchy, information hoarding | Flatter structure, gang plank, town halls |
| **Personal** | Poor listening, ego, unwillingness to communicate | Communication training, feedback culture |

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 4)

- **Structure** = who does what, who reports to whom, how decisions flow.
- **Line and staff** is where HR normally sits — advisory to line managers.
- **Matrix** = dual reporting → violates **unity of command**; main drawback is conflict and appraisal confusion.
- **Tall = narrow span, many levels, many promotions, slow.** **Flat = wide span, few levels, few promotions, fast.**
- **Wide span → fewer levels → flatter.** Narrow span → taller.
- **Graicunas:** relationships = **n(2ⁿ⁄2 + n − 1)** → 4 subordinates = 44 relationships, 6 = 222.
- **Delegation:** responsibility and authority can be delegated; **accountability cannot**.
- **Authority must always equal responsibility.**
- **HR levels:** entry (transactions) → mid (programmes) → senior (strategy).
- **Communication directions:** downward, upward, horizontal, diagonal + informal **grapevine** (single strand, gossip, probability, **cluster** — the commonest).
- **Gang plank** = Fayol's permitted shortcut across the chain of command.
