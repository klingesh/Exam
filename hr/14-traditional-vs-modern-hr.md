# HR — Topic 14: Traditional vs Modern HR Models

Personnel Management vs HRM vs Strategic HR, Administrative vs Strategic Partnership, Transactional vs Transformational, Centralised vs Decentralised, Technology and Data-Driven HR.

---

## 1. The Basic Idea

Over roughly a century, the people function has moved from **keeping records about workers** to **shaping business strategy through people**.

**Everyday analogy:** Traditional HR was the **storekeeper** — issuing what was requested, keeping the register accurate, making sure nothing was stolen. Modern HR is the **business partner** — sitting in the planning meeting and saying *"if we want to enter that market in eighteen months, here is the talent we must start building now."*

> 🎯 **This topic is almost certainly a comparison question.** Prepare it as **tables**, not paragraphs. Tables are faster to write and easier to mark, and this topic is nothing but contrasts.

---

## 2. Personnel Management vs HRM ⭐⭐⭐ (the single most asked comparison)

| Basis | **Personnel Management (Traditional)** | **HRM (Modern)** |
|-------|---------------------------------------|------------------|
| **View of people** | A **cost** to be minimised; a factor of production | An **asset/resource** to be developed; human capital |
| **Nature** | **Routine, administrative**, maintenance | **Strategic, developmental**, value-creating |
| **Approach** | **Reactive** — responds to problems | **Proactive** — anticipates needs |
| **Focus** | Compliance, welfare, record-keeping, discipline | Performance, capability, engagement, culture |
| **Time horizon** | **Short-term**, ad hoc | **Long-term**, planned and integrated |
| **Perspective on relations** | **Pluralist** — collective, union-mediated | **Unitarist** — individual, shared goals |
| **Communication** | **Indirect**, through the union or hierarchy | **Direct** to the employee |
| **Job design** | **Division of labour**, narrow jobs | **Teamwork**, enriched and flexible jobs |
| **Basis of behaviour** | **Rules, norms, custom and practice** | **Values, mission, culture** |
| **Managerial task** | **Monitoring** and controlling | **Nurturing** and enabling |
| **Pay** | Fixed grades, job evaluation, seniority-based | **Performance-related**, flexible, market-linked |
| **Conflict** | Reach a **temporary truce**; institutionalised | Manage the **climate and culture** so it doesn't arise |
| **Training** | Controlled access to specific courses | Continuous **learning organisation** |
| **Selection** | A separate, marginal, clerical task | An **integrated, key strategic** task |
| **Who does it** | The **specialist personnel department** | Largely **integrated into line management**, HR enabling |
| **Speed of decision** | Slow | Fast |
| **Measured by** | Activity — records maintained, cases closed | **Outcomes** — productivity, retention, capability, business results |

> 💡 **The one-sentence summary if you're short of time:** *"Personnel Management administers people as a cost; HRM develops people as an asset."*
>
> 📎 This comparison is largely based on **John Storey's (1992)** framework — naming him adds credibility.

---

## 3. HRM vs Strategic HRM (SHRM) ⭐⭐

HRM was itself superseded by **Strategic HRM** — the difference is **integration with business strategy**.

| Basis | **HRM** | **Strategic HRM (SHRM)** |
|-------|---------|--------------------------|
| Focus | Managing HR **functions** well | Aligning HR with **business strategy** |
| Scope | Functional and operational | **Organisation-wide and strategic** |
| Driver | HR best practice | **Business objectives** |
| Time horizon | Annual/medium term | **Long term (3–5 years)** |
| Responsibility | HR department | HR **and** top management jointly |
| Role of HR head | Functional head | **Strategic partner**, often board-level (**CHRO**) |
| Measure of success | Efficient HR processes | **Business performance** and competitive advantage |
| Example activity | Running an effective appraisal cycle | Redesigning capability and structure to enter a new market |

### The three-stage evolution
```
PERSONNEL MANAGEMENT  →  HRM  →  STRATEGIC HRM  →  (Digital / Data-driven HR)
   admin & welfare       resource     business          technology-enabled,
   compliance            development  strategy          analytics-led
   COST                  ASSET        COMPETITIVE       INSIGHT
                                      ADVANTAGE
```

### Alignment in SHRM
- **Vertical alignment** — HR strategy supports **business** strategy
- **Horizontal alignment** — HR practices reinforce **each other** (no point hiring for collaboration then rewarding only individuals)
*(Introduced in [Topic 3](03-hrm-planning-roles-and-responsibilities.md).)*

## 4. Administrative Focus vs Strategic Partnership ⭐⭐

| Basis | **Administrative focus** | **Strategic partnership** |
|-------|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| Core question | *"Was it processed correctly?"* | *"Does this help the business win?"* |
| Typical work | Payroll, records, leave, letters, statutory returns | Workforce planning, org design, culture, succession, M&A |
| Relationship to business | **Support/service** function | **Partner** at the planning table |
| Positioned as | Cost centre | **Value creator** |
| Reports to | Often Admin or Finance | **CEO/MD**; CHRO on the board |
| Success measured by | Accuracy, timeliness, compliance | Business outcomes, capability, retention of critical talent |
| Skills needed | Process, statutory, administrative | **Business acumen**, analytics, consulting, change management |
| HR's seat | Told the decision after it's made | **In the room while it's made** |

### The Ulrich model — how modern HR is organised
Recall the **three-legged stool** from [Topic 3](03-hrm-planning-roles-and-responsibilities.md):
```
HR BUSINESS PARTNERS  +  CENTRES OF EXPERTISE  +  SHARED SERVICES
(strategy with the       (design policy and       (high-volume
 business unit)           frameworks)               transactions, automated)
```
> 🔑 **The mechanism that makes the shift possible:** you cannot become strategic while drowning in transactions. Modern HR **automates and centralises the administrative layer** (shared services, HRIS, self-service, chatbots) precisely so that HR capacity moves up to the strategic layer. Technology is not a side story here — it is the **enabler** of the whole shift.

**Ulrich's four roles:** Strategic Partner · Change Agent · Administrative Expert · Employee Champion.

## 5. Transactional vs Transformational HR ⭐⭐

| Basis | **Transactional HR** | **Transformational HR** |
|-------|---------------------|------------------------|
| Nature | Routine, repetitive **transactions** | **Change** and capability building |
| Examples | Payroll, leave approval, ID cards, letters, statutory filings, query handling | Culture change, restructuring, leadership development, succession, M&A integration, digital transformation |
| Value added | **Low** per unit (but essential) | **High** |
| Volume | Very high | Low |
| Time horizon | Immediate | Long-term |
| Skills | Process accuracy, compliance | Change management, consulting, influence, analytics |
| Can it be automated? | **Largely yes** — this is the AI/HRIS target | **No** — needs judgement and relationships |
| Cost treatment | Should be minimised | Should be invested in |
| Failure looks like | Errors, delays, complaints | Failed strategy, lost talent, toxic culture |

```
        ▲  TRANSFORMATIONAL   ← low volume, HIGH value
       ╱ ╲ (culture, capability, change)
      ╱   ╲ OPERATIONAL
     ╱     ╲ (recruitment, training, appraisal cycles)
    ╱_______╲ TRANSACTIONAL    ← high volume, LOW value per unit
              (payroll, records, queries)  → AUTOMATE THIS LAYER
```
> 💡 **The strategic argument in one line:** *"Automate the transactional so you can afford the transformational."*

## 6. Centralised vs Decentralised HR ⭐

| Basis | **Centralised HR** | **Decentralised HR** |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Decision authority | At corporate HQ | With units, plants or regions |
| Consistency | **High** — uniform policy everywhere | Lower — local variation |
| Responsiveness to local needs | **Low** | **High** |
| Speed | Slower — approvals travel up | Faster — decided locally |
| Cost | Lower (economies of scale, no duplication) | Higher (duplicated HR teams) |
| Specialist expertise | Concentrated and deep | Thinner, spread out |
| Compliance control | Easier to enforce and audit | Harder — risk of divergence |
| Local relationships | Weaker | **Stronger** — HR knows the people |
| Suits | Single-location firms; standardised operations; heavily regulated sectors | Multi-location, diversified, geographically spread firms; different local labour markets and laws |
| Risk | Policies that don't fit local reality; HR seen as remote | Inconsistency, inequity between units, "policy drift" |

### The hybrid model — what most large organisations actually do
```
CENTRALISED (corporate)                DECENTRALISED (local)
├── Policy framework and standards      ├── Implementation and interpretation
├── Compensation structure & bands      ├── Local recruitment and selection
├── HRIS and data standards             ├── Employee relations and grievances
├── Statutory compliance framework      ├── Local engagement activities
├── Leadership development              ├── Day-to-day manager support
└── Centres of Expertise                └── HR Business Partners on site
```
> 🔑 **The governing principle: "Standardise the framework, localise the execution."** Pay *bands* are set centrally so the organisation stays equitable; the *hiring decision* is made locally by people who know the market and the team. This is exactly the Ulrich model expressed as a centralisation choice.

## 7. The Role of Technology in Modern HR ⭐⭐

| Technology | What it changed |
|-----------|----------------|
| **HRIS / HRMS / HCM** | One source of truth; automated records and workflows |
| **ATS** | Automated recruitment pipeline and candidate tracking |
| **LMS / LXP** | Scalable, trackable, personalised learning |
| **ESS / MSS portals** | Employees and managers self-serve — HR stops being a post office |
| **Cloud HR** | Accessible anywhere; no on-premise infrastructure; continuous updates |
| **Mobile HR apps** | Attendance, leave, payslips, approvals from a phone |
| **People analytics** | Evidence replaces opinion in people decisions |
| **AI and automation** | Screening, chatbots, prediction, personalisation *(see [Topic 13](13-ai-in-hr.md))* |
| **Collaboration tools** | Made hybrid and remote work operationally possible |
| **RPA** | Automates repetitive back-office HR processes |

### What technology delivers
1. **Efficiency** — lower cost and faster cycle times
2. **Accuracy** — fewer manual errors in payroll and records
3. **Employee experience** — instant, self-service, consumer-grade
4. **Data and insight** — the raw material for analytics
5. **Scalability** — the same HR team can support far more people
6. **Compliance** — automated statutory calculation and audit trails
7. **Capacity release** — the enabler of the strategic shift

### What technology does not solve
Bad policy digitised is still bad policy · a manager who won't give feedback won't be fixed by a feedback app · poor data quality produces confident wrong answers · over-automation can make HR feel **inhuman** exactly when empathy is needed · technology adoption fails without **change management and training**

> ⚠️ **The balanced exam point:** technology is an **enabler, not a strategy**. Organisations that buy an HRIS expecting it to transform HR are disappointed; those that redesign the process and then automate it succeed.

## 8. Data-Driven HR / People Analytics ⭐⭐⭐

**People analytics** is the use of data and statistical analysis to make better people decisions — replacing *"I feel that…"* with *"the data shows that…"*.

### Intuition-based vs data-driven HR

| Basis | **Intuition-based (traditional)** | **Data-driven (modern)** |
|-------|----------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Basis of decisions | Experience, gut feel, precedent | **Evidence and analysis** |
| Typical statement | *"Attrition is high because pay is low"* | *"Attrition is 22% in the 2–3 year band in Pune, driven by lack of promotion, not pay"* |
| Credibility with the business | Low — opinion against opinion | **High — speaks the language of business** |
| Ability to predict | None | Forecasting and modelling |
| Accountability | Hard to evaluate | Measurable against outcomes |
| Risk | Bias, anecdote, the loudest voice wins | Poor data quality; over-quantifying human matters |

### The four levels of HR analytics maturity ⭐
| Level | Type | Question | Example |
|-------|------|----------|---------|
| **1** | **Descriptive** (operational reporting) | *What happened?* | Attrition was 15% last year |
| **2** | **Diagnostic** (advanced reporting) | *Why did it happen?* | It concentrated in one location and tenure band, driven by promotion stagnation |
| **3** | **Predictive** | *What will happen?* | These 40 employees have a high probability of resigning next quarter |
| **4** | **Prescriptive** | *What should we do?* | Targeted career conversations and pay correction for this cohort |

> 🎯 **Note this is the same four-level framework used in the Systems/analytics syllabus and in [Topic 2](02-abbreviations-terms-and-metrics.md).** One framework, three places it earns marks. Most HR functions are stuck at **Level 1**.

### What data-driven HR looks like in practice
| Question | Analysis |
|----------|---------|
| Which **hiring source** gives the best performers who stay longest? | Correlate source with 1-year performance and retention |
| Which **managers** lose the most good people? | Attrition of high performers by manager |
| Does our **training** actually change performance? | Pre/post performance of trained vs untrained cohorts (Kirkpatrick Levels 3–4) |
| Is our **pay** equitable? | Regression of pay on role, grade, tenure and performance, testing for gender/location effects |
| Who is at **flight risk**, and why? | Predictive attrition model plus driver analysis |
| What is our **cost of attrition**? | Replacement cost × separations, by role criticality |
| Is our **selection process** valid? | Correlate assessment scores with later job performance *(validity — see [Topic 5](05-recruitment-selection-and-training.md))* |

### Prerequisites — and the usual failure
```
1. Clean, integrated data           ← the foundation
2. Consistent metric definitions    ← "attrition" must mean one thing
3. Analytics skills in the HR team  ← the most commonly missing piece
4. Business questions, not dashboards ← start from the decision, not the data
5. Ethical and privacy guardrails
6. Leadership willing to ACT on findings
```
> ⚠️ **The most common failure:** building beautiful dashboards nobody uses, because they were designed around **available data** rather than around a **decision someone needs to make**. Analytics that changes no decision has no value.

## 9. The Balanced View — what has NOT changed

A complete answer acknowledges the limits of the "modern is better" narrative:

1. **The administrative work still has to be done.** Payroll must be right and on time. Automating it does not make it unimportant — it makes failure more visible.
2. **Compliance has grown, not shrunk.** Data privacy, POSH, the Labour Codes and gig-work regulation have all *added* obligations.
3. **Unions still matter** in manufacturing, transport, public services and the platform economy.
4. **"Strategic partner" can become an excuse** to neglect the employee-champion role. If HR only serves management, employees lose their advocate — and that is a real criticism of modern HRM.
5. **Not every organisation is at the same stage.** A 30-person firm genuinely needs a competent generalist doing administration, not a CHRO with a people-analytics team.
6. **The unitarist assumption is contestable.** HRM assumes employer and employee interests align. Where they genuinely conflict — pay, redundancy, working hours — pretending otherwise is naïve.
7. **Technology can dehumanise.** A chatbot handling a bereavement query is efficiency in the wrong place.

> 🎯 **The best closing line for this topic:** *"Modern HR did not abandon the administrative role — it automated it, so it could add a strategic one. The measure of good HR is not whether it sits in the boardroom, but whether both the business and the employees are better off because it does."*

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 14)

- **Evolution:** Personnel Management → HRM → **Strategic HRM** → digital/data-driven HR. Cost → asset → competitive advantage → insight.
- ⭐ **PM vs HRM:** cost vs **asset** · administrative vs **strategic** · reactive vs **proactive** · short vs **long term** · pluralist/collective vs **unitarist/individual** · indirect vs **direct** communication · rules vs **values** · monitoring vs **nurturing** · fixed grades vs **performance pay** · specialist department vs **integrated with line management**. *(Storey, 1992.)*
- **HRM vs SHRM:** the difference is **alignment with business strategy** and the long-term, board-level horizon. **Vertical** alignment = HR to business; **horizontal** = HR practices to each other.
- **Administrative vs strategic partnership:** processing correctly vs helping the business win; cost centre vs value creator; told the decision vs **in the room**.
- **Ulrich:** HRBP + Centres of Expertise + Shared Services; four roles = Strategic Partner, Change Agent, Administrative Expert, Employee Champion.
- **Transactional** (payroll, records — high volume, low value, **automatable**) vs **Transformational** (culture, capability, change — low volume, high value, **not automatable**). *"Automate the transactional to afford the transformational."*
- **Centralised** = consistent, cheap, controlled but remote. **Decentralised** = responsive, local, faster but inconsistent. Most firms go **hybrid: standardise the framework, localise the execution.**
- **Technology** (HRIS, ATS, LMS, ESS, cloud, mobile, AI, RPA) is the **enabler** of the shift — it releases capacity. But it is not a strategy, and it cannot fix bad policy or bad managers.
- **People analytics maturity:** **Descriptive → Diagnostic → Predictive → Prescriptive.** Most HR functions are stuck at descriptive.
- Analytics needs **clean integrated data, consistent definitions, analytical skills, and a real business question** — dashboards that change no decision are worthless.
- **The balanced view:** administration still matters, compliance has grown, unions still exist, and "strategic partner" must not crowd out **employee champion**.
