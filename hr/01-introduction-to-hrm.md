# HR — Topic 1: Basic Introduction to HRM

Definition, Scope, Evolution, Objectives, Importance, Ethics, and Stakeholders.

---

## 1. What is HRM? (Basic Idea)

**Human Resource Management (HRM)** is the process of **acquiring, developing, motivating and retaining people** in an organisation so that both organisational goals and employee goals are achieved.

**Everyday analogy:** A cricket team owner buys machines? No — he buys **players**. He must scout them (recruitment), pick the best XI (selection), train them (development), pay them (compensation), keep them happy (engagement) and handle dressing-room fights (employee relations). That entire job is HRM.

### Standard definitions worth quoting
- **Edwin Flippo:** "HRM is the planning, organising, directing and controlling of the procurement, development, compensation, integration, maintenance and separation of human resources to the end that individual, organisational and social objectives are accomplished."
- **Michael Armstrong:** "A strategic and coherent approach to the management of an organisation's most valued assets — the people working there."

> 🎯 **Exam tip:** If asked to define HRM, give **one definition + the four verbs (acquire, develop, motivate, retain)**. That structure alone earns most of the marks.

### Why people are called "human resources"
Unlike machines or money, people are the only resource that:
- **Appreciates** with use (skills grow with experience)
- Has **feelings, motivation and free will**
- Can **quit**
- Cannot be **copied** by competitors → source of competitive advantage

## 2. Scope of HRM

The scope is usually shown as **three aspects**:

| Aspect | What it covers |
|--------|----------------|
| **Personnel aspect** | Manpower planning, recruitment, selection, placement, transfer, promotion, training, layoff, retrenchment, remuneration, productivity |
| **Welfare aspect** | Working conditions, canteen, crèche, rest rooms, housing, transport, medical, safety, health, education, recreation |
| **Industrial relations aspect** | Union–management relations, collective bargaining, grievance handling, disciplinary action, settlement of disputes |

### Functions of HRM — the two-bucket model ⭐

```
MANAGERIAL FUNCTIONS               OPERATIVE FUNCTIONS
(what every manager does)          (what HR specifically does)
────────────────────────           ─────────────────────────────
1. Planning                        1. Procurement  (HR planning, recruitment,
2. Organising                         selection, placement, induction)
3. Staffing / Directing            2. Development  (training, career & management
4. Controlling                        development, performance appraisal)
                                   3. Compensation (job evaluation, wages,
                                      incentives, bonus, benefits)
                                   4. Integration  (motivation, morale,
                                      participation, grievance, discipline)
                                   5. Maintenance  (health, safety, welfare,
                                      social security)
                                   6. Separation   (retirement, resignation,
                                      retrenchment, dismissal)
```

> 🎯 **Exam tip:** "Functions of HRM" is a guaranteed question. Memorise the operative functions as **P-D-C-I-M-S** → **P**rocurement, **D**evelopment, **C**ompensation, **I**ntegration, **M**aintenance, **S**eparation.

## 3. Evolution of HRM ⭐⭐ (Personnel Management → HRM → Strategic HRM)

This is one of the **most asked** questions in the topic. Learn the direction of travel.

| Stage | Period (approx.) | Focus |
|-------|------------------|-------|
| **Industrial Revolution** | 1800s | Factory system; labour treated as a commodity |
| **Scientific Management** (F. W. Taylor) | 1900s–1920s | Efficiency, time-and-motion study, "one best way" |
| **Human Relations / Hawthorne Studies** (Elton Mayo) | 1920s–1930s | Discovery that **social and psychological factors** raise productivity, not just pay |
| **Behavioural Science era** | 1950s–1960s | Motivation, leadership, group behaviour (Maslow, Herzberg, McGregor) |
| **Personnel Management** | 1960s–1970s | Record-keeping, welfare, compliance, administration |
| **HRM** | 1980s | People as **resources**; integration of HR functions with business |
| **Strategic HRM (SHRM)** | 1990s onwards | HR as a **strategic business partner**; HR strategy aligned to business strategy |
| **Digital / Analytics-driven HR** | 2010s onwards | HRIS, people analytics, AI-enabled HR |

### The Hawthorne Studies — know this one by name
Conducted at the **Hawthorne plant of Western Electric** (Chicago) by **Elton Mayo**. Researchers changed lighting and other conditions expecting productivity to follow. Instead productivity rose **whenever workers felt observed and cared about** — proving that **attention, group norms and morale** matter as much as physical conditions. This gave birth to the **Human Relations movement**.

> 📎 The full **Personnel Management vs HRM vs SHRM** comparison table is in [Topic 14](14-traditional-vs-modern-hr.md) — that's where it's most likely to be examined.

## 4. Goals and Objectives of HRM

### The four classic objectives
| Objective | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Societal** | Be ethically and socially responsible to society; comply with law |
| **Organisational** | Help the organisation achieve its goals; HR exists to serve the business |
| **Functional** | Keep the HR department's contribution appropriate to the organisation's needs (neither over- nor under-staffed) |
| **Personal** | Help employees achieve their personal goals so they stay motivated and retained |

### Specific objectives
1. Get the **right people in the right job at the right time**
2. **Develop** employee skills and potential
3. Provide **fair and equitable compensation**
4. Maintain **high morale and good human relations**
5. Ensure **legal compliance**
6. Achieve **optimum utilisation** of human resources
7. Build an **employer brand** that attracts talent

## 5. Importance of HRM in Organisational Success

1. **People are the only sustainable competitive advantage** — technology and capital can be bought; a skilled, committed workforce cannot be copied quickly.
2. **Right hiring reduces cost** — a bad hire costs several months of salary in lost productivity plus re-hiring cost.
3. **Retention protects investment** — training a person and then losing them transfers your investment to a competitor.
4. **Productivity and quality** depend on motivation, not just process.
5. **Legal protection** — sound HR practice avoids litigation, penalties and reputational damage.
6. **Change management** — mergers, restructuring and digital transformation succeed or fail on how people are handled.
7. **Employer brand** — good HR makes hiring cheaper and easier.
8. **Culture** — HR shapes "how things are done here", which drives everything else.

> 💡 **A strong exam line:** *"HRM converts human resources into human capital — a cost centre into a value creator."*

## 6. Ethical Considerations in HRM

Ethics in HR = doing what is **fair and right**, not merely what is legal.

### Key ethical issues
| Area | Ethical concern |
|------|-----------------|
| **Recruitment & selection** | Discrimination on gender, caste, religion, age, disability; nepotism/favouritism; misleading job adverts |
| **Compensation** | Pay inequity; gender pay gap; secrecy used to hide unfairness |
| **Performance appraisal** | Biased ratings; appraisals used to settle personal scores |
| **Privacy** | Misuse of employee data, surveillance, monitoring of personal communication |
| **Safety** | Exposing workers to hazards to save cost |
| **Layoffs** | Handling retrenchment without dignity, notice or support |
| **Harassment** | Failure to act on complaints; protecting senior offenders |
| **Whistle-blowing** | Retaliation against employees who report wrongdoing |

### Principles that guide ethical HR
- **Fairness / equity** — same rules for everyone
- **Transparency** — decisions explainable
- **Confidentiality** — employee information protected
- **Dignity and respect** — for every employee at every level
- **Legal compliance** as the floor, not the ceiling

## 7. The HR Department's Role in the Organisation

HR plays **four roles** simultaneously (based on **Dave Ulrich's model** — worth naming):

| Role | Focus | Example activity |
|------|-------|------------------|
| **Strategic Partner** | Future + process | Aligning workforce plan with business strategy |
| **Change Agent** | Future + people | Managing culture change during a merger |
| **Administrative Expert** | Day-to-day + process | Payroll, records, compliance, efficiency |
| **Employee Champion** | Day-to-day + people | Listening to employees, engagement, grievance support |

### HR as a **line** vs **staff** function
- HR is primarily a **staff (advisory) function** — it advises and supports line managers.
- HR has **line authority** only inside its own department.
- **Functional authority:** HR can insist that line managers follow HR policy (e.g. no hiring without an approved requisition).

> 🎯 **Common exam question:** *"Is HR a line or staff function?"* → **Staff/advisory to the organisation, line within its own department, with functional authority over HR policy.**

## 8. Stakeholders in HRM

A **stakeholder** is anyone affected by, or able to affect, HR decisions.

### Internal stakeholders
| Stakeholder | What they want from HR |
|-------------|------------------------|
| **Employees** | Fair pay, growth, safety, respect, job security |
| **Line managers** | Right people, on time, performing; support on discipline |
| **Top management** | HR that delivers business results at controlled cost |
| **Trade unions** | Protection of members' interests, fair bargaining |
| **Shareholders** | Productivity and profitability from the workforce |

### External stakeholders
| Stakeholder | Interest |
|-------------|----------|
| **Government / regulators** | Compliance with labour law |
| **Society / community** | Ethical employment, local jobs, CSR |
| **Customers** | Service quality delivered by employees |
| **Job seekers** | Fair, transparent recruitment |
| **Educational institutions** | Placement of students, internships |
| **Competitors** | Talent market and poaching |

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 1)

- **HRM** = acquiring, developing, motivating and retaining people to meet organisational **and** employee goals.
- **Scope** = three aspects: **personnel, welfare, industrial relations**.
- **Operative functions** = **P-D-C-I-M-S** (Procurement, Development, Compensation, Integration, Maintenance, Separation).
- **Evolution** = Scientific Management → Human Relations (**Hawthorne, Elton Mayo**) → Behavioural → **Personnel Management → HRM → Strategic HRM** → digital/analytics HR.
- **Four objectives** = societal, organisational, functional, personal.
- **Ulrich's four roles** = Strategic Partner, Change Agent, Administrative Expert, Employee Champion.
- HR is a **staff/advisory** function organisation-wide, **line** within itself, with **functional authority** on policy.
- **Stakeholders** = employees, managers, top management, unions, shareholders (internal); government, society, customers, job seekers (external).
- Best one-liner: *"People are the only resource that appreciates with use and cannot be copied."*
