# HR — Topic 3: HRM Planning, Roles & Responsibilities

Strategic HR Planning, Workforce Forecasting, Job Analysis & Design, HR Roles and HR Department Structure.

---

## 1. What is Human Resource Planning? (Basic Idea)

**Human Resource Planning (HRP)** — also called manpower planning — is the process of forecasting **how many** people and **what kind** of people the organisation will need in future, and ensuring they are available at the right time.

**Everyday analogy:** A restaurant expecting a wedding booking for 300 guests next month doesn't wait until the morning of the event to look for cooks and waiters. It works out how many hands it needs, checks who it already has, and hires the difference in advance. That is HR planning.

> **The one-line definition:** HRP = getting the **right number** of the **right people** with the **right skills** in the **right place** at the **right time** and at the **right cost**.

### Objectives of HRP
1. Forecast future **personnel needs**
2. Avoid both **overstaffing** (excess cost) and **understaffing** (lost business)
3. Cope with **change** — technology, expansion, diversification
4. Provide a base for **recruitment, training and succession**
5. Control **labour cost**
6. Identify **skill gaps** early

## 2. Strategic HR Planning — Aligning HR with Organisational Goals ⭐⭐

**Strategic HR Planning** links HR activities directly to business strategy, so that HR delivers what the business plan actually requires.

```
BUSINESS STRATEGY                     HR STRATEGY MUST SUPPLY
─────────────────                     ───────────────────────
"Grow 30% in 2 years"          →      Aggressive hiring, faster onboarding
"Cut cost by 15%"              →      Restructuring, redeployment, automation
"Enter a new country"          →      Local hiring, expatriate policy, compliance
"Become digital-first"         →      Reskilling, hiring data/tech talent
"Compete on service quality"   →      Selective hiring, heavy training, retention focus
```

### Vertical vs horizontal alignment (worth a mark)
- **Vertical alignment** — HR strategy matches **business** strategy.
- **Horizontal alignment** — HR practices match **each other** (no point hiring for teamwork then rewarding only individual performance).

### The strategic HRP process
```
1. Analyse organisational objectives and business plan
2. Forecast HR DEMAND        (how many/what skills will we need?)
3. Forecast HR SUPPLY        (who will we have, internally + externally?)
4. Identify the GAP          (demand − supply)
5. Formulate ACTION PLANS    (recruit / train / redeploy / retrench)
6. Implement
7. Monitor, review and control
```

## 3. Workforce Planning and Forecasting ⭐⭐⭐

### 3.1 Forecasting DEMAND — techniques

| Technique | How it works |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Managerial judgement** | Managers estimate their own future needs (bottom-up) or top management estimates for all (top-down) |
| **Ratio / Trend analysis** | Uses a historical ratio between a business driver and headcount |
| **Work-study / Work-load analysis** | Calculates people needed from output target ÷ output per person |
| **Delphi technique** | Repeated rounds of anonymous expert opinion until consensus |
| **Regression analysis** | Statistically relates headcount to sales, output, etc. |
| **Nominal group technique** | Structured face-to-face expert discussion and ranking |

**Worked example — ratio/trend analysis:**
Historically the company needs **1 salesperson for every ₹50 lakh of sales**. Next year's sales target is **₹100 crore**.
```
Step 1: Convert to common units → ₹100 crore = ₹10,000 lakh
Step 2: Salespeople needed = 10,000 ÷ 50
Step 3:                    = 200 salespeople
```
✅ **Demand = 200 salespeople**

**Worked example — work-load analysis:**
Annual production target = 1,20,000 units. One worker produces 500 units a year.
```
Workers needed = 1,20,000 ÷ 500 = 240 workers
```

### 3.2 Forecasting SUPPLY — techniques

| Technique | What it shows |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Skills inventory** | Database of existing employees' qualifications, skills, experience |
| **Replacement chart** | Who is ready to replace whom, right now |
| **Succession planning** | Longer-term pipeline for key positions |
| **Markov / transition analysis** | Probability of employees moving between roles or leaving |
| **Attrition analysis** | Expected losses based on past turnover |

### 3.3 The demand–supply gap ⭐ (the classic numerical)

> **Net HR requirement = Forecast demand − Projected internal supply**
>
> **Projected internal supply = Current headcount − Expected separations + Expected internal additions**

**Worked example:**
A company currently has **500** employees. Next year's plan requires **600**. Expected attrition is **15%**.

```
Step 1: Forecast demand                = 600
Step 2: Expected separations            = 15% of 500 = 75
Step 3: Projected internal supply       = 500 − 75 = 425
Step 4: Net requirement (to recruit)    = 600 − 425
Step 5:                                 = 175 employees
```
✅ **The company must recruit 175 people.**

> 🎯 **Exam tip:** the trap is forgetting attrition. Answering "600 − 500 = 100" is the most common mistake. Always subtract expected separations.

### 3.4 Action plans once the gap is known
| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| **Shortage** | Recruitment, overtime, subcontracting, promotion from within, retraining, delayed retirement |
| **Surplus** | Redeployment, reduced hours, hiring freeze, VRS (voluntary retirement), layoff, retrenchment |

## 4. Job Analysis ⭐⭐⭐

**Job Analysis** is the systematic process of collecting information about **what a job involves** — its duties, responsibilities, working conditions and the human requirements to perform it.

> 🔑 **Job analysis is the foundation of almost every other HR activity.** Recruitment, selection, training, appraisal, job evaluation and pay all depend on it. If asked "why is job analysis important?", say: *"It is the base document for the whole HR system."*

### The two outputs ⭐ (never confuse these)

| | **Job Description (JD)** | **Job Specification (JS)** |
|---|---|---|
| About | The **JOB** | The **PERSON** |
| Contains | Job title, duties, responsibilities, reporting relationships, working conditions, tools used | Qualifications, experience, skills, knowledge, physical and personal attributes |
| Question it answers | *What is to be done?* | *Who can do it?* |
| Used for | Job evaluation, appraisal, job posting | Screening and selecting candidates |

### Methods of collecting job analysis data
| Method | Note |
|--------|------|
| **Observation** | Watch the worker. Good for manual, repetitive, observable jobs; poor for knowledge work |
| **Interview** | Ask the jobholder and supervisor. Most common; risk of exaggeration |
| **Questionnaire** | Cheap and quick for large numbers; risk of misunderstanding |
| **Diary / log method** | Employee records activities over time. Accurate but burdensome |
| **Critical Incident Technique** | Collects examples of especially effective/ineffective behaviour |
| **Technical conference** | Panel of experts describes the job |
| **PAQ** (Position Analysis Questionnaire) | Standardised, structured questionnaire — 194 items |
| **Functional Job Analysis (FJA)** | Rates the job on data, people and things |

### Uses of job analysis
HR planning · recruitment · selection · training needs · performance appraisal · job evaluation and pay · career planning · safety · legal defence of hiring decisions · organisational design

## 5. Job Design

**Job Design** decides the **content, methods and relationships** of a job to satisfy both organisational requirements and the jobholder's needs.

### The three classic techniques ⭐

| Technique | What changes | Direction |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Job Rotation** | Employee moves **between** different jobs | Sideways movement |
| **Job Enlargement** | **More tasks** added at the **same** level of responsibility | **Horizontal** expansion |
| **Job Enrichment** | **More authority, autonomy and responsibility** added | **Vertical** expansion |

> 🎯 **Exam trap:** Enlargement = *more work of the same kind*. Enrichment = *more control over the work*. Enlargement can even reduce satisfaction (more of the same drudgery); enrichment usually raises it.

Also know: **Job Simplification** (breaking a job into simple repetitive tasks — raises efficiency, lowers satisfaction).

### Hackman & Oldham's Job Characteristics Model ⭐

Five core job dimensions produce three psychological states, which produce outcomes:

```
CORE DIMENSIONS              PSYCHOLOGICAL STATES        OUTCOMES
1. Skill variety      ┐
2. Task identity      ├──→   Meaningfulness of work  ┐
3. Task significance  ┘                              ├──→ High motivation
4. Autonomy           ──→    Responsibility for      │    High satisfaction
                             outcomes                │    High quality
5. Feedback           ──→    Knowledge of results    ┘    Low absenteeism
```

> **Motivating Potential Score (MPS)**
> **MPS = [(Skill variety + Task identity + Task significance) ÷ 3] × Autonomy × Feedback**

Note the multiplication: if **autonomy** or **feedback** is zero, the whole MPS collapses to zero — however varied the tasks are.

## 6. HR Roles ⭐⭐

| Role | Level | Typical responsibility |
|------|-------|------------------------|
| **HR Executive / Assistant** | Entry | Records, data entry, documentation, coordination |
| **Recruiter / Talent Acquisition Specialist** | Entry–Mid | Sourcing, screening, scheduling, offer roll-out, closing positions |
| **HR Generalist** | Mid | Handles **all** HR functions for a unit — a broad, "jack of all trades" role |
| **HR Specialist** | Mid | **Deep expertise in one area** — C&B, L&D, IR, HR analytics |
| **HR Business Partner (HRBP)** | Mid–Senior | Embedded with a business unit; links HR to that unit's strategy |
| **HR Manager** | Senior | Runs the HR function for a location/unit; leads a team; policy implementation |
| **HR Head / VP of HR** | Senior | Owns HR strategy for a large division or the company; budget owner |
| **CHRO** (Chief Human Resources Officer) | Top | Board-level; HR strategy for the whole enterprise; culture and leadership |

### Generalist vs Specialist ⭐ (common exam question)

| Basis | HR Generalist | HR Specialist |
|-------|--------------|---------------|
| Scope | Broad — all HR areas | Narrow — one area |
| Depth | Moderate | Deep |
| Typical in | Small and mid-sized firms | Large firms with CoEs |
| Career path | Towards HR Manager/HRBP | Towards Head of that function |
| Example | Handles hiring, payroll, grievances, training | Designs the entire compensation structure |

## 7. HR Responsibilities — the three levels ⭐⭐

This split appears constantly. Learn it as a pyramid.

| Level | Nature | Time horizon | Examples |
|-------|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Administrative** | **Transactional**, routine, compliance | Daily | Payroll, attendance, records, statutory returns, letters, ID cards |
| **Operational** | Running HR **programmes** | Monthly–yearly | Recruitment, training delivery, appraisal cycle, grievance handling, engagement activities |
| **Strategic** | **Transformational**, business-linked | 1–5 years | Workforce planning, organisation design, culture change, succession, M&A integration, HR analytics |

```
            ▲  STRATEGIC        ← smallest volume, highest value
           ╱ ╲ (business partner)
          ╱   ╲ OPERATIONAL
         ╱     ╲ (programmes)
        ╱_______╲ ADMINISTRATIVE  ← largest volume, lowest value-add
                  (transactions)
```

> 💡 **The modern HR argument:** automate and outsource the administrative layer (via HRIS/shared services) so HR time shifts upward to the strategic layer. This is exactly the theme of [Topic 14](14-traditional-vs-modern-hr.md).

## 8. HR Department Structure and Organisation

### How HR departments are usually organised
| Structure | Description | Suits |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| **Single HR person / small team** | One generalist does everything | Start-ups, small firms |
| **Functional structure** | Separate sub-teams for recruitment, L&D, C&B, IR | Medium–large firms |
| **Geographic / unit-based** | An HR team per plant, region or office | Multi-location firms |
| **Ulrich three-legged model** ⭐ | See below | Large organisations |

### The Ulrich three-legged model ⭐
```
┌────────────────────┬─────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐
│ HR BUSINESS        │ CENTRES OF          │ HR SHARED            │
│ PARTNERS (HRBP)    │ EXPERTISE (CoE)     │ SERVICES (HRSS)      │
├────────────────────┼─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┤
│ Sit with the       │ Deep specialists    │ High-volume           │
│ business, translate│ who design policy   │ transactions handled  │
│ strategy into HR   │ and frameworks      │ centrally/automated   │
│                    │ (C&B, L&D, TA)      │ (payroll, queries)    │
└────────────────────┴─────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘
```

### Where HR sits in the organisation
- Reports ideally to the **CEO/MD**, not to Finance or Admin — a signal that people are strategic.
- The **CHRO** on the board indicates the highest level of HR maturity.

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 3)

- **HRP** = right number, right skills, right place, right time, right cost.
- **HRP process:** objectives → forecast demand → forecast supply → gap → action plan → implement → review.
- **Net requirement = Demand − (Current headcount − Separations).** Never forget attrition.
- **Demand techniques:** managerial judgement, ratio/trend, work-load, Delphi, regression.
- **Supply techniques:** skills inventory, replacement chart, succession planning, Markov analysis.
- **Job analysis** is the **foundation of all HR activities** → produces **JD (the job)** and **JS (the person)**.
- **Enlargement = horizontal** (more tasks). **Enrichment = vertical** (more authority). **Rotation = sideways**.
- **Hackman & Oldham:** skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, feedback → **MPS = [(SV+TI+TS)/3] × Autonomy × Feedback**.
- **Generalist = broad; Specialist = deep.**
- **Three responsibility levels:** Administrative (transactional) → Operational (programmes) → Strategic (transformational).
- **Ulrich model:** HRBP + Centres of Expertise + Shared Services.
