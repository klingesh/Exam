# HR — Topic 10: Dispute Resolution

Types of Workplace Disputes, ADR Methods, Negotiation & Conflict Management Skills, Legal Considerations and Building a Resolution Culture.

---

## 1. What is a Workplace Dispute? (Basic Idea)

A **dispute** is a **disagreement between parties** at work that has escalated beyond informal resolution — between two employees, between employees and management, or between a union and management.

**Everyday analogy:** Conflict is friction; a dispute is friction that has caught fire. A little friction is normal and even useful. Fire needs a procedure.

### Conflict vs Dispute
- **Conflict** = the underlying disagreement or tension (may be unexpressed)
- **Dispute** = conflict that has been **formally raised** and requires a resolution process

### The three views of conflict ⭐

| View | Belief | Implication |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Traditional / unitary** | All conflict is bad and must be eliminated | Suppress it |
| **Human relations** | Conflict is natural and inevitable in any organisation | Accept and manage it |
| **Interactionist / modern** | Some conflict is **necessary** for performance | Maintain a *healthy minimum*; resolve destructive conflict |

### Functional vs Dysfunctional conflict ⭐

| **Functional (constructive)** | **Dysfunctional (destructive)** |
|---|---|
| About **ideas, tasks and methods** | About **personalities and emotions** |
| Improves decision quality; surfaces assumptions | Wastes energy; damages relationships |
| Encourages innovation, prevents groupthink | Creates stress, absenteeism, attrition |
| Focused on the problem | Focused on the person |
| **Should be encouraged** within limits | **Must be resolved** |

> 🎯 **A strong exam line:** *"The aim of conflict management is not zero conflict — it is zero destructive conflict. An organisation with no disagreement has no debate, and an organisation with no debate makes poor decisions."*

## 2. Types of Workplace Disputes ⭐⭐

### 2.1 By level (the syllabus split: interpersonal vs organisational)

| Level | Between | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Intrapersonal** | Within one person | Role conflict; an ethical dilemma; work–life tension |
| **Interpersonal** | Two individuals | Two colleagues clashing; employee vs supervisor |
| **Intragroup** | Within one team | Team members disputing task allocation |
| **Intergroup** | Between departments | Sales vs Production over delivery commitments |
| **Organisational** | Employees/union vs management, or structural | Wage revision dispute; restructuring; policy change |
| **Inter-organisational** | Between organisations | Principal employer vs contractor over labour |

### 2.2 By parties
- **Individual dispute** — one employee vs the employer (e.g. wrongful termination)
- **Collective dispute** — a group or union vs the employer (e.g. wage settlement)

### 2.3 By subject ⭐ (an important legal distinction)
| Type | Nature | Resolved by |
|------|--------|------------|
| **Rights dispute** | About the **interpretation or breach** of an existing right — law, contract or settlement | **Adjudication** (courts/tribunals) — someone must rule on what the right means |
| **Interest dispute** | About **creating new terms** — a wage increase, new benefits | **Negotiation / collective bargaining** — there is nothing to interpret, only to agree |

> 🔑 **Why this matters:** you cannot negotiate a rights dispute (the right already exists — either it was breached or it wasn't), and you cannot adjudicate an interest dispute (no law says what next year's bonus must be). Matching the dispute type to the right method is the whole art.

### 2.4 Common causes of workplace disputes
Wages and benefits · workload and staffing · working conditions and safety · disciplinary action and dismissal · transfer and promotion decisions · discrimination and harassment · union recognition · retrenchment, layoff and closure · change in technology or work practice · breach of an earlier settlement · poor communication and unmet expectations · scarce resources and unclear responsibility

## 3. Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) ⭐⭐⭐

**ADR** covers all methods of settling disputes **outside formal litigation** — faster, cheaper, more private and more relationship-preserving.

### The escalation ladder
```
LEAST formal, parties keep MOST control
│
├── 1. NEGOTIATION      Parties talk directly. No third party.
│
├── 2. CONCILIATION     A third party meets the sides (often separately),
│                       persuades and suggests. Non-binding.
│
├── 3. MEDIATION        A neutral third party FACILITATES the parties in
│                       reaching THEIR OWN agreement. Non-binding.
│
├── 4. ARBITRATION      A neutral third party HEARS both sides and gives
│                       a DECISION (award). Usually BINDING.
│
└── 5. ADJUDICATION /   A court or tribunal decides. Binding, enforceable,
       LITIGATION       public, appealable.
│
MOST formal, third party takes control
```

### The comparison table ⭐⭐

| Basis | **Negotiation** | **Conciliation** | **Mediation** | **Arbitration** | **Adjudication** |
|-------|----------------|-----------------|--------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Third party? | No | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes (judicial) |
| Third party's role | — | Persuades, suggests terms | **Facilitates** the parties' own solution | **Decides** the outcome | **Judges** on law |
| Who decides | The parties | The parties | The parties | **The arbitrator** | The court/tribunal |
| Binding? | If agreed | Not binding | Not binding | **Binding** (award) | **Binding**, enforceable |
| Formality | Lowest | Low | Low–moderate | Moderate — quasi-judicial | Highest |
| Cost & time | Lowest | Low | Low | Moderate | **Highest** |
| Confidential? | Yes | Yes | Yes | Usually | **No — public record** |
| Relationship | Preserved | Preserved | **Best preserved** | Strained | Often destroyed |
| Best for | Interest disputes | Industrial disputes (statutory stage) | Interpersonal conflict | Technical/contractual disputes | Rights disputes, precedent needed |

### Mediation vs Arbitration ⭐⭐⭐ — the single most asked comparison

| Basis | **Mediation** | **Arbitration** |
|-------|--------------|----------------|
| Third party's function | **Facilitator** — helps them agree | **Adjudicator** — imposes a decision |
| Who controls the outcome | **The parties** | **The arbitrator** |
| Outcome | A mutually agreed settlement | An **award** |
| Binding | **No** (unless they sign an agreement) | **Yes** |
| Evidence & procedure | Informal discussion | Formal submissions, evidence, hearings |
| Win/lose | **Win–win** sought | Usually **win–lose** |
| Relationship after | Usually intact | Usually damaged |
| Analogy | A **counsellor** helping a couple talk | A **judge** deciding who is right |

> 🎯 **The one-line answer:** *"A mediator helps you reach your own agreement; an arbitrator makes the decision for you."*

**Voluntary vs Compulsory arbitration:** voluntary = both parties agree to refer and to be bound (Section 10A of the Industrial Disputes Act). Compulsory = the government refers the dispute without the parties' consent.

## 4. Conflict Management Styles ⭐⭐⭐

The **Thomas–Kilmann** model places five styles on two axes: **assertiveness** (concern for own goals) and **cooperativeness** (concern for others' goals).

```
        High │  COMPETING              COLLABORATING
             │  (I win, you lose)      (we both win)
ASSERTIVE-   │            ╲           ╱
NESS         │             COMPROMISING
(concern     │            ╱  (we both give up something)
 for self)   │           ╱             ╲
        Low  │  AVOIDING               ACCOMMODATING
             │  (both lose)            (I lose, you win)
             └──────────────────────────────────────────
                Low        COOPERATIVENESS        High
                        (concern for others)
```

| Style | Description | Use it when | Danger |
|-------|-------------|------------|--------|
| **Competing** (forcing) | Pursue own goal at the other's expense — win/lose | Emergency; safety issue; unpopular but necessary decision; a quick decision is vital | Breeds resentment; suppresses information |
| **Collaborating** (problem-solving) | Work jointly for a solution satisfying both — **win/win** | Issue is important to both; need commitment and a lasting solution; time available | **Time-consuming**; wasteful for trivial issues |
| **Compromising** | Each side concedes something — partial satisfaction | Equal power; moderate stakes; a temporary fix; deadlock | Nobody is fully satisfied; can become a lazy default |
| **Avoiding** | Withdraw or postpone — lose/lose | Issue is trivial; emotions need to cool; someone else can resolve it better | Problems fester and grow |
| **Accommodating** (smoothing) | Yield to the other — lose/win | You are wrong; the issue matters far more to them; preserving the relationship is paramount | Your interests get ignored; encourages exploitation |

> 🔑 **The exam trap:** there is **no single best style.** Collaborating produces the best outcomes but costs the most time — using it on a trivial matter is a management failure. The skilled manager **matches the style to the situation**. Say this and you have answered the question properly.

## 5. Negotiation Skills ⭐⭐

### Two approaches

| | **Distributive** (positional) | **Integrative** (principled) |
|---|---|---|
| Assumption | **Fixed pie** — my gain is your loss | Pie can be **expanded** |
| Goal | Claim the largest share | Create mutual value |
| Information | Concealed; used tactically | Shared to find joint gains |
| Relationship | Secondary | Important; preserved |
| Outcome | Win–lose | **Win–win** |
| Example | Haggling over one salary figure | Trading a lower salary for equity, flexibility and a training budget |

### Key negotiation concepts ⭐

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **BATNA** | **Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement** — what you will do if no deal is reached. Your real source of power |
| **Reservation point** | Your walk-away limit — the worst deal you would still accept |
| **ZOPA** | **Zone Of Possible Agreement** — the overlap between both sides' acceptable ranges. **No overlap = no deal is possible** |
| **Anchoring** | The first offer disproportionately shapes the final outcome |
| **Concession** | A planned give, ideally traded for something in return |
| **Bargaining power** | Determined largely by the **strength of your BATNA**, not by volume or aggression |

> 🔑 **The most useful practical insight:** the party with the **stronger BATNA** wins the negotiation. Preparing your alternatives beforehand matters more than any technique used at the table. An employee with another offer in hand negotiates differently from one without.

### Stages of a negotiation
```
1. PREPARATION      Know your interests, your BATNA, your reservation point,
                    and research the other side's position and constraints.
2. OPENING          Set the tone; exchange opening positions (anchoring).
3. EXPLORATION      Probe INTERESTS behind the stated POSITIONS. Ask "why?"
4. BARGAINING       Trade concessions; package options rather than fight item by item.
5. AGREEMENT        Confirm terms precisely; check both sides understand the same thing.
6. IMPLEMENTATION   Record in writing; follow through; review.
```

> 💡 **Positions vs interests** — the core of principled negotiation. A union's *position* may be "15% wage rise"; its *interest* may be "protect members against inflation and be seen to have won something." Negotiating positions creates deadlock; negotiating interests creates options.

### Collective bargaining
The process where **union representatives and management negotiate** terms and conditions of employment, resulting in a **Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA)** or settlement.

| Type | Focus |
|------|-------|
| **Distributive bargaining** | Dividing a fixed gain — wages, bonus |
| **Integrative bargaining** | Joint problem-solving — productivity, safety, training |
| **Attitudinal structuring** | Reshaping the relationship and trust between the parties |
| **Intra-organisational bargaining** | Building consensus *within* each side before facing the other |

*(Union recognition and collective bargaining law are covered in [Topic 11](11-labour-law-and-legal-requirements.md).)*

## 6. Legal Considerations in Dispute Resolution ⭐⭐

### Statutory dispute-settlement machinery in India
Under the **Industrial Disputes Act, 1947** — now consolidated into the **Industrial Relations Code, 2020**:

| Body | Section (ID Act) | Function |
|------|-----------------|----------|
| **Works Committee** | 3 | Compulsory where **100+ workmen**; joint employer–worker body to promote good relations and prevent disputes |
| **Grievance Redressal Committee** | 9C | Compulsory where **20+ workers**; handles individual grievances *(see [Topic 9](09-grievance-handling.md))* |
| **Conciliation Officer** | 4 | Government officer who mediates and promotes settlement |
| **Board of Conciliation** | 5 | Larger, tripartite conciliation body for significant disputes |
| **Court of Inquiry** | 6 | Investigates and reports on matters connected with a dispute (does not adjudicate) |
| **Labour Court** | 7 | Adjudicates dismissal, discharge, standing orders, illegal strikes |
| **Industrial Tribunal** | 7A | Adjudicates wider matters — wages, bonus, retrenchment, closure |
| **National Tribunal** | 7B | Disputes of national importance or affecting establishments in more than one state |
| **Voluntary Arbitration** | 10A | Parties agree in writing to refer the dispute to an arbitrator |

> ⚠️ **Under the Industrial Relations Code, 2020**, the Labour Court and Industrial Tribunal are consolidated into a single **Industrial Tribunal**, with a **National Industrial Tribunal** above it. Mention this if the exam asks about recent reform.

### Strikes and lockouts — the legal position

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Strike** | Concerted stoppage of work by workers |
| **Lockout** | Employer closes the workplace / suspends work to pressure workers |
| **Layoff** | Employer temporarily unable to provide work (not a punishment) |
| **Retrenchment** | Permanent termination for reasons other than misconduct — i.e. redundancy |
| **Lock-in / gherao / go-slow** | Coercive tactics; generally unlawful |

**Key legal requirements (India):**
- In a **public utility service**, **14 days' notice** of a strike or lockout is required. The **IR Code, 2020 extends the 14-day notice requirement to all industrial establishments.**
- A strike or lockout is **illegal** during conciliation, arbitration or adjudication proceedings and for a specified period afterwards.
- **Retrenchment compensation** is generally **15 days' average pay for every completed year** of continuous service, with notice and government notification requirements.
- Establishments above a specified size (historically **100 workers**, raised to **300** under the IR Code for many states) need **prior government permission** for layoff, retrenchment or closure.

### Principles HR must respect in any dispute
1. **Natural justice** — hear both sides; nobody judges their own case
2. **Due process** — show-cause notice, charge sheet, domestic enquiry, then action
3. **Proportionality** — the punishment must fit the misconduct
4. **Consistency** — like cases treated alike (inconsistency itself becomes evidence of unfairness)
5. **No victimisation** for raising a dispute or union activity
6. **Documentation** at every stage
7. **Statutory compliance** — notices, timelines and permissions

> ⚠️ Thresholds, notice periods and compensation rates are amended periodically and vary by state. In an exam, quote them as *"generally"* or *"currently around"*.

## 7. Building a Culture of Conflict Resolution ⭐

| Element | What it means in practice |
|---------|--------------------------|
| **Psychological safety** | People can disagree openly without fear of punishment |
| **Early intervention** | Address friction while it is small; don't wait for a formal complaint |
| **Clear, known channels** | Everyone knows where to take a problem |
| **Trained managers** | Front-line managers can handle conflict rather than escalate or ignore it |
| **Focus on interests, not positions** | Ask "why do you want that?" instead of arguing over demands |
| **Separate the person from the problem** | Attack the issue, not each other |
| **Consistency and fairness** | Outcomes are predictable and even-handed |
| **Joint forums** | Works committees and consultation surface issues before they harden |
| **No-retaliation guarantee**, enforced | Protection is meaningless unless breaches are punished |
| **Learn from patterns** | Recurring disputes signal a system fault, not a people fault |
| **Leaders model it** | Senior people who handle disagreement well set the norm |

> 🎯 **The closing line for this topic:** *"You cannot eliminate conflict from an organisation of human beings — and you should not want to. The goal is an organisation where conflict surfaces early, is discussed openly, and is resolved at the lowest possible level."*

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 10)

- **Conflict** = the disagreement; **dispute** = conflict formally raised.
- **Interactionist view:** some conflict is necessary. **Functional** conflict is about tasks and ideas; **dysfunctional** is about personalities.
- Levels: intrapersonal → interpersonal → intragroup → intergroup → organisational.
- **Rights disputes** (breach of an existing right) → **adjudication**. **Interest disputes** (new terms) → **negotiation**.
- **ADR ladder:** Negotiation → Conciliation → **Mediation** → **Arbitration** → Adjudication. Formality and third-party control rise as you go down.
- ⭐ **Mediation = facilitator, non-binding, parties decide. Arbitration = adjudicator, binding, arbitrator decides.**
- **Thomas–Kilmann five styles:** Competing (win–lose), Collaborating (**win–win**), Compromising, Avoiding (lose–lose), Accommodating (lose–win). **No style is universally best — match it to the situation.**
- **Negotiation:** distributive (fixed pie) vs integrative (expand the pie). Know **BATNA**, **ZOPA**, reservation point, anchoring.
- **The stronger BATNA wins.** Negotiate **interests**, not positions.
- **ID Act machinery:** Works Committee (100+), Grievance Redressal Committee (20+), Conciliation Officer, Board of Conciliation, Court of Inquiry, Labour Court, Industrial Tribunal, National Tribunal, voluntary arbitration (Sec 10A). The **IR Code 2020** merges the Labour Court and Tribunal.
- **14 days' notice** for a strike/lockout in public utilities — extended to all establishments by the IR Code 2020. Strikes during conciliation/adjudication are **illegal**.
- **Retrenchment compensation** ≈ **15 days' average pay per completed year**.
- **Due process:** show-cause notice → charge sheet → domestic enquiry → proportionate action, all documented.
