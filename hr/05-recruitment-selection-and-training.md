# HR — Topic 5: Recruitment, Selection & Training

Recruitment Sources & Strategies, Selection Process, Types of Interviews, Onboarding, TNA, Training Methods, Evaluation of Training, Development & Career Planning.

---

## 1. Recruitment vs Selection (Basic Idea)

**Recruitment** is the process of **searching for and attracting** a pool of capable applicants.
**Selection** is the process of **choosing the most suitable person** from that pool.

**Everyday analogy:** Recruitment is casting a wide net to fill your boat with fish. Selection is sorting through the catch and keeping only the ones you want.

### The comparison ⭐⭐ (asked almost every time)

| Basis | **Recruitment** | **Selection** |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| Meaning | Searching and attracting applicants | Choosing the right candidate |
| Objective | Create a **large pool** | **Narrow down** to one |
| Nature | **Positive** process (invites, encourages) | **Negative** process (rejects, eliminates) |
| Sequence | Comes **first** | Comes **after** recruitment |
| Contract | No contractual relation created | Leads to a **contract of employment** |
| Cost focus | Cost of attracting | Cost of assessing |
| Techniques | Advertising, referrals, portals, campus | Tests, interviews, reference checks |

> 🎯 **The memorable line:** *"Recruitment is positive — it invites. Selection is negative — it rejects."*

## 2. Sources of Recruitment ⭐⭐⭐

### 2.1 Internal sources
Filling a vacancy from **within** the organisation.

| Source | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Promotion** | Moving an employee **up** to a higher post |
| **Transfer** | Moving **sideways** to a similar post, often another location/department |
| **Internal job posting (IJP)** | Vacancy advertised internally so employees can apply |
| **Employee referrals** | Existing employees recommend candidates (technically a bridge to external) |
| **Re-hiring former employees** | "Boomerang" hires; alumni networks |
| **Retired/retrenched employees recalled** | For short-term or specialist needs |
| **Deceased/disabled employee's dependants** | Compassionate appointment (common in PSUs) |

### 2.2 External sources
Bringing people in from **outside**.

| Source | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Advertisements** | Newspapers, trade journals, job portals |
| **Job portals / online** | Naukri, LinkedIn, Indeed, company careers page |
| **Campus recruitment** | Hiring directly from colleges — the main graduate pipeline |
| **Employment exchanges** | Government-run registers |
| **Placement agencies / consultants** | Third-party recruiters, for a fee |
| **Executive search ("head-hunting")** | For senior/confidential positions |
| **Walk-ins / write-ins / talk-ins** | Direct applications |
| **Labour contractors** | Common for unskilled and construction work |
| **Apprentices / interns converted** | Trainees absorbed into full-time roles |
| **Social media recruiting** | LinkedIn, X, Instagram; employer branding |
| **Poaching / raiding** | Hiring from a competitor |
| **Gate hiring** | Casual workers hired at the factory gate |

### 2.3 Internal vs External ⭐⭐⭐ (the key comparison)

| Basis | **Internal** | **External** |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| Cost | **Low** | High |
| Speed | **Fast** | Slower |
| Morale | **Boosts** — shows growth is possible | Can **demoralise** existing staff |
| Known quantity | Performance history already known → **lower risk** | Unknown; higher risk of a bad hire |
| Culture fit | Already fits | Uncertain |
| Induction/training | Minimal | Extensive |
| Fresh ideas | **Limited** — inbreeding, stagnation | **Brings new skills and perspectives** |
| Choice available | Restricted to existing staff | **Wide pool** |
| Politics | Can cause rivalry over promotions | Neutral |
| Best for | Junior→middle promotions, culture-critical roles | New skills, expansion, senior specialists, entry level |

> 💡 **The balanced answer examiners want:** most organisations use a **hybrid** — internal first (to protect morale and cost), external when the skill genuinely doesn't exist inside. Over-reliance on internal hiring causes **"organisational inbreeding"**; over-reliance on external hiring destroys morale and retention.

## 3. Recruitment Process and Strategies

### The process
```
1. Identify the vacancy  (manpower requisition / MRF)
2. Job analysis          → JD and JS
3. Decide the source     (internal / external)
4. Choose the method     (advertise, portal, campus, agency)
5. Attract applications
6. Screen and shortlist  → hand over to SELECTION
7. Evaluate the effectiveness (cost per hire, yield ratios, quality of hire)
```

### Modern recruitment strategies
| Strategy | What it means |
|----------|--------------|
| **Employer branding** | Building a reputation as a great place to work so candidates come to you |
| **Talent pipelining** | Maintaining a warm pool of pre-screened candidates before vacancies arise |
| **Employee referral programmes** | Incentivising staff to refer — cheapest source with the best retention record |
| **Campus and pre-placement offers** | Internships converted into full-time offers |
| **RPO** (Recruitment Process Outsourcing) | Outsourcing part or all of recruitment to a specialist |
| **Social and mobile recruiting** | Sourcing and applying via social platforms and phones |
| **Diversity hiring** | Deliberately widening the pool for under-represented groups |
| **Gig / contingent hiring** | Freelancers and contract staff for variable demand |
| **Boomerang hiring** | Re-employing good former employees |

## 4. The Selection Process ⭐⭐⭐

Selection is a **series of hurdles**; a candidate must clear each to reach the next.

```
1.  Receipt of applications
2.  Preliminary screening        ← reject obvious misfits early (cheapest stage)
3.  Application blank / form
4.  Selection tests              ← aptitude, personality, skill
5.  Selection interview(s)
6.  Reference / background check (BGV)
7.  Medical examination
8.  Final selection & approval
9.  Job offer / appointment letter
10. Placement and induction
```

> 🎯 **Exam point:** the sequence is designed so the **cheapest filters come first** and the most expensive (interviews, medicals) apply to the fewest people.

### Worked example — planning the funnel backwards ⭐

You must hire **20 people**. Historic conversion rates are: application→shortlist **25%**, shortlist→interview **40%**, interview→offer **25%**, offer→join **80%**.
How many applications do you need?

```
Step 1: Offers needed        = 20 ÷ 0.80  = 25
Step 2: Interviews needed    = 25 ÷ 0.25  = 100
Step 3: Shortlists needed    = 100 ÷ 0.40 = 250
Step 4: Applications needed  = 250 ÷ 0.25 = 1,000
```
✅ **You need about 1,000 applications to make 20 hires** (a selection ratio of 1:50).

> 💡 This is how real recruitment targets are set. Work **backwards** from hires needed through each yield ratio.
> *(Yield ratio and selection ratio formulas are in [Topic 2](02-abbreviations-terms-and-metrics.md).)*

### Types of selection tests
| Test | Measures |
|------|----------|
| **Aptitude test** | Potential/ability to learn (numerical, verbal, reasoning) |
| **Achievement / proficiency test** | Skill already acquired — a typing or coding test |
| **Intelligence test** | General mental ability (IQ) |
| **Personality test** | Traits and temperament (e.g. Big Five, MBTI, 16PF) |
| **Interest test** | What the person enjoys doing |
| **Situational judgement test** | Responses to realistic work scenarios |
| **Trade / work sample test** | Actual sample of the job's work |
| **Polygraph / integrity test** | Honesty (controversial, limited use) |
| **Assessment centre** | **Multiple exercises together** — group discussion, in-basket, role play, presentation, psychometrics — assessed by trained observers |

### Reliability and validity ⭐ (a favourite short question)

| Term | Meaning | Test of it |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Reliability** | **Consistency** — same result on repetition | Would a candidate score similarly next week? |
| **Validity** | **Accuracy** — does it measure what it claims and predict job performance? | Do high scorers actually perform better on the job? |

> 🔑 **A test can be reliable without being valid, but never valid without being reliable.**
> A weighing scale that always reads 5 kg too high is perfectly *reliable* but not *valid*.

**Types of validity:** *content* (covers the job's content), *construct* (measures the trait it claims), *criterion* — split into **predictive** (score predicts later performance) and **concurrent** (score matches current performers').

## 5. Types of Interviews ⭐⭐⭐

| Type | Description | Strength / weakness |
|------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Structured** (patterned) | Pre-set questions, same for every candidate, scored on a fixed scale | **Most reliable and fairest**; comparable; can feel rigid |
| **Unstructured** (non-directive) | Free-flowing conversation, no fixed questions | Flexible, reveals personality; **low reliability, high bias**, hard to compare |
| **Semi-structured** | Core fixed questions plus freedom to probe | Practical middle ground — most common in practice |
| **Behavioural** (BEI) | Asks for **past real examples**: *"Tell me about a time you handled conflict."* Based on the premise that **past behaviour predicts future behaviour** | Strong predictive validity; candidates can rehearse answers |
| **Situational** | Hypothetical: *"What would you do if…?"* | Good for freshers with no work history |
| **Panel / board** | **Several interviewers together**, one candidate | Balanced judgement, saves time, consistent; **intimidating** for the candidate |
| **Group interview** | **Several candidates together**, often a group discussion | Reveals teamwork and leadership; quiet strong candidates get lost |
| **Stress interview** | Deliberately hostile or rapid-fire questioning | Tests composure; risks damaging employer brand |
| **Depth / in-depth** | Detailed probing of one area of expertise | For specialist roles |
| **Telephonic / video** | Remote screening or full interview | Cheap and fast; loses non-verbal cues |
| **Exit interview** | Conducted when an employee **leaves**, to learn the real reason | Data for retention strategy |

### STAR — the framework used to run and answer behavioural interviews
```
S — Situation:  what was the context?
T — Task:       what was your responsibility?
A — Action:     what did YOU actually do?
R — Result:     what was the outcome (ideally quantified)?
```

### Interview errors (mirror the appraisal errors in [Topic 7](07-performance-management.md))
**Halo effect** · **horn effect** · **first-impression/primacy bias** · **recency bias** · **similar-to-me bias** · **stereotyping** · **contrast error** (judging against the previous candidate) · **leading questions** · **non-verbal bias**

**Remedies:** structured questions, scoring rubric, multiple/panel interviewers, interviewer training, taking notes during rather than after.

## 6. Onboarding, Induction and Placement

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Placement** | Assigning the selected candidate to the actual job/position |
| **Induction / Orientation** | **Formal introduction** to the organisation — policies, facilities, colleagues. Usually days |
| **Onboarding** | The **full integration process** — induction plus role training, goal-setting, mentoring, follow-up. Usually 30/60/90 days up to a year |

> 🎯 **Induction vs Onboarding is a likely comparison:** induction is an **event**; onboarding is a **process**. Induction tells you where the canteen is; onboarding makes you productive and committed.

### What good onboarding covers
- **Compliance** — paperwork, policies, statutory forms
- **Clarification** — role, goals, expectations, KRAs
- **Culture** — values, norms, "how we work here"
- **Connection** — manager, team, buddy/mentor, cross-functional contacts

### Why it matters
Poor onboarding is a leading cause of **early attrition** (people leaving within 90 days). Structured onboarding improves **time-to-productivity**, engagement and first-year retention.

## 7. Training Needs Analysis (TNA) ⭐⭐

**TNA** is the systematic process of identifying **the gap between the skills employees have and the skills the job requires**.

> **Training need = Required (desired) performance − Actual performance**

### The three levels of TNA ⭐ (a guaranteed sub-question)

| Level | Question it answers | Data used |
|-------|--------------------|-----------|
| **Organisational analysis** | *Where in the organisation is training needed?* Does it fit strategy and resources? | Business goals, productivity data, attrition, quality/customer complaints, new technology |
| **Task / job analysis** | *What must be taught?* Which knowledge and skills does the job require? | JD, JS, competency framework, task lists, standards |
| **Person / individual analysis** | *Who needs training?* Which specific employees fall short? | Appraisal results, tests, observation, self-assessment, 360° feedback |

### Methods of collecting TNA data
Performance appraisal records · observation · questionnaires and surveys · interviews with employees and managers · skill/competency gap matrix · customer complaints · analysis of errors, wastage and accidents · exit interview themes · assessment centres

> ⚠️ **Common exam trap:** not every performance problem is a training problem. If the cause is **poor tools, unclear goals, weak motivation or bad process**, training will waste money. TNA must first ask: *"Is this a skill gap or a system gap?"*

## 8. Training Delivery Methods ⭐⭐

### 8.1 On-the-job methods (learning **while doing** the actual job)

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Job instruction training (JIT)** | Step-by-step "show, tell, do, check" for a specific task |
| **Coaching** | Superior guides the employee on performance, day to day |
| **Mentoring** | A senior guides a junior's **long-term career** and development |
| **Job rotation** | Planned movement across jobs to build breadth |
| **Apprenticeship** | Long structured training under a skilled worker (trades) |
| **Internship** | Students gain practical exposure |
| **Understudy / assistant-to** | Trainee works as an assistant to a senior, learning by observation |
| **Committee assignments** | Trainee joins a committee to solve a real problem |

### 8.2 Off-the-job methods (learning **away from** the workplace)

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Lecture / classroom** | Trainer presents to a group — cheap for large numbers, passive |
| **Case study** | Analysis of a real business situation — develops judgement |
| **Role play** | Trainees act out roles — good for interpersonal and sales skills |
| **Business games / simulations** | Competitive decision-making exercises |
| **In-basket exercise** | Trainee prioritises a pile of realistic memos and problems |
| **Vestibule training** | A replica of the actual workplace set up for training |
| **Sensitivity / T-group training** | Unstructured group discussion to build self-awareness |
| **Conferences and seminars** | Discussion-based, expert-led |
| **Programmed instruction** | Self-paced material with built-in feedback |
| **E-learning / online / LMS** | Digital modules; **MOOCs**; anytime-anywhere |
| **Blended learning** | Deliberate mix of online and classroom |
| **Behaviour modelling** | Watch a model performer, then practise and get feedback |

### 8.3 Comparison ⭐

| Basis | **On-the-job** | **Off-the-job** |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| Location | At the workplace | Away from the workplace |
| Cost | **Low** | Higher |
| Production disruption | Possible — real work, real mistakes | None |
| Transfer to job | **Immediate and direct** | Needs deliberate transfer effort |
| Learning environment | Noisy, pressured, unstructured | Calm, structured, safe to fail |
| Suits | Practical, technical, routine skills | Concepts, theory, behavioural and managerial skills |

### 8.4 Classroom vs Online (the syllabus contrast)

| Basis | Classroom | Online |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Interaction | High, face-to-face | Lower unless deliberately designed |
| Cost per head | High (venue, travel, trainer) | **Low, and scales cheaply** |
| Flexibility | Fixed time and place | **Self-paced, anywhere** |
| Consistency | Varies with the trainer | **Identical content for everyone** |
| Tracking | Manual | **Automatic via LMS** |
| Best for | Behavioural skills, discussion, team building | Compliance, product knowledge, large dispersed groups |

## 9. Evaluation of Training Effectiveness ⭐⭐⭐

### Kirkpatrick's Four-Level Model — learn this cold

| Level | Name | What is measured | How |
|-------|------|-----------------|-----|
| **1** | **Reaction** | Did they **like** it? Satisfaction with the training | Feedback forms, "happy sheets" |
| **2** | **Learning** | Did they **learn** it? Gain in knowledge/skill | Pre- and post-tests, demonstrations |
| **3** | **Behaviour** | Are they **using** it on the job? Change in workplace behaviour | Observation, manager and 360° feedback, after 2–3 months |
| **4** | **Results** | Did the **business** improve? | Productivity, quality, cost, sales, attrition, customer satisfaction |

**Phillips' Level 5 — ROI:** converts Level 4 results into money and compares with cost.

> **Training ROI (%) = ((Monetary benefit − Training cost) ÷ Training cost) × 100**

**Worked example:** A programme costs **₹4,00,000**. It reduces rework, saving a measured **₹10,00,000**.
```
ROI = ((10,00,000 − 4,00,000) ÷ 4,00,000) × 100
    = (6,00,000 ÷ 4,00,000) × 100
    = 150%
```
✅ **ROI = 150%** — every rupee spent returned ₹1.50 on top of itself.

> 🎯 **The exam insight:** most organisations only measure **Level 1** because it is easy, and almost none reach Level 4. The higher the level, the more valuable — and the harder — the measurement. Say this and you show real understanding.

### Training vs Development ⭐ (common comparison)

| Basis | **Training** | **Development** |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| Focus | Present **job** | Future **career and growth** |
| Scope | Narrow — specific skills | Broad — overall personality and capability |
| Duration | Short-term | Long-term, continuous |
| Aim | Improve current performance | Prepare for future roles |
| Initiated by | Mostly the **organisation** | Largely **self-driven**, supported by the organisation |
| Typical for | Operatives and technical staff | Managers and high-potential employees |
| Result | Skill acquired | Potential realised |

*(Also know **Education** — general, theoretical, institution-based, unrelated to a specific job.)*

## 10. Employee Development and Career Planning

### Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Career** | The sequence of jobs and roles a person holds over working life |
| **Career planning** | The **individual** setting career goals and identifying how to reach them |
| **Career management** | The **organisation** designing paths, moves and development to meet both needs |
| **Career development** | Outcome of the two working together |
| **Career path** | The typical progression route through roles |
| **Career anchor** | The self-image and value a person will not give up (Schein) |
| **Career plateau** | Point where further promotion becomes unlikely |
| **Dual career ladder** | Parallel technical and managerial progression, so specialists can advance without becoming managers |
| **Succession planning** | Preparing internal successors for **key positions** |
| **IDP** | Individual Development Plan — written plan of an employee's development actions |

### Career stages
```
1. Exploration    (~up to 25) — education, trying out, finding fit
2. Establishment  (~25–35)    — first real job, learning, seeking acceptance, early promotion
3. Mid-career     (~35–50)    — peak productivity; growth, maintenance OR plateau/decline
4. Late career    (~50–60)    — mentoring others, using experience, less new learning
5. Decline / disengagement (~60+) — preparing for retirement, handover
```

### Schein's career anchors (know a few by name)
Technical/functional competence · General managerial competence · **Autonomy/independence** · **Security/stability** · Entrepreneurial creativity · Service/dedication to a cause · Pure challenge · **Lifestyle**

### Management development techniques
Job rotation · coaching and mentoring · committee assignments · action learning · **assessment/development centres** · special projects · in-house leadership programmes · external executive education · **job enrichment** · understudy assignments

> 💡 **Why organisations invest in career planning:** it improves **retention** (people leave when they can't see a future), builds a **succession pipeline**, reduces external hiring cost, and increases engagement. The strongest exam line is: *"People don't leave jobs, they leave the absence of a next job."*

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 5)

- **Recruitment = positive** (attract a pool). **Selection = negative** (reject to find one).
- **Internal sources:** promotion, transfer, IJP, referrals — cheap, fast, boosts morale, but risks **inbreeding**.
- **External sources:** ads, portals, campus, agencies, head-hunting — brings fresh skills, costs more, slower.
- **Selection sequence:** screening → application → tests → interview → reference check → medical → offer → placement. Cheapest filters first.
- **Plan the funnel backwards** through yield ratios to find how many applicants you need.
- **Reliability = consistency. Validity = accuracy.** A test can be reliable without being valid, never valid without being reliable.
- **Structured interviews are the most reliable**; unstructured the most biased. **Behavioural (BEI)** assumes past behaviour predicts future behaviour → answer using **STAR**.
- **Panel** = many interviewers, one candidate. **Group** = many candidates.
- **Induction is an event; onboarding is a process.**
- **TNA has three levels:** organisational (where), task (what), person (who). Not every performance gap is a training gap.
- **On-the-job** = cheap, immediate transfer. **Off-the-job** = structured, safe, better for concepts.
- **Kirkpatrick:** Reaction → Learning → Behaviour → Results (+ Phillips **ROI**). Most firms stop at Level 1.
- **Training ROI = ((Benefit − Cost) ÷ Cost) × 100.**
- **Training = present job, narrow, short. Development = future career, broad, long.**
- **Career planning is individual; career management is organisational.** **Dual career ladder** lets specialists progress without managing.
