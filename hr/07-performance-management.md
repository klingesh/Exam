# HR — Topic 7: Performance Management

Appraisal Methods, Setting Standards & Objectives, Feedback & Coaching, PIPs, PMS Software and Calibration.

---

## 1. Performance Management vs Performance Appraisal (Basic Idea)

**Performance appraisal** is the **periodic evaluation** of an employee's performance against expectations.
**Performance management** is the **continuous process** of setting goals, monitoring, developing and rewarding performance to achieve organisational objectives.

**Everyday analogy:** Performance appraisal is the **exam at the end of term**. Performance management is the **whole year** — the syllabus, the classes, the homework, the corrections and the exam together. An exam without teaching tells you the result but doesn't improve it.

### The comparison ⭐⭐⭐ (asked very frequently)

| Basis | **Performance Appraisal** | **Performance Management** |
|-------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| Nature | An **event** | A **continuous process/system** |
| Frequency | Periodic — annually or half-yearly | Ongoing, all year |
| Focus | **Evaluating** past performance | **Improving** future performance |
| Orientation | Backward-looking | Forward-looking |
| Approach | Reactive; corrective | Proactive; developmental |
| Owner | HR-driven | **Line-manager-driven**, HR-enabled |
| Tools | Rating forms | Goal-setting, feedback, coaching, development plans |
| Scope | Narrower — one part of the system | Broader — includes appraisal |
| Outcome | A rating | Improved capability and business results |

> 🎯 **The line to remember:** *"Appraisal is a part of performance management, not a substitute for it."*

### Objectives of performance management
1. Align individual goals with **organisational** goals
2. Provide a basis for **pay, promotion and reward** decisions
3. Identify **training and development** needs
4. Give employees **clarity and feedback**
5. Identify **high potential** for succession
6. Provide documented grounds for **transfer, demotion or termination**
7. Improve **communication** between manager and employee

                             ▼
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐

                             ▼

## 3. Setting Performance Standards and Objectives ⭐⭐

### SMART goals
| Letter | Means | Weak goal | SMART goal |
|--------|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **S** | Specific | "Improve sales" | "Increase sales of Product X in the South region" |
| **M** | Measurable | "Do better" | "by 15%" |
| **A** | Achievable | "by 500%" | realistic given resources |
| **R** | Relevant | unrelated to strategy | supports the growth objective |
| **T** | Time-bound | "soon" | "by 31 March" |

**Full SMART example:** *"Increase sales of Product X in the South region by 15% over last year, by 31 March, without increasing the discount rate."*

### KRA vs KPI vs Target ⭐ (frequently confused)

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **KRA** (Key Result Area) | The **broad area** of responsibility where results are expected | Customer retention |
| **KPI** (Key Performance Indicator) | The **measure** used to track that area | Customer churn rate |
| **Target** | The **specific number** to be achieved | Reduce churn from 12% to 8% |

### MBO — Management by Objectives ⭐⭐
Developed from **Peter Drucker's** work. Superior and subordinate **jointly set** objectives; performance is judged against **agreed, measurable results** rather than traits.

**The MBO process:**
```
1. Set organisational objectives
2. Cascade to departmental objectives
3. Manager and employee JOINTLY set individual objectives
4. Agree the action plan and resources
5. Employee performs; periodic review and self-control
6. Appraise against the objectives
7. Feed the result into rewards and the next cycle
```

| Advantages | Limitations |
|-----------|-------------|
| Objective and measurable — reduces bias | Time-consuming, paperwork-heavy |
| Employee participation → higher commitment | Overemphasis on **quantifiable** goals; ignores quality and behaviour |
| Role clarity | Encourages short-term focus |
| Links individual to organisational goals | Hard where goals are interdependent across teams |
| Employee self-control and ownership | Needs genuine top-management support to work |

### OKRs — the modern cousin
**Objectives and Key Results**: an ambitious qualitative **Objective** plus 2–5 measurable **Key Results**. Typically quarterly, transparent across the company, and deliberately **decoupled from pay** so people set stretch goals honestly.

> 🎯 **MBO vs OKR:** MBO is annual, cascaded top-down, and tied to appraisal and pay. OKR is quarterly, largely bottom-up, public, and separated from pay to encourage ambition.

## 4. Performance Appraisal Methods ⭐⭐⭐

### 4.1 Traditional methods

| Method | How it works | Note |
|--------|-------------|------|
| **Ranking** | Employees ranked best to worst overall | Simple; no idea of the *gap* between ranks |
| **Paired comparison** | Every employee compared with every other, one pair at a time | More reliable than simple ranking; comparisons = **n(n−1)/2** |
| **Grading** | Employees slotted into pre-defined grades (A/B/C or Outstanding/Good/Average/Poor) | Easy but crude |
| **Forced distribution** | Ratings forced into a fixed distribution (bell curve) | Prevents rating inflation; see below |
| **Checklist** | Rater ticks statements that describe the employee | Simple; rater doesn't actually evaluate, just reports |
| **Critical incident** | Rater records specific instances of very effective/ineffective behaviour | Behaviour-based and factual; needs continuous record-keeping |
| **Graphic rating scale** | Traits/factors rated on a scale (e.g. 1–5) | **Most widely used**; quick; but trait-based and prone to halo error |
| **Essay / free-form** | Rater writes a descriptive account | Rich detail; not comparable across raters |
| **Confidential report** | Descriptive report, historically not shared with the employee | Government practice; no feedback value |
| **Field review** | An HR specialist interviews managers and rates on their behalf | Reduces manager bias; expensive |

### 4.2 Modern methods

| Method | How it works |
|--------|-------------|
| **MBO** | Appraisal against jointly agreed measurable objectives *(see above)* |
| **360-degree feedback** | Feedback gathered from all directions *(see below)* |
| **BARS** (Behaviourally Anchored Rating Scale) | Each point on the scale is anchored to a **described real behaviour**, not an abstract adjective. Combines critical incidents with rating scales — much more objective |
| **Assessment / development centre** | Multiple exercises (group discussion, role play, in-basket, presentations) observed by trained assessors |
| **Psychological appraisal** | Assesses **potential** rather than past performance — intelligence, emotional stability, motivation |
| **Human resource accounting** | Treats the employee as an asset; compares the **cost** of the employee with the **value contributed** |
| **Balanced Scorecard** | Performance viewed on four perspectives: **financial, customer, internal process, learning & growth** |
| **Continuous / agile performance management** | Frequent lightweight check-ins replacing the annual review |

### 4.3 Graphic rating scale vs BARS ⭐

| Basis | Graphic Rating Scale | BARS |
|-------|---------------------|------|
| Anchored to | Abstract traits ("initiative: 1–5") | **Specific observable behaviours** |
| Objectivity | Lower — raters interpret differently | Higher — everyone reads the same behavioural anchor |
| Development cost | Low | **High** — needs critical incidents collected per job |
| Feedback quality | Vague ("you got 3 on initiative") | Concrete and actionable |

### 4.4 360-Degree Feedback ⭐⭐⭐

Feedback collected from **everyone around** the employee, not just the boss.

```
                      SUPERVISOR
                          │
                          ▼
      PEERS  ─────►   EMPLOYEE   ◄─────  INTERNAL / EXTERNAL
                       (+ SELF)              CUSTOMERS
                          ▲
                          │
                    SUBORDINATES
                 (upward feedback)
```

**Related variants:** **180-degree** = self + supervisor (sometimes peers). **540/720-degree** = adds external stakeholders such as customers and suppliers.

| Advantages | Limitations |
|-----------|-------------|
| Multiple perspectives → more balanced and complete | Time-consuming and administratively heavy |
| Reduces single-rater bias | Raters may **collude**, or settle scores anonymously |
| Excellent for **behavioural and leadership** competencies | Conflicting feedback can confuse the employee |
| Raises self-awareness (self vs others' view) | Anonymity can be compromised in small teams |
| Employees accept peer feedback more readily | Poor fit for **pay decisions** — best used for **development** |

> 🎯 **The key exam judgement:** 360-degree feedback is best used **developmentally**. The moment it drives pay, honesty collapses — people rate friends generously and rivals harshly.

### 4.5 Forced Distribution / the Bell Curve ⭐

Ratings are forced into fixed proportions, e.g.:

| Rating | Forced % |
|--------|----------|
| Outstanding | 10% |
| Exceeds expectations | 20% |
| Meets expectations | 40% |
| Below expectations | 20% |
| Unsatisfactory | 10% |

*(GE's well-known "vitality curve" used a 20–70–10 split.)*

| Advantages | Criticisms |
|-----------|-----------|
| Stops **rating inflation** where everyone is "excellent" | Assumes performance is normally distributed in **every** team — false in small or uniformly strong teams |
| Forces managers to genuinely differentiate | Damages teamwork; creates internal competition |
| Makes reward budgets predictable | Demotivates people forced into low buckets unfairly |
| Identifies top talent and chronic underperformers | Many large firms (including GE and Microsoft) have **abandoned** it |

## 5. Appraisal Errors and Biases ⭐⭐

| Error | What happens |
|-------|-------------|
| **Halo effect** | One **good** trait colours the whole rating positively |
| **Horn effect** | One **bad** trait drags the whole rating down |
| **Central tendency** | Rater gives everyone middling scores to avoid trouble |
| **Leniency error** | Rater is systematically too generous |
| **Strictness / severity error** | Rater is systematically too harsh |
| **Recency effect** | Only the **last few weeks** are remembered |
| **Primacy effect** | First impressions dominate the whole period |
| **Similar-to-me bias** | Higher ratings for people like the rater |
| **Stereotyping** | Judging by group membership rather than performance |
| **Contrast error** | Rating relative to the previous person rated, not to the standard |
| **Spillover effect** | Last year's rating carried into this year |
| **Attribution error** | Blaming the person for failures actually caused by the system |

### Remedies
1. **Train raters** on the errors and the scale
2. Use **behaviour-based** methods (BARS, critical incident) instead of trait-based
3. Maintain **continuous documentation** — kills the recency effect
4. Use **multiple raters** (360-degree, panel)
5. **Calibration** meetings *(see section 8)*
6. Require **written justification** for extreme ratings
7. Separate **potential** assessment from **performance** assessment

## 6. Performance Feedback and Coaching ⭐⭐

### Principles of effective feedback
- **Timely** — close to the event, not saved for the annual review
- **Specific** — describe behaviour, not personality
- **Behaviour-focused** — *"you missed three deadlines"*, not *"you're careless"*
- **Balanced** — reinforce what worked as well as what didn't
- **Two-way** — invite the employee's view
- **Private** — criticism is never given publicly
- **Forward-looking** — end with an agreed action

### The SBI feedback model
```
S — Situation:  "In yesterday's client review…"
B — Behaviour:  "…you presented the data before the context…"
I — Impact:     "…and the client was confused about the recommendation."
```

> ⚠️ **The "feedback sandwich"** (praise–criticism–praise) is widely taught but has a known weakness: people hear only the praise, or learn to dread every compliment. **SBI is the safer model** — describe the situation, the behaviour and its impact, then agree the change.

### The GROW coaching model ⭐
```
G — Goal:     What do you want to achieve?
R — Reality:  Where are you now? What's actually happening?
O — Options:  What could you do? What else?
W — Will / Way forward: What WILL you do, by when?
```

### Coaching vs Mentoring vs Counselling

| Basis | **Coaching** | **Mentoring** | **Counselling** |
|-------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Focus | Performance and skills | Long-term career growth | Personal or emotional issues |
| Time frame | Short-term, task-linked | Long-term relationship | As needed |
| Usually by | Immediate manager | A senior, often outside the reporting line | Trained counsellor / EAP |
| Direction | Structured, goal-driven | Guidance, sharing experience | Supportive, non-directive |

## 7. Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) ⭐

A **PIP** is a formal, written, time-bound plan to help an underperforming employee reach the required standard.

### When to use one
Performance is **consistently** below standard and informal feedback and coaching have already failed — and the cause is genuinely **capability or effort**, not unclear goals or missing resources.

### What a PIP must contain
```
1. Specific performance gaps, with evidence and examples
2. The exact standard expected (measurable)
3. Support provided — training, mentoring, tools, reduced load
4. Review checkpoints (typically every 2 weeks)
5. Duration (commonly 30, 60 or 90 days)
6. Consequences if the standard is not met
7. Signatures of employee and manager
```

### Possible outcomes
| Outcome | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Standard met | PIP closed; employee returns to normal cycle |
| Partial improvement | PIP may be extended once |
| No improvement | Redeployment, demotion, or termination |

> 🎯 **The two-sided nature of a PIP.** Genuinely it is a **development tool** — a documented, supported chance to improve. In practice it is also the organisation's **legal documentation** that due process was followed before termination. A good exam answer mentions both.

## 8. Performance Calibration ⭐

**Calibration** is a meeting where managers review each other's proposed ratings **before they are finalised**, to ensure consistency across teams.

### The problem it solves
Manager A is generous and rates his team mostly "Outstanding". Manager B is strict and rates an equally good team "Meets expectations". Without calibration, B's team is unfairly penalised in pay and promotion — and the ratings are meaningless as a comparison.

### How it works
```
1. Managers submit proposed ratings
2. Managers meet with HR and a common seniority level
3. Each manager JUSTIFIES ratings with evidence
4. Ratings are compared against a shared standard
5. Outliers are challenged and adjusted
6. Final ratings agreed and released
```

### Benefits
- **Consistency and fairness** across departments
- Reduces individual manager bias
- Creates a shared understanding of what each rating actually means
- Defensible pay and promotion decisions
- Managers learn from each other's standards

> 💡 **Calibration vs forced distribution:** calibration seeks *consistency* through discussion and evidence; forced distribution imposes *quotas* regardless of actual performance. Calibration is the fairer and now far more common approach.

## 9. Performance Management Software and Systems

### What a modern PMS does
| Capability | Benefit |
|-----------|---------|
| Goal setting and **cascading** | Every goal visibly links to a business objective |
| **Continuous check-ins** | Replaces one annual conversation with regular ones |
| **360-degree feedback** collection | Automated, anonymised, easy to administer |
| Real-time **dashboards** | Managers and HR see progress live |
| **Calibration** support | Distribution views and outlier flags |
| Integration with **payroll and L&D** | Rating flows into increments and training plans |
| **Analytics** | Correlates performance with attrition, engagement, hiring source |
| Documentation trail | Legal protection for pay and exit decisions |

Common platforms: SAP SuccessFactors, Workday, Oracle HCM, Darwinbox, Zoho People, Keka, Lattice, 15Five.

### Where systems go wrong
- **Digitising a broken process** — an online form for a badly designed appraisal is still a bad appraisal
- Managers treat it as **compliance box-ticking** rather than a conversation
- Too many mandatory fields → rushed, low-quality input
- No manager training → the tool changes but the bias doesn't

## 10. The Modern Shift: Annual Review → Continuous Performance Management

| Basis | **Traditional annual appraisal** | **Continuous performance management** |
|-------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Frequency | Once a year | Ongoing check-ins (monthly/quarterly) |
| Feedback | Retrospective, often a surprise | Real-time, no surprises |
| Goals | Set once, often stale by mid-year | Reviewed and reset as priorities change |
| Rating | Central to the process | De-emphasised or dropped entirely |
| Focus | Judging and ranking | Coaching and developing |
| Employee experience | Anxiety-inducing, bureaucratic | Conversational, developmental |
| Suits | Stable, predictable work | Fast-changing, project-based work |

> 💡 Several large organisations have **dropped annual ratings** in favour of continuous feedback. The honest counter-argument for the exam: pay, promotion and legal defensibility still require **some** documented differentiation — so most firms end up with continuous feedback **plus** a lighter annual summary rating.

## ✅ Quick Recap (Topic 7)

- **Appraisal = an event, backward-looking. Performance management = a continuous system, forward-looking.** Appraisal is a part of it.
- **Cycle:** Planning → Monitoring → Reviewing → Rewarding/Developing.
- **SMART** = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
- **KRA = the area. KPI = the measure. Target = the number.**
- **MBO** (Drucker) = jointly set, measurable objectives. Strength: objectivity. Weakness: over-quantification and paperwork.
- **Paired comparisons = n(n−1)/2.**
- **BARS** anchors each scale point to a real described behaviour → far more objective than a graphic rating scale.
- **360-degree** = self + supervisor + peers + subordinates + customers. Best used for **development, not pay**.
- **Forced distribution** stops rating inflation but assumes a bell curve exists in every team; widely abandoned.
- **Errors:** halo, horn, central tendency, leniency, strictness, recency, primacy, similar-to-me, contrast, spillover.
- **Feedback:** use **SBI** (Situation–Behaviour–Impact). **Coaching:** use **GROW** (Goal–Reality–Options–Will).
- **PIP** = written, time-bound improvement plan; both a development tool **and** legal documentation.
- **Calibration** = managers cross-check ratings for consistency. Consistency by discussion, not by quota.
- The trend is from the **annual review** toward **continuous performance management**.
