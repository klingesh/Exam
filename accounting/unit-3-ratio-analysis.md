# Accounting — Unit III: Ratio Analysis, Funds Flow & Cash Flow

Importance, Limitations, Types of Ratios, and Flow Statements.

## 1. What is Ratio Analysis?

A **ratio** expresses the relationship between two figures from the financial statements. **Ratio analysis** is a tool to interpret those numbers to judge a company's **profitability, liquidity, solvency, and efficiency**.

**Analogy:** Just as a doctor checks BP, pulse, and sugar to judge health, an analyst checks ratios to judge a company's financial health.

A ratio can be expressed as:
- **Pure ratio** (2:1), **Percentage** (25%), **Times** (5 times), or **Days** (45 days).

### Importance / Uses
1. **Measures performance** — profitability & efficiency.
2. **Judges financial position** — liquidity & solvency.
3. **Helps comparison** — over time (trend) and with competitors.
4. **Aids decision-making** — for managers, investors, lenders.
5. **Forecasting & budgeting** — planning future.
6. **Simplifies data** — turns bulky statements into meaningful indicators.

### Limitations
1. Based on **past** (historical) data — may not predict future.
2. **Ignores price-level changes** (inflation).
3. **Window dressing** — statements can be manipulated.
4. **Only quantitative** — ignores quality (management, morale).
5. No single ratio is meaningful **alone** — needs comparison/standard.
6. Different firms use **different accounting policies** — comparison distorted.

## 2. Types of Ratios (with formulas & interpretation)

### A. LIQUIDITY RATIOS — *Can the firm pay short-term dues?*

| Ratio | Formula | Ideal | Meaning |
|-------|---------|-------|---------|
| **Current Ratio** | Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities | 2 : 1 | Ability to meet short-term obligations. |
| **Quick / Acid-Test / Liquid Ratio** | (Current Assets − Stock − Prepaid Exp) ÷ Current Liabilities | 1 : 1 | Immediate paying ability (excludes slow-moving stock). |
| **Absolute Liquid / Cash Ratio** | (Cash + Marketable Securities) ÷ Current Liabilities | 0.5 : 1 | Most conservative liquidity test. |

*Current Assets:* cash, debtors, stock, bills receivable, prepaid expenses.
*Current Liabilities:* creditors, bills payable, outstanding expenses, short-term loans.

---

### B. SOLVENCY / LEVERAGE RATIOS — *Can the firm survive long-term & repay debt?*

| Ratio | Formula | Meaning |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Debt-Equity Ratio** | Debt (Long-term) ÷ Equity (Shareholders' funds) | Proportion of borrowed vs owned funds. Ideal ≈ 2:1 (varies). |
| **Proprietary Ratio** | Shareholders' Funds ÷ Total Assets | Share of owners in total assets. Higher = safer. |
| **Total Debt Ratio** | Total Debt ÷ Total Assets | How much of assets financed by debt. |
| **Interest Coverage Ratio** | EBIT ÷ Interest | How many times profit covers interest. Higher = safer. |
| **Capital Gearing Ratio** | Fixed-interest bearing funds ÷ Equity shareholders' funds | Measures gearing (high = highly geared). |

---

### C. PROFITABILITY RATIOS — *Is the firm making good profits?*

**Based on Sales:**
| Ratio | Formula |
|-------|---------|
| **Gross Profit Ratio** | (Gross Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100 |
| **Net Profit Ratio** | (Net Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100 |
| **Operating Ratio** | (Cost of Goods Sold + Operating Exp) ÷ Net Sales × 100 |
| **Operating Profit Ratio** | (Operating Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100 |

**Based on Investment:**
| Ratio | Formula |
|-------|---------|
| **Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)** | (EBIT ÷ Capital Employed) × 100 |
| **Return on Equity (ROE)** | (Net Profit after tax − Pref. Div) ÷ Equity Shareholders' Funds × 100 |
| **Return on Assets (ROA)** | (Net Profit ÷ Total Assets) × 100 |

*Capital Employed = Fixed Assets + Working Capital = Equity + Long-term Debt.*

---

### D. TURNOVER / ACTIVITY / EFFICIENCY RATIOS — *How well are resources used?*

| Ratio | Formula | Meaning |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Stock/Inventory Turnover** | Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Average Stock | How fast stock is sold. Higher = better. |
| **Debtors Turnover** | Net Credit Sales ÷ Average Debtors | How fast money is collected. |
| **Debt Collection Period** | 365 ÷ Debtors Turnover | Avg days to collect from debtors. Lower = better. |
| **Creditors Turnover** | Net Credit Purchases ÷ Average Creditors | How fast we pay suppliers. |
| **Creditors Payment Period** | 365 ÷ Creditors Turnover | Avg days to pay creditors. |
| **Fixed Asset Turnover** | Net Sales ÷ Net Fixed Assets | Efficiency of using fixed assets. |
| **Working Capital Turnover** | Net Sales ÷ Working Capital | Efficiency of using working capital. |

---

### E. MARKET VALUE RATIOS — *What do investors think?*

| Ratio | Formula | Meaning |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Earnings Per Share (EPS)** | (Net Profit − Pref. Div) ÷ No. of Equity Shares | Profit earned per share. |
| **Price-Earnings (P/E) Ratio** | Market Price per Share ÷ EPS | How much investors pay per ₹1 of earnings. |
| **Dividend Per Share (DPS)** | Dividend to Equity ÷ No. of Equity Shares | Dividend per share. |
| **Dividend Payout Ratio** | (DPS ÷ EPS) × 100 | % of earnings paid as dividend. |
| **Dividend Yield** | (DPS ÷ Market Price) × 100 | Return from dividends. |
| **Book Value per Share** | Equity Shareholders' Funds ÷ No. of Equity Shares | Net worth per share. |

## 3. Funds Flow Analysis

**Funds Flow Statement** shows the **movement of funds (working capital)** between two balance sheet dates — where funds came from (**sources**) and where they went (**applications/uses**).

Here **"Funds" = Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities.**

### Purpose
- Explains **why** financial position changed even if profit was earned.
- Shows how long-term funds were raised and used.
- Helps in planning and dividend/financing decisions.

### Two statements involved:
**1. Schedule/Statement of Changes in Working Capital** — compares each current asset & current liability across the two years to find net increase/decrease in working capital.

**2. Funds Flow Statement:**

| Sources of Funds | Applications (Uses) of Funds |
|------------------|------------------------------|
| Funds from Operations (net profit + non-cash items) | Funds Lost in Operations |
| Issue of shares / debentures | Redemption of shares/debentures |
| Long-term loans raised | Repayment of long-term loans |
| Sale of fixed assets/investments | Purchase of fixed assets/investments |
| Decrease in Working Capital | Payment of dividend / tax |
|  | Increase in Working Capital |

**Funds from Operations** = Net Profit + Depreciation + Goodwill/Preliminary exp written off + Loss on sale of assets − Profit on sale of assets − Non-operating incomes.

## 4. Cash Flow Analysis

**Cash Flow Statement** shows **inflows and outflows of cash & cash equivalents** during a period. Prepared under **AS-3 / Ind AS-7**, classified into three activities:

| Activity | What it includes | Examples |
|----------|------------------|----------|
| **Operating** | Main revenue-producing activities | Cash from customers, payments to suppliers/employees, tax paid |
| **Investing** | Buying/selling long-term assets & investments | Purchase/sale of machinery, investments, interest & dividend received |
| **Financing** | Changes in capital & borrowings | Issue of shares/debentures, loans raised/repaid, dividend paid |

**Net Cash Flow = Operating + Investing + Financing cash flows.**
This should reconcile: Opening Cash + Net Cash Flow = Closing Cash.

### Methods for Operating Activities
- **Direct Method:** shows actual cash receipts & payments.
- **Indirect Method (common):** starts with Net Profit before tax, adjusts non-cash items (add depreciation, etc.) and working-capital changes.

---

## 5. Funds Flow vs Cash Flow (exam favorite)

| Basis | Funds Flow Statement | Cash Flow Statement |
|-------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Basis | Working capital | Cash & cash equivalents |
| Concept | Broader (all current items) | Narrower (only cash) |
| Purpose | Long-term financial analysis | Short-term liquidity analysis |
| Schedule of WC changes | Prepared | Not prepared |
| Accounting standard | Not mandatory | Mandatory (Ind AS-7) |

## ✅ Quick Recap (Unit III)
- **Liquidity:** Current (2:1), Quick (1:1).
- **Solvency:** Debt-Equity, Proprietary, Interest Coverage.
- **Profitability:** GP%, NP%, ROCE, ROE.
- **Turnover:** Stock, Debtors, Creditors turnover & periods.
- **Market:** EPS, P/E, Dividend Payout/Yield.
- **Funds Flow** = movement of *working capital*; **Cash Flow** = movement of *cash* (Operating/Investing/Financing).
