# Accounting — Unit IV: Marginal Costing & Break-Even Analysis

Meaning, Importance, Marginal vs Absorption Costing, Break-Even Analysis, Decision Making.

---

## 1. Understanding Costs First (foundation)

- **Fixed Cost:** Stays the same regardless of output (rent, salaries, insurance). *Per unit it falls as output rises.*
- **Variable Cost:** Changes directly with output (raw material, direct labour, direct power). *Per unit it stays the same.*
- **Semi-variable Cost:** Part fixed + part variable (e.g., telephone bill = fixed rental + call charges).

---

## 2. What is Marginal Costing?

**Marginal Cost** = the **additional cost of producing one more unit** = total variable cost per unit.

**Marginal Costing** is a technique where **only variable costs are charged to products**, and **fixed costs are treated as period costs** (written off fully against the period's contribution). Fixed costs are NOT included in product/inventory value.

### Key concept: CONTRIBUTION
> **Contribution = Sales − Variable Cost**
> **Contribution = Fixed Cost + Profit**

Contribution is the amount that first covers fixed costs; whatever is left is profit.

**Per unit:** Contribution per unit = Selling Price per unit − Variable Cost per unit.

### Importance / Advantages of Marginal Costing
1. **Simple** — no arbitrary fixed-cost apportionment.
2. **Cost control** — separates controllable (variable) from fixed costs.
3. **Profit planning** — via break-even and contribution.
4. **Decision-making** — pricing, make-or-buy, product mix, accept/reject orders.
5. **Avoids profit distortion** from stock valuation changes.

### Limitations
- Difficult to split costs into fixed & variable exactly.
- Ignores fixed costs in product cost — not suitable for long-term pricing.
- Not accepted for external financial reporting / tax.

---

## 3. Marginal Costing vs Absorption Costing

**Absorption Costing** (Full/Total costing) charges **both fixed AND variable** manufacturing costs to products. Fixed costs are "absorbed" into unit cost.

| Basis | Marginal Costing | Absorption Costing |
|-------|------------------|--------------------|
| Fixed cost treatment | Period cost (written off fully) | Product cost (included in unit cost) |
| Stock/Inventory valued at | Variable cost only | Variable + fixed cost |
| Cost per unit | Only variable | Variable + share of fixed |
| Profit measure | Contribution | Gross/Net profit |
| Effect of stock changes | No distortion | Profit varies with stock levels |
| Use | Internal decisions | External reporting / statutory |

> ⚠️ When production ≠ sales, the two methods give **different profits** because of the fixed cost carried in closing stock under absorption costing.

---

## 4. Break-Even Analysis (Cost-Volume-Profit / CVP Analysis)

**Break-Even Point (BEP)** = the level of sales at which **total revenue = total cost**, i.e., **no profit, no loss**. Contribution exactly equals fixed cost here.

### Key Formulas

> **P/V Ratio (Profit-Volume Ratio) = (Contribution ÷ Sales) × 100**
> Also = (Change in Profit ÷ Change in Sales) × 100

> **BEP (in units) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per unit**

> **BEP (in ₹/value) = Fixed Cost ÷ P/V Ratio**

> **Margin of Safety = Actual Sales − Break-Even Sales**
> **Margin of Safety (units) = Profit ÷ Contribution per unit**
> **MOS Ratio = (Margin of Safety ÷ Actual Sales) × 100**

> **Sales for target profit (units) = (Fixed Cost + Desired Profit) ÷ Contribution per unit**
> **Sales for target profit (₹) = (Fixed Cost + Desired Profit) ÷ P/V Ratio**

> **Profit = (Sales × P/V Ratio) − Fixed Cost**

---

### Worked Example
Selling price = ₹50/unit; Variable cost = ₹30/unit; Fixed cost = ₹1,00,000; Actual sales = 8,000 units.

- Contribution per unit = 50 − 30 = **₹20**
- P/V Ratio = (20 ÷ 50) × 100 = **40%**
- **BEP (units)** = 1,00,000 ÷ 20 = **5,000 units**
- **BEP (₹)** = 1,00,000 ÷ 0.40 = **₹2,50,000**
- Actual sales value = 8,000 × 50 = ₹4,00,000
- **Margin of Safety** = 4,00,000 − 2,50,000 = **₹1,50,000** (or 3,000 units)
- **Profit** = (4,00,000 × 40%) − 1,00,000 = 1,60,000 − 1,00,000 = **₹60,000**

**To earn a target profit of ₹1,40,000:**
Required sales (units) = (1,00,000 + 1,40,000) ÷ 20 = **12,000 units**

### Break-Even Chart (concept)
- X-axis = output/sales volume; Y-axis = costs & revenue.
- Total Cost line and Sales line **intersect at the BEP**.
- Left of BEP = loss area; Right of BEP = profit area.
- The **angle of incidence** (angle between sales & total cost lines) — wider angle = higher profit rate.

---

## 5. Decision-Making Using Marginal Costing

The golden rule: **choose the option that maximizes total contribution** (fixed costs are usually unavoidable in the short run).

### (a) Make or Buy
Compare the **marginal (variable) cost of making** vs the **buying price**. Make if variable cost of making < purchase price (assuming spare capacity).

### (b) Accept or Reject a Special Order
Accept a special/export order at a lower price **if the price still exceeds variable cost** (gives positive contribution) and spare capacity exists — because fixed costs are already covered by regular sales.

### (c) Product Mix / Key Factor (Limiting Factor)
When a resource is limited (labour hours, material, machine hours), rank products by **Contribution per unit of the limiting factor** (e.g., contribution per labour hour), not just contribution per unit.

### (d) Pricing Decisions
Set price above variable cost to ensure contribution; useful in recession or to enter new markets.

### (e) Shutdown or Continue
Continue in the short run **as long as the product earns contribution** toward fixed costs. Shut down only if the product's contribution is negative or avoidable fixed costs saved exceed contribution lost.

### (f) Product Elimination
Drop a product only if it gives **negative contribution**. A product with positive contribution helps cover common fixed costs even if it shows a "loss" after fixed cost allocation.

---

## ✅ Quick Recap (Unit IV)
- **Contribution = Sales − Variable Cost = Fixed Cost + Profit.**
- **P/V Ratio = Contribution ÷ Sales × 100.**
- **BEP (units) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per unit;  BEP (₹) = Fixed Cost ÷ P/V Ratio.**
- **Margin of Safety = Actual Sales − BEP Sales.**
- **Marginal costing** treats fixed cost as period cost; **absorption costing** includes it in product cost.
- Decisions: pick option with **highest contribution**; for limited resources use **contribution per unit of key factor**.
