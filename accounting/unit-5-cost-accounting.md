# Accounting — Unit V: Cost Accounting

Basic Concepts & Importance, Cost Sheet, Direct/Indirect Cost, Overheads, ABC Costing.

---

## 1. What is Cost Accounting?

**Cost Accounting** is the process of **recording, classifying, analyzing, and controlling costs** of producing goods or services. It tells management *what it costs* to make a product and *how to control* that cost.

- **Cost** = the amount of resources (money) spent on a product/service.
- **Costing** = the technique/process of ascertaining cost.
- **Cost Accountancy** = the broader application of costing + cost accounting for control & decision-making.

### Financial Accounting vs Cost Accounting
| Basis | Financial Accounting | Cost Accounting |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Purpose | Profit & financial position for outsiders | Cost control for management |
| Users | External (investors, tax) | Internal (management) |
| Scope | Whole business | Product/process/department wise |
| Statutory | Mandatory | Optional (except some industries) |

### Importance / Objectives
1. **Ascertain cost** per unit/job/process.
2. **Cost control & reduction.**
3. **Fix selling prices** and prepare tenders/quotations.
4. **Measure efficiency** and reduce wastage.
5. **Help decision-making** (make-or-buy, product mix).
6. **Prepare budgets** and set standards.

## 2. Classification of Costs

### By Nature (Elements)
| Element | Direct | Indirect |
|---------|--------|----------|
| **Material** | Direct Material (raw material in product) | Indirect Material (lubricants, cotton waste) |
| **Labour** | Direct Labour (workers making product) | Indirect Labour (supervisor, cleaner) |
| **Expenses** | Direct Expenses (special mould hire, royalty) | Indirect Expenses (rent, power, depreciation) |

- **Direct Cost** = can be directly traced/identified to a product. (Direct Material + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses = **Prime Cost**.)
- **Indirect Cost** = cannot be directly traced; shared across products = **Overheads**.

### By Function
- **Factory/Works/Production overhead**, **Administration overhead**, **Selling overhead**, **Distribution overhead**.

### By Behaviour
- **Fixed, Variable, Semi-variable** (see Unit IV).

### By Controllability
- **Controllable** vs **Uncontrollable** costs.

## 3. Cost Sheet (Statement of Cost)

A **Cost Sheet** is a statement showing the **total cost and per-unit cost** of a product, broken into stages. It builds up cost step by step:

```
                                             Amount
Direct Materials                               xxx
Direct Labour (Wages)                          xxx
Direct Expenses                                xxx
-----------------------------------------------------
(1) PRIME COST                                 xxx
Add: Factory/Works Overheads                   xxx
-----------------------------------------------------
(2) WORKS / FACTORY COST                       xxx
Add: Administration Overheads                  xxx
-----------------------------------------------------
(3) COST OF PRODUCTION                         xxx
Add: Selling & Distribution Overheads          xxx
-----------------------------------------------------
(4) COST OF SALES / TOTAL COST                 xxx
Add: Profit                                    xxx
-----------------------------------------------------
(5) SALES                                      xxx
```

### The Four Key Totals (memorize!)
1. **Prime Cost** = Direct Material + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses
2. **Works/Factory Cost** = Prime Cost + Factory Overheads
3. **Cost of Production** = Works Cost + Administration Overheads
4. **Cost of Sales** = Cost of Production + Selling & Distribution Overheads
5. **Sales** = Cost of Sales + Profit

### Adjustments (advanced but common)
- **Direct Material Consumed** = Opening Stock of raw material + Purchases + carriage inward − Closing Stock.
- Adjust **Work-in-Progress** at works cost stage.
- Adjust **Finished Goods stock** at cost of production stage.
- Scrap sale value is deducted from factory overhead/cost.

### Simple Example
Direct material ₹50,000; Direct wages ₹30,000; Direct expenses ₹5,000; Factory OH ₹17,000; Admin OH ₹8,000; Selling OH ₹10,000; Profit 20% on cost.
- Prime Cost = 50,000 + 30,000 + 5,000 = **₹85,000**
- Works Cost = 85,000 + 17,000 = **₹1,02,000**
- Cost of Production = 1,02,000 + 8,000 = **₹1,10,000**
- Cost of Sales = 1,10,000 + 10,000 = **₹1,20,000**
- Profit (20% of 1,20,000) = ₹24,000 → **Sales = ₹1,44,000**

## 4. Overheads — Classification & Procedure

**Overhead** = total of all **indirect costs** (indirect material + indirect labour + indirect expenses).

### Classification of Overheads
1. **By Function:** Factory, Administration, Selling, Distribution.
2. **By Behaviour:** Fixed, Variable, Semi-variable.
3. **By Element:** Indirect material, indirect labour, indirect expenses.

### Procedure for Accounting Overheads (4 steps)
1. **Collection** — gather all indirect costs from invoices, payrolls, etc.
2. **Allocation** — assign whole overheads to a department where directly identifiable (e.g., a department's own supervisor salary).
3. **Apportionment** — split common overheads among departments on a fair basis:
   | Overhead | Basis of Apportionment |
   |----------|------------------------|
   | Rent, rates | Floor area |
   | Depreciation | Value of asset |
   | Lighting | No. of light points / area |
   | Canteen, welfare | Number of employees |
   | Power | Machine hours / HP |
4. **Absorption** — charge overheads to products/units via an **overhead absorption rate**.

### Overhead Absorption Rates
- **% of Direct Material** = (Overheads ÷ Direct Material) × 100
- **% of Direct Wages** = (Overheads ÷ Direct Wages) × 100
- **Labour Hour Rate** = Overheads ÷ Direct Labour Hours
- **Machine Hour Rate** = Overheads ÷ Machine Hours
- **Rate per Unit** = Overheads ÷ Units produced

> **Over-absorption** = absorbed more than actual; **Under-absorption** = absorbed less than actual. Adjusted in costing P&L.

## 5. Activity-Based Costing (ABC)

### Concept
Traditional costing spreads overheads using a single basis (like labour hours), which can be inaccurate when products differ a lot. **ABC** assigns overheads to products based on the **actual activities** that drive those costs. "Products consume activities; activities consume resources."

### Key Terms
- **Cost Pool** — a group of costs related to one activity (e.g., total machine-setup cost).
- **Cost Driver** — the factor that *causes* the cost of an activity (e.g., number of setups, number of orders, number of inspections).

### Procedure (steps)
1. **Identify main activities** (machine setup, ordering, quality inspection, material handling).
2. **Create cost pools** — group overhead costs by activity.
3. **Identify cost driver** for each activity.
4. **Calculate cost driver rate** = Total activity cost ÷ Total cost driver units.
5. **Assign costs to products** = Cost driver rate × cost driver units used by each product.

**Mini example:** Setup cost pool = ₹1,00,000; total setups = 500 → rate = ₹200/setup. A product needing 20 setups absorbs 20 × 200 = ₹4,000.

### Importance / Advantages
- **Accurate product costing** — especially with diverse products.
- **Better pricing & profitability analysis.**
- **Identifies non-value-adding activities** for cost reduction.
- Supports **better decisions** (product mix, outsourcing).

### Applications
- Manufacturing with **many products & high overheads**.
- Service industries (banks, hospitals, logistics).
- Where overheads are a large % of total cost.

### Limitations
- Expensive and time-consuming to implement.
- Choosing correct cost drivers can be difficult.
- Not suitable for small firms with low overheads.

## ✅ Quick Recap (Unit V)
- **Direct cost** → traceable (Prime Cost); **Indirect cost** → shared (Overheads).
- **Cost Sheet stages:** Prime → Works → Cost of Production → Cost of Sales → Sales.
- **Overhead steps:** Collection → Allocation → Apportionment → Absorption.
- **ABC:** overheads assigned via **cost drivers** & **cost pools** → more accurate than traditional single-rate costing.
