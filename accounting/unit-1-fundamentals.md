# Accounting — Unit I: Fundamentals

Book-Keeping, Accounting Conventions & Assumptions, Concepts & Principles, Single vs Double Entry, Accounting Process, Capital vs Revenue.

## 1. What is Accounting? (The Big Picture)

**Accounting** is the process of **identifying, recording, classifying, summarizing, and communicating** financial information about a business so that users can make decisions.

Think of it as the **"language of business."** Just like language turns thoughts into words, accounting turns business transactions into numbers that everyone can understand.

**The flow:**
```
Transaction → Record → Classify → Summarize → Report → Interpret
(happens)     (journal)  (ledger)  (trial bal.) (financials) (analysis)
```

### Book-Keeping vs Accounting
| Book-Keeping | Accounting |
|--------------|------------|
| Recording transactions (clerical, routine) | Recording + summarizing + analyzing + interpreting |
| First stage | Broader — includes book-keeping |
| Done by book-keeper | Done by accountant |
| Answers "what happened?" | Answers "what does it mean?" |

**Simple analogy:** Book-keeping is *writing down* every rupee that comes in and goes out. Accounting is *making sense* of those numbers to run the business better.

## 2. Accounting Concepts, Conventions & Assumptions

These are the **agreed rules** that make accounting consistent and comparable across all businesses. Split into three buckets:

### A. Basic Assumptions (the foundation — GAAP calls these "postulates")

| Assumption | Meaning (plain English) |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Business Entity** | The business is separate from its owner. Owner's personal expenses ≠ business expenses. |
| **Money Measurement** | Only things measurable in money are recorded. (Employee morale isn't recorded, even if valuable.) |
| **Going Concern** | Assume the business will continue operating for the foreseeable future (not shutting down soon). This is why we spread asset cost over years via depreciation. |
| **Accounting Period** | Business life is divided into equal periods (usually 1 year) to measure performance. |
| **Accrual** | Record revenue when *earned* and expenses when *incurred* — not when cash actually moves. |

### B. Accounting Concepts (principles for measurement)

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Dual Aspect** | Every transaction has two sides (debit & credit). Foundation of double-entry: **Assets = Liabilities + Capital**. |
| **Cost Concept** | Assets are recorded at their *purchase (historical) cost*, not market value. |
| **Revenue Recognition (Realization)** | Revenue is recognized when it is earned/realized (goods delivered), not when order is received. |
| **Matching** | Match expenses with the revenues they helped generate in the same period. |

### C. Accounting Conventions (customs/practices)

| Convention | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Consistency** | Use the same methods year after year so results are comparable. |
| **Conservatism (Prudence)** | "Anticipate no profit, but provide for all possible losses." Record losses early, profits only when certain. |
| **Materiality** | Record/disclose only items significant enough to affect decisions. Ignore trivial ones. |
| **Full Disclosure** | Report all relevant information so users aren't misled. |

> 🧠 **Memory tip:** *Assumptions* = ground rules of existence; *Concepts* = how to measure; *Conventions* = customs of presentation.

## 3. Single Entry System vs Double Entry System

### Single Entry System
- **Incomplete** system — records only *some* aspects (usually cash and personal accounts).
- No fixed rules; ignores the dual aspect for many transactions.
- Used by small businesses / sole traders.
- **Cannot** prepare a reliable Trial Balance or accurate Balance Sheet.
- Profit found by comparing capital: **Profit = Closing Capital − Opening Capital + Drawings − Additional Capital**.

### Double Entry System
- **Complete, scientific** system based on the **Dual Aspect** concept.
- Every transaction affects **two accounts** — one **debited**, one **credited**, for equal amounts.
- Total Debits **always** = Total Credits.

**Example:** You buy furniture for ₹10,000 cash.
- Furniture A/c (asset) increases → **Debit** Furniture ₹10,000
- Cash A/c (asset) decreases → **Credit** Cash ₹10,000

| Feature | Single Entry | Double Entry |
|---------|--------------|--------------|
| Completeness | Incomplete | Complete |
| Dual aspect | Ignored | Followed |
| Trial Balance | Not possible | Possible |
| Accuracy | Low | High |
| Suitable for | Small firms | All businesses |

### The Golden Rules of Double Entry
Traditional classification of accounts:

| Account Type | Rule | Example |
|--------------|------|---------|
| **Personal** (persons/firms) | Debit the **receiver**, Credit the **giver** | Paid to Ram → Debit Ram |
| **Real** (assets) | Debit **what comes in**, Credit **what goes out** | Bought machine → Debit Machine |
| **Nominal** (expenses/incomes) | Debit all **expenses/losses**, Credit all **incomes/gains** | Paid rent → Debit Rent |

**Modern rule (Accounting Equation approach):**
| Element | Increase | Decrease |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Asset | Debit | Credit |
| Liability | Credit | Debit |
| Capital | Credit | Debit |
| Income | Credit | Debit |
| Expense | Debit | Credit |

## 4. The Accounting Process (Accounting Cycle)

The step-by-step journey of a transaction:

```
1. Journal      → record transactions chronologically (day book)
2. Ledger       → post entries into individual accounts
3. Trial Balance→ list all balances to check Dr = Cr
4. Adjustments  → outstanding/prepaid, depreciation, etc.
5. Trading A/c  → find Gross Profit (sales − cost of goods sold)
6. P&L A/c      → find Net Profit (gross profit − expenses)
7. Balance Sheet→ show financial position (Assets = Liabilities + Capital)
```

### 4.1 Journal Entry (example)
Started business with ₹1,00,000 cash:
```
Cash A/c          Dr.   1,00,000
    To Capital A/c              1,00,000
(Being business started with cash)
```

### 4.2 Ledger
A ledger "account" collects all entries for one item (e.g., Cash A/c) in a **T-format** — debit on left, credit on right. The balancing figure is carried to the trial balance.

### 4.3 Trial Balance
A statement listing every ledger balance. **If total debits = total credits, arithmetic is likely correct.** It's the bridge between the ledger and final accounts.

### 4.4 Final Accounts
- **Trading Account** → Gross Profit/Loss
- **Profit & Loss Account** → Net Profit/Loss
- **Balance Sheet** → snapshot of assets, liabilities, capital on a date.

**The Accounting Equation (never forget this):**
> **Assets = Liabilities + Capital (Owner's Equity)**

## 5. Capital vs Revenue — Expenditure & Receipts

This is a **favorite exam topic**. Getting classification wrong distorts profit and the balance sheet.

### Capital Expenditure
- Spent to **acquire/improve a long-term asset** or increase earning capacity.
- Benefit lasts **more than one year**.
- Shown in the **Balance Sheet** (asset side).
- Examples: buying machinery, building, purchasing a vehicle, major upgrade extending life.

### Revenue Expenditure
- Spent for **day-to-day running**; benefit is **short-term** (within the year).
- Shown in the **P&L Account**.
- Examples: rent, salaries, repairs, electricity, purchase of raw materials.

| Basis | Capital Expenditure | Revenue Expenditure |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Purpose | Acquire/improve asset | Run daily operations |
| Benefit period | Long-term (>1 yr) | Short-term (≤1 yr) |
| Shown in | Balance Sheet | P&L Account |
| Example | Buy machine ₹5,00,000 | Machine repair ₹5,000 |

### Capital Receipts
- Non-recurring; from **financing/investing**, not from normal operations.
- Examples: capital introduced by owner, loans taken, sale of fixed assets.
- Shown in **Balance Sheet**.

### Revenue Receipts
- Recurring; from **normal business operations**.
- Examples: sales revenue, commission received, interest/rent received.
- Shown in **P&L Account**.

> 💡 **Deferred Revenue Expenditure:** A revenue expense whose benefit spreads over a few years (e.g., a large advertising campaign). Written off gradually, not all at once.

## ✅ Quick Recap (Unit I)
- **Accounting = language of business**; book-keeping is just the recording part.
- **Assumptions** (entity, going concern, accrual…), **Concepts** (dual aspect, matching…), **Conventions** (consistency, conservatism…).
- **Single entry** = incomplete; **Double entry** = complete, Dr = Cr always.
- Golden rules: Personal (Dr receiver, Cr giver), Real (Dr in, Cr out), Nominal (Dr expenses, Cr incomes).
- **Cycle:** Journal → Ledger → Trial Balance → Final Accounts.
- **Accounting Equation:** Assets = Liabilities + Capital.
- **Capital** items = long-term, go to Balance Sheet; **Revenue** items = short-term, go to P&L.
