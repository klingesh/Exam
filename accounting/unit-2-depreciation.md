# Accounting — Unit II: Depreciation

Meaning, Importance, Methods, and Accounting Procedure.

---

## 1. What is Depreciation? (Basic Idea)

**Depreciation** is the **gradual, permanent reduction in the value of a fixed asset** due to use, wear and tear, passage of time, or obsolescence.

**Everyday analogy:** A new bike worth ₹1,00,000 is not worth ₹1,00,000 after 3 years of riding. It loses value every year. That yearly loss = depreciation.

**Key point:** Depreciation applies to **fixed/tangible assets** used in the business (machinery, buildings, vehicles, furniture, equipment). Land usually is *not* depreciated (it doesn't wear out).

### Causes of Depreciation
1. **Wear and tear** — physical usage.
2. **Passage of time** — some assets lose value just with age (e.g., a lease).
3. **Obsolescence** — becomes outdated due to new technology.
4. **Depletion** — for natural resources like mines (using them up).
5. **Accidents** — sudden damage.

### Related terms (don't confuse!)
- **Depreciation** → tangible fixed assets (machine).
- **Amortization** → intangible assets (patents, goodwill, copyrights).
- **Depletion** → natural/wasting assets (mines, oil wells).

---

## 2. Importance / Objectives of Depreciation

1. **True profit** — depreciation is an expense; ignoring it overstates profit.
2. **True financial position** — assets shown at correct (reduced) value in Balance Sheet.
3. **Fund for replacement** — sets aside value so the asset can be replaced later.
4. **Correct cost of production** — depreciation is part of cost; needed for pricing.
5. **Legal/tax compliance** — companies must charge depreciation (Companies Act, Income Tax Act).
6. **Matching concept** — spreads asset cost over the years it generates revenue.

---

## 3. Key Terms & the Core Formula

- **Cost of asset** = Purchase price + installation + freight + any cost to make it usable.
- **Scrap / Residual / Salvage value** = estimated value at the end of useful life.
- **Useful life** = number of years the asset is expected to be used.
- **Depreciable amount** = Cost − Scrap value.

---

## 4. Methods of Depreciation

### Method 1: Straight Line Method (SLM) / Fixed Installment Method

The **same amount** of depreciation is charged every year.

**Formula:**
> **Annual Depreciation = (Cost − Scrap Value) ÷ Useful Life (years)**

> **Rate of Depreciation % = (Annual Depreciation ÷ Cost) × 100**

**Worked Example:**
Machine cost = ₹1,00,000; Scrap value = ₹10,000; Life = 5 years.
- Annual Depreciation = (1,00,000 − 10,000) ÷ 5 = **₹18,000 per year**

| Year | Opening Value | Depreciation | Closing Value |
|------|---------------|--------------|---------------|
| 1 | 1,00,000 | 18,000 | 82,000 |
| 2 | 82,000 | 18,000 | 64,000 |
| 3 | 64,000 | 18,000 | 46,000 |
| 4 | 46,000 | 18,000 | 28,000 |
| 5 | 28,000 | 18,000 | 10,000 (scrap) |

**Pros:** Simple; equal charge each year. **Cons:** Ignores that repairs increase as asset ages; asset never becomes fully zero unless no scrap.

---

### Method 2: Written Down Value Method (WDV) / Diminishing/Reducing Balance Method

Depreciation is charged at a **fixed percentage on the reducing (book) value** each year. So the rupee amount is **high in early years and lower later**.

**Formula:**
> **Depreciation = Book Value at start of year × Rate %**

**Worked Example:**
Machine cost = ₹1,00,000; Rate = 20% p.a. (WDV).

| Year | Opening Value | Depreciation (20%) | Closing Value |
|------|---------------|--------------------|---------------|
| 1 | 1,00,000 | 20,000 | 80,000 |
| 2 | 80,000 | 16,000 | 64,000 |
| 3 | 64,000 | 12,800 | 51,200 |
| 4 | 51,200 | 10,240 | 40,960 |

**Rate formula (if scrap & life given):**
> Rate = [1 − (Scrap ÷ Cost)^(1/n)] × 100, where n = life in years.

**Pros:** More realistic (higher depreciation when asset is new & efficient; total charge of dep + repairs stays fairly even). **Cons:** Asset value never reaches zero.

---

### SLM vs WDV — Comparison (common exam question)

| Basis | Straight Line (SLM) | Written Down Value (WDV) |
|-------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Basis of charge | Original cost | Reducing book value |
| Amount each year | Same (fixed) | Decreasing |
| Depreciation trend | Constant | High early, low later |
| Book value at end | Can become zero | Never zero |
| Total (Dep + Repairs) | Rises over time | Roughly even |
| Suitable for | Assets with steady use (furniture, buildings) | Assets that lose value fast early (machinery, vehicles) |

---

### Other Methods (know the idea)

| Method | Idea / Formula |
|--------|----------------|
| **Annuity Method** | Considers interest on capital invested in the asset. Uses annuity tables. |
| **Sinking Fund (Depreciation Fund)** | Depreciation is invested outside the business each year so funds are available to replace the asset. |
| **Insurance Policy Method** | Premium paid to an insurance policy that matures = replacement cost. |
| **Sum of Years' Digits (SYD)** | Accelerated. Depreciation = (Remaining life ÷ Sum of years) × Depreciable amount. E.g. 5-yr life: sum = 5+4+3+2+1 = 15; Year 1 = 5/15. |
| **Units of Production / Machine Hour Rate** | Based on usage. Dep per unit = (Cost − Scrap) ÷ Total expected units; then × units used. |
| **Depletion Method** | For mines: Dep per unit = (Cost − Scrap) ÷ Estimated total output; × units extracted. |
| **Revaluation Method** | Asset revalued at year-end; fall in value = depreciation (used for loose tools, livestock). |

## 5. Accounting Procedure (Journal Entries)

There are two common ways to record depreciation.

### (A) When a Provision for Depreciation Account is NOT maintained
Depreciation is charged directly to the asset.

```
1. Charging depreciation:
   Depreciation A/c        Dr.
       To Asset A/c

2. Transferring to P&L:
   Profit & Loss A/c       Dr.
       To Depreciation A/c
```
Asset appears in Balance Sheet at reduced (net) value.

### (B) When a Provision for Depreciation Account IS maintained
Asset stays at original cost; accumulated depreciation collected separately.

```
1. Charging depreciation:
   Depreciation A/c                 Dr.
       To Provision for Depreciation A/c

2. Transferring to P&L:
   Profit & Loss A/c                Dr.
       To Depreciation A/c
```
In Balance Sheet: Asset at cost **less** accumulated Provision for Depreciation.

### Sale of Asset — using Asset Disposal Account
```
1. Transfer cost:        Asset Disposal A/c   Dr.  → To Asset A/c
2. Transfer accumulated dep: Provision for Dep A/c Dr. → To Asset Disposal A/c
3. Sale proceeds:        Bank A/c Dr.  → To Asset Disposal A/c
4. Profit on sale:       Asset Disposal A/c Dr. → To P&L A/c   (if credit side bigger)
   Loss on sale:         P&L A/c Dr. → To Asset Disposal A/c   (if debit side bigger)
```

**Profit/Loss on sale = Sale Price − Book Value (WDV) on date of sale.**

---

## ✅ Quick Recap (Unit II)
- **Depreciation** = permanent fall in fixed asset value (use, time, obsolescence).
- **SLM:** (Cost − Scrap) ÷ Life → same amount yearly.
- **WDV:** Rate % on reducing book value → decreasing amount; never reaches zero.
- Amortization = intangibles; Depletion = natural resources.
- Two recording methods: direct (asset reduced) or via **Provision for Depreciation A/c** (asset at cost).
- **Profit/Loss on sale = Sale Price − Book Value.**
